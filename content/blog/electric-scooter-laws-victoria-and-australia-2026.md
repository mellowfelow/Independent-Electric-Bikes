Electric scooters occupy a legal grey area across most of Australia. Victoria ran a trial that has now transitioned into permanent regulations, while other states have their own rules — some permissive, some restrictive. Here is the complete picture for 2026.

## Victoria: The Trial Becomes Permanent

Victoria's e-scooter trial began in early 2022 and has since moved to permanent legislation. The current rules for riding a legal e-scooter on public roads and paths in Victoria are:

- **Maximum speed**: 25 km/h (electronically limited)
- **Maximum power**: 500 W rated output
- **Helmet**: mandatory — must meet AS/NZS 2063
- **Age**: must be 16 or older
- **Where you can ride**: bike lanes, shared paths, roads with a speed limit of 50 km/h or less
- **Where you cannot ride**: footpaths, roads with speed limits above 50 km/h
- **Lights**: front white light and rear red light required after dark
- **Bell**: required

Riders must give way to pedestrians on shared paths. Riding under the influence of alcohol or drugs carries the same penalties as drink-driving.

## Rental vs Private Scooters

The rules above apply to both rental scooters (Lime, Neuron) and privately owned scooters — but with one critical difference. Rental scooters are **geo-fenced** and **speed-limited by the operator** to comply automatically. Private scooters must be purchased compliant or limited by the rider.

If your scooter is capable of more than 25 km/h or more than 500 W, it is **not legal to ride on public roads or paths** in Victoria. It is legal to ride on private property with the landowner's permission.

## Other States

### New South Wales
NSW legalised e-scooters for public use in 2024. The rules mirror Victoria: 25 km/h, 500 W, helmet required, no footpath riding. Riders must be 16+. E-scooters can use bike lanes and shared paths.

### Queensland
Queensland was the first state to fully legalise private e-scooters. Rules: 25 km/h, helmet required, footpath riding allowed (unlike VIC and NSW), bike paths allowed, roads only where the speed limit is 50 km/h or less.

### South Australia
SA permits e-scooters on roads, bike lanes, and shared paths. Speed limit 25 km/h. Helmet mandatory. No footpath riding.

### Western Australia
WA permits e-scooters on shared paths and local roads up to 50 km/h zones. Speed limit 25 km/h. Helmet mandatory. Minimum age 16.

### Tasmania, ACT, Northern Territory
Rules vary and are evolving. Check your local transport authority for the most current regulations before riding.

## What About More Powerful Scooters?

Performance scooters — models with dual motors, 1000 W+, and top speeds of 50–80 km/h — are **not legal for public road or path use anywhere in Australia**. They are classified as motor vehicles and would require registration, CTP insurance, and a licence to ride on public roads. No state currently offers a registration pathway for e-scooters in this category.

These scooters are legal to ride on **private property** with the landowner's consent. Many owners use them on private estates, farms, and within gated communities.

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

In Victoria, riding a non-compliant e-scooter (overpowered, no helmet, on a footpath) can result in fines of $185–$925. Police have been actively enforcing these rules, particularly in CBD areas and on popular shared paths like the Yarra Trail.

## Buying a Legal E-Scooter

When purchasing, confirm:

1. **Speed limit**: the scooter's maximum speed must be 25 km/h or lower (or capable of being software-limited to 25 km/h)
2. **Motor power**: 500 W rated or under
3. **Lights and bell**: front white, rear red, and an audible bell
4. **Braking**: at least one independent braking system on each wheel (front and rear)

Popular compliant models include the Segway Ninebot E2 Pro, Segway Ninebot Max G2, and similar commuter-focused scooters from established brands.

## Insurance

E-scooter insurance is not mandatory in any Australian state, but it is recommended. Several Australian insurers now offer personal liability and theft cover for e-scooters, typically $100–$250 per year. Your home and contents insurance may already cover theft — check your policy.

## The Bottom Line

E-scooters are legal for public use in most Australian states in 2026, provided they meet the 25 km/h and 500 W limits. Performance models remain private-property only. Always check your state's current regulations, wear a helmet, and ride with lights after dark.

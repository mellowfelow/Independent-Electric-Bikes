Tyre size affects everything: speed, comfort, grip, puncture resistance, and range. Choosing the right tyre for your e-bike and your riding style is one of the simplest upgrades you can make.

## Understanding Tyre Sizes

### The Numbers

E-bike tyres are labelled with two or three numbers. The most common system is **ETRTO** (European Tyre and Rim Technical Organisation), shown as **width-diameter** in millimetres.

**Example: 50-622**
- 50 = tyre width in mm (50 mm = about 2 inches)
- 622 = rim diameter in mm (622 mm = 700c / 29")

You will also see the traditional sizing:
- **700 × 50c** = 700c wheel, 50 mm wide tyre (same as 50-622)
- **27.5 × 2.4"** = 27.5" wheel, 2.4" wide tyre (same as 60-584)
- **26 × 2.0"** = 26" wheel, 2.0" wide tyre (same as 50-559)
- **20 × 4.0"** = 20" wheel, 4.0" wide tyre (fat tyre, used on some e-bikes)

### Common E-Bike Wheel Sizes

| Wheel size | ETRTO diameter | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 700c / 29" | 622 mm | Road, commuter, hybrid e-bikes |
| 27.5" / 650b | 584 mm | Mountain e-bikes, some commuters |
| 26" | 559 mm | Older mountain bikes, some budget e-bikes |
| 20" | 406 mm | Folding e-bikes, cargo bikes |
| 24" | 507 mm | Some cargo bikes, kids' e-bikes |

## Width: The Most Important Choice

### Narrow Tyres (28–38 mm)

**Characteristics:**
- Lower rolling resistance (faster on smooth roads)
- Less comfortable (transmit more vibration)
- Less grip on loose or wet surfaces
- Higher puncture risk (less rubber between the road and the tube)
- Lower weight

**Best for:** road-focused commuting on smooth, sealed roads. Speed-oriented riders.

**Pressure:** 60–100 PSI

### Medium Tyres (40–50 mm)

**Characteristics:**
- Good balance of speed, comfort, and grip
- Adequate puncture resistance
- Handle rough roads, gravel paths, and light trails
- The sweet spot for most e-bike commuters

**Best for:** urban commuting on mixed surfaces (sealed roads, bike paths, some gravel). The default width on most commuter e-bikes.

**Pressure:** 40–65 PSI

### Wide Tyres (50–65 mm)

**Characteristics:**
- Very comfortable (absorb bumps and vibration)
- Excellent grip on loose surfaces (gravel, dirt, wet roads)
- Good puncture resistance (more rubber)
- Higher rolling resistance (slower on smooth roads)
- Heavier

**Best for:** rough roads, gravel paths, trail riding, comfort-focused riders, heavier riders (wider tyres distribute weight better).

**Pressure:** 30–50 PSI

### Fat Tyres (80–120+ mm / 3.0–5.0")

**Characteristics:**
- Maximum comfort and grip
- Float over sand, snow, and very loose surfaces
- Very high rolling resistance (noticeably slower on sealed roads)
- Heavy (1–2 kg per tyre)
- The motor compensates for the rolling resistance, making fat tyres more practical on e-bikes than on regular bikes

**Best for:** beach riding, sand, snow, off-road adventure. Style-oriented riders who like the fat tyre aesthetic.

**Pressure:** 10–20 PSI (yes, very low — this is how they grip)

## Tyre Pressure: Finding the Sweet Spot

### Too High
- Harsh ride (every bump transmitted to your body)
- Reduced grip (less tyre contact with the road)
- Higher puncture risk from sharp impacts (the tyre cannot absorb the hit)
- Faster rolling on smooth surfaces

### Too Low
- Sluggish feeling (the tyre deforms and creates more rolling resistance)
- Higher risk of pinch flats (the tube gets pinched between the rim and an obstacle)
- Reduced battery range (the motor works harder to overcome rolling resistance)
- Better grip and comfort

### Optimal Pressure

Start with the manufacturer's recommended range (printed on the tyre sidewall) and adjust:
- **Heavier riders:** add 5–10 PSI above the middle of the range
- **Lighter riders:** subtract 5–10 PSI
- **Wet conditions:** subtract 5 PSI for more grip
- **Rough roads:** subtract 5–10 PSI for more comfort and puncture absorption
- **Smooth roads, maximum range:** inflate to the upper end of the range

**Check pressure weekly.** Tyres lose 1–3 PSI per week naturally.

## Puncture Protection

E-bike tyres take more abuse than regular bike tyres — the higher speeds, heavier weight, and longer distances mean more exposure to glass, thorns, and road debris.

### Puncture-Resistant Tyres

Look for tyres with a built-in puncture protection layer — a strip of kevlar, rubber, or similar material between the tread and the tube that prevents sharp objects from reaching the tube.

**Top puncture-resistant tyres:**
- **Schwalbe Marathon Plus:** the gold standard. Virtually puncture-proof. Heavy (700–900 g per tyre) and slower rolling, but you will almost never get a flat.
- **Continental Contact Plus:** excellent puncture protection, lighter than Marathon Plus, good rolling.
- **Schwalbe Marathon GT 365:** good protection with better rolling than Marathon Plus. All-season compound.

### Tubeless Setup

Tubeless tyres (no inner tube — the tyre seals directly to the rim with sealant inside) are increasingly common on e-bikes:
- Sealant plugs small punctures automatically (you keep riding)
- Lower pressures possible (better comfort and grip without pinch flat risk)
- Slightly lower rolling resistance (no tube friction)
- Setup requires compatible rims and tyres, plus sealant that needs topping up every 3–6 months

### Tyre Liners

A plastic strip between the tube and tyre that adds a layer of puncture protection. Cheaper than puncture-resistant tyres ($15–$30 per wheel) but adds weight and can shift inside the tyre.

## E-Bike-Specific Tyres

Some tyres are specifically rated for e-bikes:
- **ECE-R75 rated:** meets the European standard for e-bike tyres (tested at higher speeds and loads)
- **Schwalbe "E" designation (E-25, E-50):** rated for e-bikes up to 25 km/h (E-25) or 50 km/h (E-50, for speed pedelecs)
- **Continental "E-Bike" series:** designed for the higher loads and speeds of e-bikes

**Are e-bike-specific tyres necessary?** For a standard 250 W, 25 km/h e-bike, a quality regular tyre (Marathon Plus, Contact Plus) is fine. E-bike-rated tyres are a safety margin, not a legal requirement.

## When to Replace

Replace tyres when:
- **Tread is worn smooth** (no visible pattern remaining)
- **Sidewall cracks** (dry rot from age or UV exposure)
- **Frequent punctures** (the protection layer is worn through)
- **Visible fabric or cords** showing through the rubber
- **Age:** even unused tyres degrade. Replace after 4–5 years regardless of wear.

## Bottom Line

For most e-bike commuters, a 40–50 mm puncture-resistant tyre (Schwalbe Marathon Plus or Continental Contact Plus) is the ideal choice — fast enough on sealed roads, comfortable over rough patches, and virtually puncture-proof. Check pressure weekly, inflate to the middle of the recommended range, and replace when the tread wears smooth or the sidewalls crack. The motor compensates for the rolling resistance of wider, heavier tyres, so prioritise comfort and puncture resistance over speed.

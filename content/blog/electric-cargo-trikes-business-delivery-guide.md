Electric cargo trikes are replacing vans in Australian cities. Three wheels, a large cargo box, and a powerful motor — they handle deliveries, mobile businesses, and logistics in congested urban areas where vans are slow, expensive, and often illegal to park.

## Why Three Wheels?

A standard cargo bike (two wheels) can carry 100–200 kg, but the load makes it unstable at low speed and when stationary. Loading and unloading requires a kickstand or wall to lean against.

A cargo trike (three wheels) stays upright by itself. You can load and unload without holding the bike. You can stop in traffic without putting a foot down. Children, pets, and fragile cargo stay level. The trade-off is width, weight, and manoeuvrability — trikes are wider, heavier, and slower to turn.

**Two main trike configurations:**
- **Delta (two rear wheels, one front):** the rear cargo box sits between two rear wheels. More common for heavy loads and business use. Wider turning radius.
- **Tadpole (two front wheels, one rear):** two front wheels with a cargo box between them. Better cornering (lower centre of gravity at the front). Less common for heavy cargo.

For business and delivery use, the **delta configuration** dominates — it places the cargo weight over the driven rear axle for better traction.

## Business Use Cases

### Last-Mile Delivery

E-cargo trikes are the fastest last-mile delivery vehicle in congested city centres:
- No parking search (park at the door, on the footpath, in bike racks)
- Use bike lanes and shared paths (where legally permitted)
- Zero emissions zones — compliant in all Australian cities
- Operating cost: $0.10–$0.30/km (electricity + maintenance) vs $0.80–$1.50/km for a van

**Companies using cargo trikes for delivery in Australia:** Australia Post (trial programs), Amazon (international programs), and numerous small courier and logistics operators.

### Mobile Food and Beverage

Coffee carts, ice cream trikes, juice bars, and food service trikes are a growing category:
- Lower startup cost than a food truck ($5,000–$15,000 vs $50,000–$150,000)
- No vehicle registration or driver's licence required (if the trike is pedal-assist under 250 W and 25 km/h)
- Mobile — move to where the customers are (markets, events, office precincts, beaches)
- Unique and eye-catching — the trike itself is marketing

### Trades and Services

Plumbers, electricians, cleaners, gardeners, and maintenance workers in urban areas can use cargo trikes for tool transport:
- Carry 100–300 kg of tools and equipment
- Park anywhere — no parking meters, no fines, no loading zones
- Service multiple jobs in a CBD without driving between them

### Waste Collection and Recycling

Several Australian councils and waste operators use e-cargo trikes for:
- CBD bin collection (replacing noisy, polluting collection trucks on narrow streets)
- Recycling pickups
- Green waste collection from parks and gardens

## Specifications to Consider

### Cargo Capacity

| Trike class | Cargo weight | Cargo volume | Use case |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light (250 W) | 80–120 kg | 200–400 L | Small deliveries, personal use |
| Medium (250 W+) | 150–250 kg | 400–800 L | Last-mile delivery, mobile food |
| Heavy (500 W+) | 250–500 kg | 800–1500 L | Commercial logistics, waste |

**Note:** trikes exceeding 250 W motor power or 25 km/h assisted speed are not classified as bicycles under Australian law. They may require registration, licensing, and insurance — check your state's regulations.

### Battery and Range

Commercial cargo trikes need large batteries for all-day operation:
- **500 Wh:** 30–50 km loaded (light commercial use, half-day)
- **1000 Wh:** 50–80 km loaded (full-day delivery route)
- **1500+ Wh (dual battery):** 80–120 km loaded (intensive commercial use)

**Charging:** standard 240 V outlet. A 1000 Wh battery charges in 4–6 hours. For all-day operation, charge during lunch or carry a spare battery.

### Motor

- **Rear hub motor:** simple, reliable, direct drive to the rear wheel(s). The standard for cargo trikes.
- **Mid-drive motor:** drives through the gears, more efficient on hills. More complex to maintain on a trike (chain/belt routing to the rear axle).
- **Dual rear hub motors (one per rear wheel):** some heavy-duty trikes use two motors for better traction and hill climbing. Increases cost and complexity.

### Braking

A loaded cargo trike weighing 200–400 kg (cargo + trike + rider) requires serious braking:
- **Hydraulic disc brakes** on all three wheels (minimum)
- **180 mm or 203 mm rotors** (larger = better heat dissipation under heavy loads)
- **Parking brake** (mechanical lock to prevent the trike rolling when parked on a slope)

### Cargo Box

- **Weatherproof lid or cover** (rain protection for deliveries)
- **Tie-down points** (secure loose cargo)
- **Lockable** (theft prevention during multi-stop routes)
- **Removable or modular** (swap between flat bed, box, and food service configurations)

## Legal Considerations in Australia

### Classification

A cargo trike is legally a **bicycle** if:
- Motor power is 250 W or less (continuous rated)
- Motor assist cuts off at 25 km/h
- The rider must pedal for the motor to assist (pedal-assist, not throttle-only)

If any of these conditions are not met, the trike is a **motor vehicle** and must be registered, insured, and ridden with a licence.

### Where You Can Ride

As a bicycle:
- Roads (in the left lane or bike lane)
- Bike lanes and shared paths (check width restrictions — some paths exclude wide cargo bikes)
- Footpaths in some states (Queensland, Tasmania, ACT — but not Victoria, NSW, or South Australia for adult riders)

**Width:** some bike lanes and shared paths are too narrow for a cargo trike (typically 80–120 cm wide). Check your route.

### Insurance

- Standard bicycle insurance covers cargo trikes classified as bicycles
- Commercial use may require business insurance (public liability, cargo insurance)
- If the trike is classified as a motor vehicle, compulsory third-party insurance is required

## Cost Analysis: Cargo Trike vs Van

| | Electric cargo trike | Small delivery van |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | $5,000–$20,000 | $25,000–$50,000 |
| Fuel/energy per year | $100–$300 (electricity) | $3,000–$6,000 (fuel) |
| Registration | $0 (if bicycle) | $800–$1,500/year |
| Insurance | $200–$500/year | $1,500–$3,000/year |
| Parking | $0 | $2,000–$5,000/year (CBD) |
| Maintenance | $300–$600/year | $1,500–$3,000/year |
| **Total annual cost** | **$600–$1,400** | **$8,800–$18,500** |

The trike saves $8,000–$17,000 per year in operating costs. For a business making 10–30 deliveries per day within a 10 km radius, the trike pays for itself in 3–6 months.

## Limitations

- **Speed:** 25 km/h max (legally, as a bicycle). Not suitable for routes requiring highway speeds.
- **Range:** limited by battery. Not suitable for routes exceeding 50–80 km per day without recharging.
- **Weather:** riders are exposed to rain, wind, and heat. Some trikes have canopies, but full weather protection requires a cabin (which pushes the vehicle into motor vehicle classification).
- **Road surface:** trikes are less stable on rough, unpaved, or steeply cambered roads. The fixed rear axle does not lean into corners like a bicycle.
- **Width:** 80–120 cm wide trikes do not fit through standard doorways or narrow laneways.

## Bottom Line

Electric cargo trikes are a proven, cost-effective alternative to delivery vans for urban last-mile logistics, mobile businesses, and trade services operating within a 10–15 km radius. They save thousands in annual operating costs, park anywhere, and operate in zero-emission zones. The limitations — speed, range, and weather exposure — are manageable for most urban commercial applications.

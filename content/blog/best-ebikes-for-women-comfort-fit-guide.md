The best e-bike for a woman is the one that fits. Not a "women's bike" with a pastel colour and lower specs — a bike with the right frame geometry, reach, standover height, and contact points for the rider's body.

## The Problem with "Women's" Bikes

Many bike brands sell "women's-specific" models that are simply men's bikes with a step-through frame, a different saddle, narrower handlebars, and sometimes lower-spec components — at the same price. This is marketing, not engineering.

**What actually differs between riders (regardless of gender):**
- Height (determines frame size)
- Torso-to-leg ratio (determines reach vs standover)
- Shoulder width (determines handlebar width)
- Hand size (determines brake lever reach and grip diameter)
- Sit bone width (determines saddle width)

Women statistically tend to have proportionally longer legs relative to torso length, narrower shoulders, and smaller hands — but these are averages, and individual variation is massive. Fit is individual.

## What Actually Matters: Fit

### Frame Size
The most important measurement. Use the manufacturer's size chart as a starting point, then fine-tune.

**How to check:**
1. **Standover height:** stand over the top tube with flat feet. At least 2–3 cm of clearance.
2. **Seat height:** with the saddle at the correct height (slight bend in the knee at the bottom of the pedal stroke), can you touch the ground with your toes?
3. **Reach:** with hands on the grips and elbows slightly bent, your back should be at a comfortable angle.

### Step-Through vs Step-Over Frame

**Step-through:** easy to mount and dismount. Useful for riders in work clothes, older riders, riders who carry children, and anyone who values easy mounting.

**Step-over:** the traditional diamond frame. Marginally stiffer (irrelevant for commuting).

Neither is inherently "for women" or "for men." Choose based on your mounting preference.

### Handlebar Width
Standard e-bike handlebars are 680–720 mm wide. Riders with narrower shoulders may find these too wide. Handlebars can be cut narrower at a bike shop ($20–$30). Target: handlebar width should approximately match shoulder width.

### Brake Lever Reach
Riders with smaller hands may not fully reach the brake levers. Most brake levers have a reach adjustment screw — turn it to bring the lever closer to the grip.

### Saddle
The right saddle depends on sit bone width and riding position — not gender.

**Measure sit bones:** sit on corrugated cardboard on a hard surface. The two deepest impressions show your sit bone width.

**Saddle width:**
- Sit bones under 100 mm: narrow saddle (130–143 mm)
- Sit bones 100–120 mm: medium saddle (143–155 mm)
- Sit bones over 120 mm: wide saddle (155–168 mm)

### Crank Length
Shorter riders (under 165 cm) may benefit from 165 mm cranks instead of the standard 170–175 mm, reducing hip angle at the top of the pedal stroke.

## E-Bike Features That Matter for Comfort

### Weight
E-bikes weigh 18–28 kg. For riders who lift the bike regularly, consider lighter aluminium frames (20–24 kg) or smaller batteries.

### Low Centre of Gravity
A battery integrated into the down tube keeps the centre of gravity low, making the bike more stable and easier to handle at low speed.

### Adjustable Stem
Lets you change handlebar height and angle without replacing parts — valuable for dialling in the perfect position.

## Recommended Styles by Use Case

### Daily Commuting
- Step-through frame, integrated lights and mudguards, rear rack, 400–500 Wh battery, 40–45 mm tyres

### Fitness and Recreation
- Mid-drive motor, wider tyre clearance, 500+ Wh battery, multiple assist levels

### Carrying Children
- Step-through frame, strong rear rack (25+ kg), low centre of gravity, hydraulic disc brakes, 500+ Wh battery

### Trail and Adventure
- Mountain e-bike geometry, mid-drive motor (70+ Nm), dropper seatpost, 625+ Wh battery

## Sizing Quick Reference

| Rider height | Typical frame size | Wheel size |
|---|---|---|
| Under 155 cm | XS / 38–42 cm | 27.5" or 26" |
| 155–165 cm | S / 42–47 cm | 27.5" or 700c |
| 165–175 cm | M / 47–52 cm | 700c / 29" |
| 175–185 cm | L / 52–56 cm | 700c / 29" |
| Over 185 cm | XL / 56–60 cm | 700c / 29" |

**Always test ride before buying.** Ride for at least 10 minutes, including a hill and stop-start section, before deciding.

## Bottom Line

The best e-bike for a woman is not a "women's bike" — it is a bike that fits. Focus on frame size, reach, saddle width, handlebar width, and brake lever reach. Step-through frames are practical but not inherently gendered. Test ride, adjust contact points, and choose based on fit and function, not marketing labels.

An e-bike chain works harder than a regular bike chain. The motor adds torque, the bike is heavier, and riders cover more distance. A neglected chain stretches faster, wears out the cassette and chainring, and eventually snaps — leaving you stranded mid-commute.

## Why E-Bike Chains Wear Faster

A standard bicycle chain wears at a rate proportional to the force applied to it. On an e-bike:
- **Motor torque multiplies chain load.** A 250 W motor with 50–80 Nm of torque adds significant force to the chain, especially during acceleration and hill climbing.
- **Heavier bikes = more force.** A 25 kg e-bike requires more chain tension to accelerate than a 10 kg road bike.
- **Higher average speeds.** E-bike riders cruise at 25 km/h instead of 18–20 km/h, meaning the chain moves faster and under greater load for more kilometres.
- **Mid-drive motors are the worst offenders.** Mid-drive systems route all motor power through the chain. A hub motor does not — the chain carries only pedal force.

## Chain Wear: What It Means and How to Measure It

Chain "stretch" is not the chain physically stretching — it is the pins and rollers inside each link wearing down, increasing the distance between links. A new chain has exactly 12.7 mm (0.5 inch) between each pin. As the chain wears, this distance increases.

### Measuring Chain Wear

Use a **chain wear indicator tool** (also called a chain checker). These cost $10–$20 and give an instant reading:
- **0.5 % wear:** the chain should be replaced soon. On an e-bike, replace at this point to protect the cassette.
- **0.75 % wear:** the chain must be replaced immediately. If you ride past this point, the worn chain damages the cassette and chainring — meaning you replace all three instead of just the chain.
- **1.0 % wear:** the chain and cassette are both damaged. Replace both.

**E-bike rule of thumb:** check chain wear every 500 km. Replace the chain when it reaches 0.5 % wear. On a mid-drive e-bike ridden daily, this is every 1,500–3,000 km — roughly every 3–6 months for a commuter.

## Cleaning the Chain

A clean chain lasts longer, shifts better, and runs quieter.

### What You Need
- Chain degreaser (bike-specific, not WD-40)
- A chain cleaning device (a plastic box with brushes that clips onto the chain) or an old toothbrush
- Clean rags
- Chain lubricant (see below)
- Latex gloves (optional, but black hands are annoying)

### Steps
1. **Shift to the smallest rear cog** (least chain tension)
2. **Apply degreaser** to the chain while turning the cranks backward. Let it soak for 2–3 minutes.
3. **Scrub** with the chain cleaning device or toothbrush. Turn the cranks backward through 10–15 full revolutions.
4. **Rinse** with water (a gentle hose or wet rag — never a high-pressure washer, which forces water into bearings and seals)
5. **Dry** the chain thoroughly with a clean rag. Moisture causes rust.
6. **Lubricate** (see below)

### Frequency
- **Commuters riding in dry conditions:** clean every 2–4 weeks
- **Commuters riding in rain or on wet roads:** clean every 1–2 weeks
- **Trail and off-road riders:** clean after every muddy ride

## Lubricating the Chain

Lubrication reduces friction between the chain's pins and rollers. The right lube depends on your conditions.

### Dry Lube (Wax-Based)
- **Best for:** dry conditions, road riding, commuting in fair weather
- **Properties:** goes on wet, dries to a waxy film. Does not attract dust and grit. Stays clean.
- **Downside:** washes off in rain. Must be reapplied after wet rides.
- **Application interval:** every 150–300 km in dry conditions

### Wet Lube (Oil-Based)
- **Best for:** rain, mud, wet conditions, winter commuting
- **Properties:** stays on in water. Does not wash off easily.
- **Downside:** attracts dirt and grit like a magnet. The chain becomes black and gritty quickly. Requires more frequent cleaning.
- **Application interval:** every 200–400 km in wet conditions

### Wax Immersion
- **Best for:** obsessive chain care, maximum chain life
- **Properties:** the chain is removed, degreased completely, and dipped in molten paraffin wax. The wax fills every pin and roller, providing the cleanest possible lubrication.
- **Downside:** requires chain removal and re-waxing every 300–500 km. Not practical for most commuters.
- **Chain life extension:** 50–100 % longer chain life compared to drip lube

### How to Apply Drip Lube
1. Ensure the chain is clean and dry
2. Apply one drop of lube to each roller while turning the cranks backward slowly
3. Continue turning the cranks for 30 seconds to work the lube into the links
4. Wipe the exterior of the chain with a clean rag — lube on the outside attracts dirt and does nothing useful. The lubrication happens inside the rollers.

**Common mistake:** spraying the chain with WD-40 or a general-purpose lubricant. WD-40 is a solvent and water displacer, not a lubricant. It strips existing lube and evaporates, leaving the chain unprotected.

## E-Bike-Specific Chain Considerations

### Strengthened Chains
Some manufacturers sell chains specifically rated for e-bikes. These have hardened pins and plates to handle the extra torque. Shimano's e-bike chains (CN-E8000 series) and KMC's e-bike chains (e-series) are designed for this purpose.

**Are they necessary?** On a hub-motor e-bike, a standard chain is fine — the motor does not load the chain. On a mid-drive e-bike with 60+ Nm of torque, an e-bike-rated chain is a worthwhile investment.

### Quick Links
Most modern chains use a quick link (master link) for easy removal. This makes cleaning and wax immersion practical. Check that the quick link is rated for e-bike use if you are running a mid-drive system.

### Chain Line
Mid-drive motors can cause chain-line issues (the chain running at an angle between the chainring and cassette). Cross-chaining (big ring + big cog, or small ring + small cog) accelerates wear. Avoid extreme gear combinations.

## When to Replace the Cassette and Chainring

A worn chain damages the cassette and chainring teeth. If you replace chains at 0.5 % wear consistently, the cassette will last through 2–3 chains (4,000–8,000 km on a mid-drive e-bike). The chainring lasts through 3–5 chains.

**Signs of a worn cassette:**
- The chain skips under load (especially on the most-used cogs)
- Teeth look pointed or shark-finned instead of flat-topped
- A new chain skips on the old cassette

**Signs of a worn chainring:**
- Teeth are hooked or asymmetric
- The chain rides up on the teeth instead of sitting in the valleys

## Bottom Line

Chain maintenance is the single most impactful thing you can do for your e-bike's drivetrain longevity. Check wear every 500 km, clean every 2–4 weeks, lubricate with the right product for your conditions, and replace at 0.5 % wear. On a mid-drive e-bike, this saves you from premature cassette and chainring replacements — which cost 3–5 times more than a chain.

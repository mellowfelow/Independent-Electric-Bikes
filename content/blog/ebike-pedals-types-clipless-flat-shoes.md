Pedals are the contact point between you and the bike. On an e-bike, the right pedal choice affects comfort, safety, power transfer, and how quickly you can put a foot down in traffic.

## Flat Pedals

The default on almost every e-bike. A flat platform with grip pins or rubber tread. You step on, you ride. You step off, you stop.

### Advantages
- **No special shoes required.** Ride in sneakers, work shoes, boots, or sandals.
- **Instant foot-down.** In traffic, you can put your foot down immediately — no unclipping, no twisting, no delay. On a heavy e-bike in stop-start city traffic, this matters.
- **Easy to learn.** No technique to master. No risk of failing to unclip and falling over at a traffic light (the "clipless fall" — every cyclist does it at least once).
- **Walk normally.** Step off the bike and walk into the office, shop, or cafe without clacking around in cycling shoes.

### Disadvantages
- **Foot can slip.** In wet conditions, on rough terrain, or during hard pedalling, your foot can slide off a flat pedal. Grip pins help but do not eliminate this.
- **Less power transfer.** You can only push down on flat pedals — you cannot pull up. This limits pedalling efficiency, though on an e-bike with motor assist, the difference is marginal.
- **Shin strikes.** If your foot slips, the grip pins on a quality flat pedal will scrape your shin. Painful and bloody.

### Best Flat Pedals for E-Bikes
Look for:
- **Wide platform** (100 mm+ width) — more stable, better grip
- **Concave shape** — cradles the foot, prevents sliding
- **Replaceable grip pins** — adjustable traction
- **Sealed bearings** — last longer than bushings, especially on heavy e-bikes
- **Metal construction** (alloy or chromoly) — more durable than plastic, though heavier

### Best For
- Commuters in regular clothes
- Casual riders
- Riders who stop frequently (delivery, errands, school runs)
- Beginners
- Anyone who does not want cycling-specific shoes

## Clipless Pedals

"Clipless" is a confusing name — it means the pedal uses a cleat-and-mechanism system instead of the old toe clips. A cleat bolted to the sole of your cycling shoe locks into a spring-loaded mechanism on the pedal. Twist your heel to release.

### Advantages
- **Secure connection.** Your foot cannot slip off the pedal, even over bumps, in rain, or during hard efforts.
- **Full pedal stroke.** You can push down AND pull up, using more muscle groups. This is more efficient — though on an e-bike with 250 W of assist, the efficiency gain is less significant than on a road bike.
- **Consistent foot position.** The cleat positions your foot in the same place every time — optimal for knee health and power transfer.

### Disadvantages
- **Special shoes required.** Clipless shoes have stiff soles with bolt holes for the cleat. You cannot ride in regular shoes (or you can, but it is terrible).
- **Slow foot-down.** To put your foot down, you must twist your heel to unclip. This takes a fraction of a second — but in a panic stop on a heavy e-bike, that fraction matters.
- **Walking.** Road-style clipless shoes (Look, SPD-SL, Speedplay) have exposed cleats on the sole. Walking on hard surfaces is awkward, loud, and wears the cleats. Mountain-style clipless shoes (SPD) have recessed cleats and walk more normally.
- **Learning curve.** Every new clipless user falls over at least once when they forget to unclip at a stop. On a 25 kg e-bike, this fall is harder and more embarrassing than on a 9 kg road bike.

### Clipless Systems for E-Bikes

**SPD (Shimano Pedaling Dynamics) — two-bolt mountain style:**
The best clipless system for e-bike commuting. Recessed cleats mean you can walk normally. Double-sided pedals (clip in from either side). Adjustable release tension. Compatible with shoes that look almost normal (Shimano, Giro, and others make SPD-compatible shoes that resemble hiking shoes or casual sneakers).

**SPD-SL, Look, Speedplay — three-bolt road style:**
Designed for road cycling performance. Exposed cleats, terrible for walking, single-sided pedals. Not practical for commuting or daily e-bike use. Only relevant if you are using your e-bike for road training.

### Best For
- Performance-focused riders (road, gravel, mountain)
- Long-distance touring
- Riders who want maximum efficiency and do not stop frequently
- Riders already experienced with clipless systems

## Platform Pedals with Toe Clips / Straps

A middle ground: flat pedals with a cage or strap that holds your foot in place. You slide your foot in to secure it, pull it back to release.

### Advantages
- Ride in any shoe
- More secure than flat pedals alone
- No cleats or special equipment needed

### Disadvantages
- Slower to get in and out than both flats and clipless
- Can trap your foot in a crash (dangerous)
- Straps wear out and loosen over time
- The cage/strap can be awkward to find with your foot when starting

**Verdict:** largely outdated. Flat pedals with good grip pins or SPD clipless pedals are better options in almost every situation.

## Pedal Size and E-Bike Weight

E-bikes are heavier than regular bikes. This means:
- **More force on the pedals** during acceleration and climbing
- **More stress on pedal bearings** from the extra weight
- **More inertia when the foot slips** — a slip at speed on a heavy bike is more destabilising

**Recommendation:** choose pedals with **sealed bearings** (not bushings) and a **wide platform** (100 mm+). Cheap pedals with loose bearings wear out quickly on a heavy e-bike.

## Shoes for E-Bike Commuting

### If Using Flat Pedals
- **Flat, grippy soles** (rubber, not leather). Skate shoes (Vans, Converse) have flat soles that grip well.
- **Stiff soles** reduce foot fatigue on longer rides. Flexible soles (running shoes) let the pedal dig into your foot.
- **Avoid heels, sandals, and loose shoes** — they slip on pedals and can catch on the bike.

### If Using SPD Clipless
- **SPD touring/casual shoes** look like normal shoes with a recessed cleat. Shimano CT5, Giro Rumble, and similar models are designed for commuting — walk normally, clip in on the bike.
- **SPD mountain shoes** are stiffer and grippier but look more "sporty." Good if you do not care about office appearance.

## Bottom Line

For most e-bike commuters, **quality flat pedals with grip pins** are the right choice — ride in any shoe, put your foot down instantly in traffic, walk normally at your destination. If you have clipless experience and want the connection, **SPD (two-bolt) pedals with commuter shoes** are the best clipless option for daily riding. Avoid road-style clipless on a commuter e-bike — the walking penalty and slow foot-down are not worth the marginal efficiency gain when you have a motor.

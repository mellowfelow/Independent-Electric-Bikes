E-bike touring is cycling's most accessible adventure format. The motor assist means you can cover 80–120 km per day without being an elite athlete, carry camping gear without being crushed by the weight, and tackle headwinds and hills without ending the day in exhaustion. Australia's vast distances and varied terrain make it one of the best countries in the world for e-bike touring — if you plan correctly.

## Range Planning: The Critical Factor

On tour, running out of battery means pedalling a 25–30 kg bike loaded with 10–20 kg of gear. That is a miserable experience. Range planning is the single most important part of e-bike tour preparation.

### Daily Distance and Battery Budget

| Assist level | Range per 500 Wh charge | Comfortable daily distance |
|---|---|---|
| Eco (minimal assist) | 80–120 km | 80–100 km |
| Tour (moderate assist) | 50–80 km | 60–80 km |
| Sport (heavy assist) | 35–50 km | 40–50 km |

For touring, **Eco mode** should be your default, with Tour or Sport reserved for headwinds, steep climbs, and the last 10 km of a long day. Discipline with assist levels is the difference between finishing each day with 20 % battery and running dry 15 km from camp.

### Charging on Tour

**Accommodation stops:** charge overnight at hotels, motels, caravan parks, and hostels. Most e-bike chargers draw 100–250 W — less than a hairdryer. Ask politely and you will rarely be refused a power point.

**Cafe stops:** a full charge takes 4–6 hours, but a 2-hour lunch stop can add 30–50 % charge. Plan lunch at a cafe with outdoor power points.

**Carry a spare battery:** if your bike supports dual batteries or a range extender, the extra 500 Wh doubles your range and eliminates battery anxiety. The trade-off is 3–4 kg of extra weight.

**Solar charging:** portable solar panels (100 W folding panels) can charge an e-bike battery in 6–10 hours of direct sun. Practical for rest days or remote camping, but too slow for daily top-ups.

## Gear and Packing

### Panniers and Bags

Touring loads go on the bike, not on your body. A backpack on a long ride causes back pain, shoulder strain, and excessive sweating.

**Setup:**
- **Rear panniers (2 × 20 L):** clothing, food, cooking gear
- **Front panniers (2 × 12 L):** tools, spare parts, rain gear, camera
- **Frame bag or top tube bag:** valuables, phone, snacks, wallet
- **Handlebar bag:** map, sunscreen, quick-access items

**Total carrying capacity:** 60–80 litres is typical for a self-supported tour.

**Weight distribution:** aim for 60 % rear, 40 % front. Too much weight at the rear makes the front wheel light and the steering twitchy.

### Tools and Spares

Carry enough to fix common issues far from a bike shop:
- Spare inner tube (2) or tubeless plug kit
- Tyre levers and mini pump
- Multi-tool with Allen keys and screwdrivers
- Chain links (quick links for your chain type)
- Brake pads (one set)
- Zip ties and electrical tape
- Spoke key
- E-bike charger (non-negotiable — this is the equivalent of a fuel can)

### Camping Gear (If Self-Supported)

If you are camping rather than staying in accommodation:
- Lightweight tent (1.5–2 kg)
- Sleeping bag (rated to the lowest expected temperature)
- Sleeping pad
- Compact stove and fuel
- Cookware and utensils
- Water filter or purification tablets (for remote routes)

Total camping gear weight: 5–8 kg.

## Australian Touring Routes

### Great Ocean Road (Victoria)

- **Distance:** 250 km (Torquay to Allansford)
- **Duration:** 3–5 days
- **Terrain:** rolling coastal hills, sealed road, some sections with no shoulder
- **Highlights:** Twelve Apostles, rainforest sections, coastal cliffs
- **Charging:** towns every 30–50 km with accommodation and cafes
- **Difficulty:** moderate — some steep climbs near Apollo Bay

### Murray to Mountains Rail Trail (Victoria)

- **Distance:** 100+ km of connected trails
- **Terrain:** flat to gently rolling, sealed rail trail
- **Highlights:** wineries, historic towns, Beechworth, Bright
- **Charging:** towns every 20–30 km
- **Difficulty:** easy — one of the best beginner touring routes in Australia

### Munda Biddi Trail (Western Australia)

- **Distance:** 1,000+ km (Perth to Albany)
- **Terrain:** mixed — fire trails, gravel, singletrack, some sealed sections
- **Highlights:** jarrah forest, remote wilderness, Southern Ocean coast
- **Charging:** huts and towns spaced 40–80 km apart — battery planning is critical
- **Difficulty:** hard — remote, rough surfaces, long distances between services

### East Coast Tasmania

- **Distance:** 300 km (Launceston to Hobart via the east coast)
- **Terrain:** rolling hills, sealed roads, quiet traffic
- **Highlights:** Bay of Fires, Freycinet, Maria Island
- **Charging:** towns every 30–50 km
- **Difficulty:** moderate — some exposed coastal sections with wind

### Brisbane Valley Rail Trail (Queensland)

- **Distance:** 161 km
- **Terrain:** flat, crushed gravel rail trail
- **Highlights:** Lockyer Valley, rural Queensland scenery
- **Charging:** towns at regular intervals
- **Difficulty:** easy

## Safety on Tour

- **Visibility:** Australian rural roads often lack bike lanes and shoulders. Wear high-visibility clothing and run lights at all times, including during the day.
- **Water:** in remote sections, carry 3+ litres of water. Australian heat dehydrates faster than you expect.
- **Sun protection:** SPF 50+ sunscreen, UV-blocking sunglasses, and a helmet with a visor.
- **Wildlife:** kangaroos are most active at dawn and dusk. They are unpredictable and a collision at speed is dangerous. Wombat burrows on trail edges can collapse under a wheel.
- **Communication:** carry a charged phone and consider a personal locator beacon (PLB) or satellite communicator (e.g., Garmin inReach) for remote routes.

## Bottom Line

E-bike touring opens long-distance cycling to riders who would not attempt it on a regular bike. The motor assist means you can cover 80+ km per day with gear, tackle headwinds and hills without being destroyed, and arrive at camp with energy left to enjoy it. Start with a short rail trail (2–3 days) to test your setup and find your daily rhythm, then graduate to the multi-day routes that make Australian touring world-class.

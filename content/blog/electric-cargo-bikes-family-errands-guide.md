A cargo e-bike replaces a second car. It carries children, groceries, school bags, and everything else a suburban family hauls on a daily basis — without petrol, parking, registration, or insurance.

## Types of Cargo E-Bikes

### Long John (Front-Loading)

A long chassis with a cargo box or platform between the handlebars and the front wheel. The rider sits behind the box on a normal saddle.

**Capacity:** 100–200 kg payload (including rider). The box carries 1–4 children on benches, or 200+ litres of cargo.

**Examples:** Urban Arrow Family, Babboe City, Riese & Müller Load, Tern HSD

**Best for:** families with young children (the box is visible, enclosed, and safe), large grocery runs, transporting bulky items.

**Handling:** long wheelbase makes the bike stable at speed but slow to turn. The steering feels different from a regular bike — you steer the handlebars, which turns the cargo box, which turns the front wheel via a linkage. Takes 1–2 rides to get used to.

### Long Tail (Rear-Loading)

An extended rear rack behind the rider. Children sit on the rack with foot pegs and handles, or cargo is strapped to the extended platform.

**Capacity:** 60–100 kg on the rear platform. Carries 1–2 children or a large load of shopping.

**Examples:** Tern GSD, Yuba Spicy Curry, RadPower RadWagon, Benno Boost

**Best for:** families who need a cargo bike that rides like a normal bike. The long tail is narrower than a long john, parks in a normal bike space, and handles more naturally.

**Handling:** feels close to a regular bike, but heavier and with more momentum. The rear load makes the bike stable but slower to accelerate.

### Mid-Tail

A shorter extension than a long tail — adds one child seat or a compact cargo platform.

**Capacity:** 30–60 kg on the rear.

**Best for:** one-child families or light cargo. More compact than a long tail, fits in a standard bike rack.

### Front Basket / Rear Rack (Standard E-Bike)

Not a cargo bike, but worth mentioning: a standard commuter e-bike with a front basket and rear rack carries a surprising amount — a child seat on the rack plus shopping in the basket. For families with one child and moderate cargo needs, this may be enough.

## What Can You Carry?

### Children
- **Long john box:** 1–4 children (ages 0–8) on benches with harnesses. Rain cover available.
- **Long tail rack:** 1–2 children (ages 1–10) on child seats or the deck with foot pegs and handles.
- **Plus school bags, lunch boxes, and a spare jacket.** The bike carries everything the children need.

### Groceries
A long john box holds 2–4 bags of groceries (50–100 L). A long tail with panniers holds a similar amount. This covers a normal weekly shop for a family of four.

### Bulky Items
Flat-pack furniture, plants, small appliances, cases of drinks, bags of potting mix. If it fits in the boot of a hatchback, it probably fits on a cargo bike (or strapped to one).

### Work Gear
Tradespeople, photographers, musicians, and market stallholders use cargo bikes to carry tools, equipment, and stock. A long john with a flat platform (instead of a box) is a versatile work vehicle.

## Choosing a Cargo E-Bike

### Motor
**Mid-drive, 60–85 Nm torque.** Cargo bikes carry heavy loads up hills — you need a strong motor. Hub motors work on flat terrain but struggle with loaded climbs.

**Recommended systems:** Bosch Cargo Line (85 Nm), Shimano EP8 (85 Nm), Bafang M620 (160 Nm for extreme loads).

### Battery
**625 Wh minimum.** Loaded cargo riding drains the battery faster than solo commuting. A 625 Wh battery provides 40–60 km with a full load on mixed terrain. For longer daily use, choose 750 Wh or a dual battery system.

### Brakes
**Hydraulic disc, 180–203 mm rotors.** A loaded cargo bike can weigh 150–200 kg (bike + rider + children + cargo). You need brakes that stop that weight from 25 km/h, every time, in every condition.

### Tyres
**Wide, puncture-resistant.** Schwalbe Pick-Up or Marathon Plus are standard on quality cargo bikes. Wide tyres (50–60 mm) provide stability and comfort under heavy loads.

### Gearing
**Wide range for hills.** Loaded cargo bikes need a very low gear for steep climbs. A 1×12 drivetrain with a 10-51T cassette, or an Enviolo CVT hub, provides the range needed.

### Accessories
- **Rain cover (long john):** essential for carrying children in Australian weather
- **Child harnesses:** non-negotiable for any children riding in the box or on the rack
- **Kickstand:** double-leg kickstand that supports the loaded bike upright
- **Lights:** integrated, battery-powered lights
- **Lock:** a heavy-duty lock — cargo bikes are expensive and targeted

## Practical Considerations

### Storage
Cargo bikes are large. A long john is 2.5–2.8 metres long. It does not fit in a standard apartment bike room. You need:
- A garage or carport
- A covered outdoor space (with a bike cover)
- A dedicated indoor storage area

### Parking
A long john is wider than a standard bike and does not fit in most bike racks. You park it like a motorcycle — alongside racks, against walls, or in designated cargo bike spaces (increasingly common in Australian cities).

A long tail parks like a normal bike — it fits in standard racks.

### Range with Load
Expect 30–50 % less range when fully loaded compared to riding solo. A 625 Wh battery that gives 80 km solo might give 40–55 km with two children and groceries.

### Speed
Loaded cargo bikes are slower to accelerate and slower on hills. Cruise speed on flat ground is 20–23 km/h. Accept this — a cargo bike is not for speed; it is for carrying.

## Bottom Line

A cargo e-bike replaces a second car for school runs, grocery shopping, errands, and short commutes. A long john carries more (1–4 children, large loads) but is harder to park and store. A long tail carries less (1–2 children, moderate loads) but rides and parks like a normal bike. Budget $4,000–$12,000 for a quality cargo e-bike — and save $5,000–$13,000 per year by not running a second car. The motor makes the heavy loads practical; the savings make it economical; and the children love it.

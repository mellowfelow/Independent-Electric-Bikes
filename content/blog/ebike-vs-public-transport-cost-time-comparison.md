Is an e-bike cheaper than public transport? For most Australian commuters, the answer is yes — and the gap is larger than you expect.

## The Cost Comparison

### Public Transport (Melbourne Example)

A daily myki fare for Zone 1+2 costs $10.60 (2026 prices). Weekly cap: $47.70. Annual cost:

| | Daily | Weekly | Annual (48 weeks) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zone 1+2 full fare | $10.60 | $47.70 | $2,290 |
| Zone 1 only | $10.60 | $47.70 | $2,290 |
| Concession (half fare) | $5.30 | $23.85 | $1,145 |

**Sydney Opal:** slightly cheaper with transfer discounts and the weekly cap ($50). Annual cost: ~$2,400.

**Brisbane go card:** similar range, $2,000–$2,400/year for regular commuters.

### E-Bike

| Cost | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | $2,500 (average commuter e-bike) | One-time cost, amortised over 5–7 years |
| Electricity | $30–$60/year | 500 Wh battery, charged 200 times/year at $0.30/kWh |
| Tyres | $60–$100/year | One set per year for daily commuters |

### The Verdict

| | Public transport | E-bike (no insurance) | E-bike (with insurance) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual cost | $2,200–$2,400 | $810–$1,260 | $1,010–$1,660 |

An e-bike saves $500–$1,600 per year compared to public transport — and the savings increase every year after the bike is paid off.

## The Time Comparison

Cost is only half the equation. Time is the other half.

### Public Transport Time

A typical Melbourne commute on public transport:
1. Walk to the stop/station (5–10 min)
2. Wait for the service (5–15 min, depending on frequency)
3. Ride the service (15–40 min, depending on distance and transfers)
4. Walk from the stop to the workplace (5–10 min)
5. **Total: 30–75 minutes door-to-door**

Add 10–20 minutes if you need to transfer between services.

### E-Bike Time

The same commute on an e-bike:
1. Walk to the bike (0–2 min — it is at your door)
2. Ride (distance / 20 km/h average = time)
3. Park and lock (2–5 min)
4. **Total: typically 15–45 minutes door-to-door**

**For a 10 km commute:**
- Public transport: 40–60 minutes (including walking and waiting)
- E-bike: 30–35 minutes (at 20 km/h average including stops)

**For a 15 km commute:**
- Public transport: 50–75 minutes
- E-bike: 45–50 minutes

**For a 5 km commute:**
- Public transport: 25–40 minutes
- E-bike: 15–20 minutes

The e-bike is faster for most commutes under 15 km — and the time is predictable. No waiting for late trains, no missed connections, no signal failures.

## What Public Transport Does Better

- **Long distances (20+ km):** a train covers 20 km in 20 minutes. An e-bike takes 60+ minutes. For long commutes, public transport wins on time.
- **Weather immunity:** trains and buses run in all weather. On an e-bike, you ride in rain, heat, and wind.
- **Multitasking:** on a train, you can read, work on a laptop, or sleep. On an e-bike, you ride.
- **No theft risk:** you do not need to lock a train.
- **No fitness required:** riding an e-bike is easy, but it still requires some physical effort. Public transport requires none.
- **No gear required:** no helmet, no lights, no rain jacket, no bike lock.

## What the E-Bike Does Better

- **Door-to-door:** no walking to/from stops, no waiting, no transfers
- **Reliability:** no service delays, cancellations, or overcrowding
- **Exercise:** 30 minutes of moderate exercise per day, built into your commute
- **Flexibility:** leave when you want, take any route, stop anywhere
- **No crowds:** no standing in packed carriages, no antisocial passengers
- **Weekend and off-peak:** public transport frequency drops on evenings and weekends. The e-bike is always available.
- **Mental health:** outdoor exercise reduces stress, anxiety, and depression — benefits that a train commute does not provide

## The Hybrid Approach

Many commuters combine both:
- **E-bike to the station, train for the long leg:** ride 3–5 km to the station, lock the bike, take the train for 15–30 km, walk from the destination station. This combines the e-bike's door-to-station speed with the train's long-distance efficiency.
- **Folding e-bike on the train:** fold the bike, carry it on the train, unfold at the destination station. No locking, no theft risk, complete door-to-door flexibility. Folding e-bikes weigh 17–22 kg — manageable but not light.
- **E-bike in good weather, public transport in bad:** ride when it is dry, take the train when it rains. This maximises the health and cost benefits while avoiding wet riding.

## Break-Even Analysis

At what point does the e-bike pay for itself compared to public transport?

**Assumptions:**
- E-bike purchase price: $2,500
- Annual running cost: $600
- Public transport annual cost: $2,300
- Net annual saving: $1,700

**Break-even: 1.5 years.** After 18 months, the e-bike has paid for itself. Every year after that is $1,700 in savings.

If you add insurance ($300/year), break-even extends to about 1.8 years. Still under two years.

## Bottom Line

For commutes under 15 km, an e-bike is cheaper and often faster than public transport in every Australian capital city. The annual saving is $500–$1,600 depending on your route and whether you insure the bike. The break-even point is under two years. The bonus — daily exercise, fresh air, and zero dependence on service reliability — is worth more than the financial saving alone.

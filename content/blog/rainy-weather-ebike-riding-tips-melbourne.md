Melbourne averages 150+ rain days per year. If you only ride in dry weather, you lose a third of your commuting days. With the right preparation, rain riding on an e-bike is safe, comfortable, and — once you get used to it — completely routine.

## Is It Safe to Ride an E-Bike in the Rain?

Yes. Modern e-bikes are designed for wet conditions. Batteries, motors, controllers, and displays are sealed to at least IP54 (protected against splashing water from all directions). Most quality brands rate their components to IPX5 or higher.

**What you should NOT do:**
- Submerge the battery or motor (avoid riding through deep puddles or flooded sections)
- Pressure-wash the bike (high-pressure water forces past seals)
- Charge the battery while it is wet (dry the charging port first)

**What is perfectly fine:**
- Riding in steady rain for your entire commute
- Riding through shallow puddles (under 5 cm)
- Parking outside in the rain for a few hours

## Tyre Setup: The Most Important Factor

Traction on wet roads depends almost entirely on your tyres.

### Tyre Pressure

**Lower your pressure by 10–15 %** when riding in rain. This increases the contact patch (the area of tyre touching the road), which improves grip. If you normally run 65 PSI, drop to 55–60 PSI for wet days.

Do not go too low — under-inflated tyres feel sluggish, increase rolling resistance, and risk pinch flats.

### Tyre Tread

On sealed roads, tread pattern matters less than rubber compound. Slick or semi-slick tyres with a soft rubber compound grip wet bitumen better than heavily treaded tyres with a hard compound. The common belief that slicks are dangerous in the wet is a myth — it applies to cars travelling fast enough for aquaplaning (80+ km/h), not bicycles at 25 km/h.

**Recommended tyres for wet commuting:**
- Schwalbe Marathon Plus — puncture-resistant, good wet grip, the default commuter tyre
- Continental Contact Plus — similar performance, slightly softer compound
- Schwalbe Marathon GT 365 — a four-season tyre with a reflective sidewall stripe

### Puncture Protection

Wet roads lift debris — glass, wire, thorns — that dry roads keep pinned to the surface. Puncture-resistant tyres (with a Kevlar or SmartGuard belt) are essential for rain commuting. Fixing a flat in the rain on the side of a busy road is miserable.

## Braking in the Rain

Wet conditions increase stopping distance by 30–50 %. Adjust your riding accordingly:

- **Start braking earlier.** Give yourself twice the dry-weather stopping distance at intersections.
- **Use both brakes evenly.** Front brake bias can cause a front-wheel slide on wet surfaces. Apply both brakes progressively.
- **Scrub the rotors.** On the first few stops after rain begins, lightly drag the brakes to clear the water film from the disc rotors. Hydraulic brakes recover quickly — usually within one wheel revolution.
- **Avoid painted road markings.** White lane markings, pedestrian crossings, and arrow markings are extremely slippery when wet. Ride around them, not over them.
- **Avoid metal surfaces.** Tram tracks, drain grates, manhole covers, and bridge expansion joints are almost frictionless when wet. Cross them at a right angle (perpendicular) to minimise the chance of a wheel slipping.

## Visibility

Rain reduces driver visibility. You are harder to see in the rain than in dry conditions — especially at dawn, dusk, and in heavy downpours.

- **Run lights in every rain ride**, even during the day. Front white, rear red, both on flash mode.
- **Wear a high-visibility rain jacket** — bright yellow or orange, with reflective panels.
- **Reflective tyre sidewalls** add visibility from the side, where drivers at intersections need to see you.
- **Avoid riding directly behind trucks and buses** — their spray creates a blind zone for following traffic.

## Clothing and Gear

### Rain Jacket

A cycling-specific rain jacket is cut longer in the back (covers your lower back when leaning forward), has sealed seams, and is breathable enough to vent body heat.

**Budget picks:** Decathlon Btwin 500 rain jacket ($40–$60). Does the job for a 30-minute commute.

**Premium picks:** Gore Wear C3 or Endura Hummvee ($150–$250). Better breathability for longer rides, more durable water repellency over time.

### Rain Pants or Overshoes

Your lower half gets wetter than your upper half (wheel spray, road spray, rain running down your jacket). Waterproof overpants or shoe covers keep you dry below the waist.

If you wear work clothes, change at the office — do not try to ride in office trousers with overpants. Carry your work clothes in a waterproof pannier.

### Mudguards

**Full-length mudguards are non-negotiable for rain riding.** Without them, a wet road sends a continuous rooster tail of dirty water up your back and into your face. Front and rear guards, extending close to the ground, block 90 % of this spray.

SKS Bluemels are the benchmark. If your bike does not have mudguard mounting eyelets, clip-on guards (SKS Speedrocker or Crud Roadracer) are a compromise — less effective but better than nothing.

### Gloves

Waterproof cycling gloves keep your hands warm and functional in cold rain. Wet, cold hands lose grip strength and reaction time. Neoprene gloves (2–3 mm) work well for temperatures above 5 °C. Below that, insulated waterproof gloves are necessary.

### Glasses or Visor

Rain in your eyes is blinding. Clear or yellow-tinted cycling glasses keep water out of your eyes while maintaining visibility. A helmet with a visor (like a mountain bike helmet) also works.

## Post-Ride Maintenance

Riding in the rain is fine. Leaving the bike wet and dirty afterwards accelerates wear.

After every wet ride:

1. **Wipe down the frame, chain, and drivetrain** with a dry cloth
2. **Re-lube the chain** — rain washes off chain lubricant. Use a wet-rated lube (e.g., Finish Line Wet) for the rain season
3. **Dry the charging port** before plugging in
4. **Check brake pads** — wet riding wears brake pads faster than dry riding. Inspect weekly during the wet season
5. **Clean the battery contacts** if you notice any corrosion (a cotton bud with contact cleaner)

Once a week during the wet season, do a more thorough clean: rinse the bike with a gentle hose (not a pressure washer), clean the drivetrain with degreaser, re-lube, and check bolt torques.

## Route Planning for Wet Weather

- **Prefer bike paths over roads.** Less traffic spray, no tram tracks, fewer painted markings.
- **Avoid known flood spots.** If a section of your route floods in heavy rain, plan an alternative.
- **Allow extra time.** Wet-weather commuting is 10–15 % slower than dry — lower speeds, more cautious braking, and slower intersections.
- **Check the forecast before you leave.** A light shower is fine. A severe thunderstorm with lightning is not — lightning and a metal bicycle frame is a bad combination. Wait it out.

## Bottom Line

Rain riding on an e-bike is safe and practical with the right tyres, mudguards, brakes, lights, and clothing. The biggest adjustment is mental — once you ride through your first wet commute and arrive dry under your rain jacket, it stops feeling like a big deal. Melbourne riders who commit to all-weather commuting reclaim 50+ riding days per year that fair-weather riders lose.

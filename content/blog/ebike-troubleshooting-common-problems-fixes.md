Most e-bike problems have simple causes and simple fixes. Before you take the bike to a shop, check this list — you can solve 80 % of issues at home with basic tools.

## Motor Not Engaging

### Symptoms
You pedal, but the motor does not kick in. No assist, no power.

### Causes and Fixes

**1. Battery not connected or not turned on.**
- Check that the battery is fully seated in its mount (push it until it clicks).
- Turn the battery on (most have a power button — press it).
- Check the battery charge level. If it is flat, charge it.

**2. Display not turned on.**
- The motor will not engage until the display is powered on and an assist level is selected.
- Press the power button on the display.
- Select an assist level (Eco, Tour, Sport, or Turbo — not Off).

**3. Brake lever sensor stuck.**
- E-bikes cut motor power when you pull the brakes. If a brake sensor is stuck in the "braking" position, the motor stays off permanently.
- Check both brake levers — are they returning fully to the released position?
- Check the brake sensor magnets (small magnets near the brake lever). If one has shifted, the sensor reads "braking" constantly. Realign the magnet.

**4. Speed sensor misaligned.**
- Pedal-assist systems use a cadence or torque sensor on the crank. If the sensor magnet is misaligned, the system does not detect pedalling.
- Check the sensor magnet on the crank arm (or the magnet ring on the bottom bracket). It should be 1–3 mm from the sensor.
- Check the sensor wire — is it connected? Is it damaged?

**5. Loose connector.**
- E-bikes have multiple electrical connectors (motor, battery, display, sensors). A loose or corroded connector breaks the circuit.
- Check all visible connectors. Unplug and replug each one firmly. Look for corrosion or bent pins.

## Battery Not Charging

### Symptoms
You plug in the charger, but the battery does not charge (no indicator light change, or the light stays red).

### Causes and Fixes

**1. Charger not working.**
- Check the charger's indicator light. Most chargers have a light that turns on when plugged into the wall (before connecting to the battery). If no light, the charger may be dead.
- Try a different power outlet.
- Check the charger cable for damage.

**2. Battery too hot or too cold.**
- Most BMS (Battery Management Systems) prevent charging if the battery is above 45 °C or below 0 °C.
- Bring the battery indoors to room temperature (20–25 °C) and try again.

**3. BMS shutdown.**
- If the battery was fully discharged (below the safe voltage), the BMS may have shut down to protect the cells.
- Leave the charger connected for 30–60 minutes. Some BMS units need time to "wake up" from a deep discharge.
- If the battery still will not charge after an hour, the BMS or cells may be damaged. Take it to a shop.

**4. Dirty charging port.**
- Dust, dirt, or moisture in the charging port can prevent connection.
- Clean the port with a dry cloth or compressed air. Do not use water.

## Reduced Range

### Symptoms
The battery drains much faster than it used to. Where you once got 50 km, you now get 30 km.

### Causes and Fixes

**1. Battery age.**
- Lithium-ion batteries degrade over time. After 500–800 charge cycles, capacity drops to 70–80 % of the original. This is normal.
- If the battery is 3+ years old and heavily used, reduced range is expected. The fix is a new battery.

**2. Cold weather.**
- Battery capacity drops 10–30 % in cold weather (below 10 °C). This is temporary — capacity returns when the battery warms up.
- Store the battery indoors overnight. Start the ride with a warm battery.

**3. Tyre pressure too low.**
- Under-inflated tyres increase rolling resistance, which drains the battery faster.
- Check tyre pressure weekly. Inflate to the recommended PSI (printed on the tyre sidewall).

**4. Higher assist level than usual.**
- If you have been riding in Turbo instead of Eco, range drops dramatically.
- Switch to a lower assist level for longer range.

**5. Headwind or hillier route.**
- Environmental factors affect range. A windy day or a hillier route uses more energy.
- This is normal. Adjust expectations or carry a spare battery for long rides.

## Unusual Noises

### Clicking

**From the crank area:**
- Loose crank arm bolts. Tighten to the specified torque (usually 35–40 Nm for a mid-drive, 12–14 Nm for pedals).
- Loose bottom bracket. This requires a bike shop to service.
- Loose chainring bolts. Tighten evenly.

**From the wheel:**
- Loose spoke. Squeeze spokes in pairs — if any are noticeably loose, they need tensioning (bike shop job).
- Something caught in the spokes (cable tie, leaf, zip tie).

### Grinding

**From the motor:**
- On a mid-drive: this often indicates worn gears inside the motor. Book a motor service.
- On a hub motor: bearing wear. The motor needs new bearings (bike shop job — hub motor bearings last 5,000–15,000 km).

**From the brakes:**
- Contaminated brake pads (oil, lubricant, or cleaning product on the pads or rotor). Clean the rotor with isopropyl alcohol. If the pads are contaminated, replace them (brake pads: $20–$40 per pair).
- Worn brake pads. Replace when the pad material is thinner than 1 mm.

### Whining or Humming

**From the motor (normal):**
- Hub motors hum at higher speeds. This is normal electromagnetic noise.
- Mid-drive motors may whine under heavy load (steep hill, high assist). Normal.

**Loud or new whining:**
- Could indicate a bearing starting to fail. If the sound is new and getting louder, book a service.

## Display Errors

### Error Codes
Most e-bike displays show error codes when something is wrong. Common codes:

| Code pattern | Typical meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| E01 / Error 1 | Motor communication error | Check motor connector. Power cycle the bike. |
| E02 / Error 2 | Throttle error | Check throttle connector and cable. |
| E03 / Error 3 | Hall sensor error (motor) | Motor service needed (bike shop). |
| E04 / Error 4 | Brake sensor error | Check brake sensors and magnets. |
| E05 / Error 5 | Controller overheating | Stop riding. Let the controller cool down. |
| E06 / Error 6 | Battery communication error | Check battery connector. Reseat the battery. |
| E07 / Error 7 | Speed sensor error | Check speed sensor alignment (1–3 mm gap). |

**Note:** error codes vary by manufacturer. Check your e-bike's manual for the specific codes. Most manuals are available online as PDFs.

### Display Blank / Not Turning On
- Check the display connector (where the cable plugs into the display unit and the main harness).
- Check the battery is on and charged.
- Try a hard reset: disconnect the battery for 30 seconds, reconnect, and power on.

## Chain Dropping

### Causes and Fixes
- **Front chainring worn or bent.** Inspect the teeth — shark-fin-shaped teeth indicate a worn chainring. Replace it.
- **Chain too long.** A stretched or too-long chain does not tension properly. Replace the chain (every 2,000–4,000 km on an e-bike).
- **Derailleur limit screws misadjusted.** The limit screws prevent the chain from falling off the smallest and largest cogs. Adjust them (YouTube has excellent guides for your specific derailleur model).
- **Chain guide missing.** Many e-bikes with mid-drive motors use a chain guide to prevent chain drop. If yours was removed or damaged, reinstall or replace it.

## When to Go to a Bike Shop

Some problems need professional tools and expertise:
- Motor internal issues (grinding, intermittent power, error codes that do not clear)
- Battery cell replacement or BMS repair
- Hydraulic brake bleeding
- Wheel truing and spoke tensioning
- Bottom bracket replacement
- Suspension fork servicing
- Firmware updates (some brands require dealer-only software)

**Annual service:** even if nothing seems wrong, book an annual service. A mechanic will catch wear issues (chain stretch, brake pad wear, bearing play) before they become failures.

## Bottom Line

Most e-bike problems come from loose connectors, flat batteries, misaligned sensors, or normal wear items (chains, brake pads, tyres). Before visiting a shop, check the battery, display, connectors, brake sensors, and speed sensor. Carry a basic toolkit (Allen keys, tyre levers, pump, spare tube) and know how to fix a flat and reset your display. For motor internals, battery issues, and hydraulic brakes, a qualified e-bike mechanic is the right call.

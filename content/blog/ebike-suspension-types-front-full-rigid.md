Suspension absorbs bumps. On a regular bike, the difference between rigid, front suspension, and full suspension is a comfort and control question. On an e-bike — heavier, faster, and ridden on worse roads — suspension becomes a practical question about what you can ride without damaging the bike or yourself.

## The Three Types

### Rigid (No Suspension)

No suspension fork, no rear shock. The frame and tyres are the only things absorbing road vibration.

**Weight:** lightest option. No suspension components to add weight.

**Maintenance:** zero suspension maintenance. Nothing to service, nothing to fail.

**Efficiency:** most efficient. No energy lost to suspension movement (suspension "bob" absorbs some pedalling force on soft setups).

**Comfort:** least comfortable on rough surfaces. Every bump transmits directly to your hands, arms, and saddle. On smooth sealed roads, this is fine. On gravel, potholes, or broken pavement, it is tiring over long distances.

**Best for:** smooth road commuting, flat bike paths, riders on a budget, riders who prioritise low weight and zero maintenance.

**Tyre compensation:** wide tyres (40–50 mm) at moderate pressure provide significant vibration damping on a rigid bike. A rigid e-bike with 45 mm tyres rides more comfortably than a rigid bike with 25 mm tyres.

### Hardtail (Front Suspension Only)

A suspension fork on the front wheel. The rear is rigid (no rear shock).

**Travel:** 80–120 mm for commuting and light trail riding. 120–160 mm for mountain biking.

**Weight:** adds 1–2 kg over rigid.

**Maintenance:** fork service every 50–100 hours of riding (or annually). Involves replacing the oil and seals. Cost: $50–$150 at a bike shop.

**Comfort:** significantly more comfortable than rigid over rough surfaces. The fork absorbs potholes, kerb drops, roots, and rocks. Your hands and arms stay comfortable on long rough rides.

**Efficiency:** small energy loss from fork bob (suspension compressing under pedalling force). Most quality forks have a lockout lever — lock the fork on smooth roads for full efficiency, unlock it on rough roads.

**Best for:** most e-bike riders. A hardtail with 100 mm travel handles urban commuting, gravel paths, light trails, and touring. It is the best balance of comfort, weight, maintenance, and cost.

### Full Suspension (Front + Rear)

A suspension fork on the front and a rear shock (with a pivoting rear triangle or flex stay).

**Travel:** 100–120 mm for trail riding. 140–170 mm for enduro/all-mountain. 180–200 mm for downhill.

**Weight:** adds 2–4 kg over rigid. A full-suspension e-bike typically weighs 22–28 kg.

**Maintenance:** fork service + rear shock service every 50–100 hours. Pivot bearings need checking annually. More components = more maintenance. Cost: $100–$300 per year.

**Comfort:** the most comfortable over any surface. The rear shock absorbs bumps that a hardtail sends through the saddle and spine. On rough trails, the rear wheel tracks the ground better — more traction, more control.

**Efficiency:** more energy loss than hardtail (rear suspension absorbs some pedalling energy). Modern designs minimise this (anti-squat geometry, lockout levers), but full suspension is always less efficient than hardtail on smooth roads. On an e-bike, the motor compensates — this efficiency loss matters less than on a regular bike.

**Best for:** mountain e-biking, rough trails, riders with back problems (rear suspension reduces spinal load), and riders who prioritise comfort above all else.

## Suspension Components

### The Fork

The front suspension fork contains two key elements:

**Spring:** supports the rider's weight and controls how the fork compresses.
- **Coil spring:** a metal coil inside each fork leg. Simple, reliable, consistent feel. Heavier. Found on budget to mid-range forks ($100–$400).
- **Air spring:** a sealed air chamber. Lighter, adjustable (change spring rate by adding or removing air with a shock pump). Found on mid-range to premium forks ($300–$1,500+).

**Damper:** controls how fast the fork compresses and rebounds.
- **Compression damping:** controls how fast the fork compresses (absorbs the bump). More compression damping = firmer feel, less dive under braking.
- **Rebound damping:** controls how fast the fork extends after compressing. Too fast = the fork bounces (pogo stick). Too slow = the fork stays compressed (packs down on repeated bumps). Adjust rebound so the fork returns smoothly without bouncing.

### The Rear Shock

Same principles as the fork — spring (air or coil) + damper (compression + rebound). Rear shocks are smaller and lighter than forks because the rear wheel carries less of the braking load.

**Key spec: stroke length.** The rear shock stroke multiplied by the frame's leverage ratio determines rear wheel travel. A 55 mm stroke shock with a 2.5:1 leverage ratio gives 137 mm of rear wheel travel.

## Suspension Setup for E-Bikes

E-bikes are heavier than regular bikes (20–28 kg vs 10–14 kg). This means:
- The rider + bike system weighs more, so suspension springs need to be stiffer
- More weight compresses the suspension further at rest (more "sag")
- The motor adds torque through the drivetrain, which can cause pedal-induced bob on full suspension

### Sag
Sag is how much the suspension compresses under the rider's weight (without moving). Correct sag ensures the suspension works through its full range.

**Target sag:**
- Fork: 20–25 % of total travel
- Rear shock: 25–30 % of total travel

**How to set:** sit on the bike in riding position. Measure how much the fork/shock compresses. Adjust air pressure (air spring) or swap coil springs (coil spring) until sag is correct. On an e-bike, include the battery weight — set sag with the battery installed.

### Rebound
Set rebound so the suspension returns to full extension in about 1 second after compressing. Too fast (bouncy) is dangerous on rough ground — the bike can throw you. Too slow makes the suspension feel dead and unresponsive.

### Lockout
Lock out the suspension on smooth roads. This eliminates bob and makes the bike more efficient. Unlock for rough surfaces. Some forks have a remote lockout lever on the handlebars — convenient for switching on the fly.

## Suspension Alternatives

### Suspension Seatpost
A telescoping seatpost with a spring or elastomer that absorbs vertical bumps. Adds 30–50 mm of rear-end compliance without the weight and complexity of full rear suspension.

**Cost:** $50–$200.
**Best for:** rigid and hardtail e-bikes where rear comfort is needed but full suspension is not justified.

### Wide Tyres at Low Pressure
Tyres are the first line of vibration absorption. Running wider tyres (40–50 mm) at moderate pressure (30–40 PSI) provides meaningful cushioning over rough roads. This is free, adds negligible weight, and works on any bike.

### Ergon or Gel Grips
Padded grips with ergonomic shapes reduce hand vibration and pressure. Cost: $30–$60. Effective on rigid and hardtail bikes for hand comfort.

## Bottom Line

For most e-bike commuters and recreational riders, a hardtail (front suspension, 100 mm travel) is the right choice — it handles urban roads, gravel, and light trails comfortably with minimal maintenance. Rigid is fine for smooth roads and budget builds. Full suspension is for mountain e-biking and riders with back problems who need maximum comfort. On an e-bike, the motor compensates for suspension weight and efficiency losses, so full suspension is more practical than on a regular bike. Set sag correctly (heavier system than a regular bike), adjust rebound, and lock out the fork on smooth roads.

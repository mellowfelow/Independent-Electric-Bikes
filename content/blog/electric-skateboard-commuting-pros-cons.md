# Commuting on an Electric Skateboard: Pros, Cons, and Practical Advice

Electric skateboards occupy a strange spot in the commuting world. They are faster than walking, lighter than e-bikes, and more fun than the bus. They are also less practical than any of those in certain situations. This guide looks honestly at what works and what does not when using an electric skateboard as daily transport in Australia.

## The Case For

**Speed.** A typical electric skateboard cruises at 25 to 30 km/h on flat ground. That is significantly faster than walking (5 km/h) or cycling at a casual pace (15 to 20 km/h). A 5 km commute takes about 10 to 12 minutes on an electric skateboard.

**Portability.** Most electric skateboards weigh 6 to 9 kg and can be carried under one arm or strapped to a backpack. You can take them on trains, buses, and into offices without the logistical hassle of locking up and securing a bicycle or e-bike.

**Cost.** After the initial purchase (typically $800 to $2,000 for a commuter-quality board), running costs are minimal. Electricity to charge is negligible. Maintenance costs are low. Compare that to fuel, parking, insurance, and registration for a car, or even public transport fares over time.

**No parking.** You carry it inside. No hunting for bike racks, no worrying about theft, no paying for parking.

**Last mile solution.** Electric skateboards excel as the connection between public transport and your destination. Drive to the train station, ride the train, skate the last 2 km to the office. This combination can make public transport viable for trips that would otherwise require a car because the station is too far from your workplace.

**Fun.** This is subjective but worth mentioning. Commuting on an electric skateboard is genuinely enjoyable in a way that driving or taking the bus is not. Starting your workday with a ride in the morning air is a real quality-of-life improvement for many people.

## The Case Against

**Weather dependence.** Rain kills an electric skateboard commute. Wet surfaces are dangerously slippery on urethane wheels, and most boards are not waterproof. In Melbourne, which averages rain on about 150 days per year, you need a backup plan for roughly 40 percent of your commutes. Sydney and Brisbane are drier but still have wet periods.

**Road surfaces.** Australian urban infrastructure is not built for small-wheeled vehicles. Cracked footpaths, raised pavers, gravel patches, and poorly maintained bike lanes are common. Each is a potential hazard on an electric skateboard that would barely register on a bicycle.

**No exercise.** Unlike cycling, electric skateboarding provides minimal physical exercise. If your commute doubles as your fitness routine, a skateboard will not serve that purpose.

**Cargo capacity.** You can carry a backpack. That is about it. Groceries, large bags, or anything bulky is impractical to carry while riding. If your commute regularly includes errands, an electric skateboard is limiting.

**Sweat and appearance.** On hot Australian summer days, even standing on a board at 30 km/h generates wind that helps, but you are still arriving at work after standing in the sun. In full safety gear (helmet, pads, gloves), you will need to change when you arrive. Factor in the time and facilities for this.

**Safety risk.** Electric skateboards are the least protected form of powered transport. No frame, no handlebars, no seat, no crumple zone. A fall at commuting speed on hard pavement causes real injuries. This risk is manageable with gear and experience, but it is not zero.

**Legal ambiguity.** In most Australian states, the legal status of electric skateboards on roads and footpaths is unclear or restrictive. Riding may technically be illegal even where it is commonly done and not enforced.

## Route Planning

Not every commute route works for an electric skateboard. Before committing to skateboard commuting, ride your entire route on a weekend to assess it.

**Surface quality.** Note any sections with rough pavement, gravel, construction zones, or road crossings with high kerbs. A route that looks fine in a car may have obstacles you never noticed.

**Traffic.** Assess how much of your route has dedicated bike lanes or shared paths versus riding on the road or footpath alongside traffic. Higher traffic means higher risk.

**Hills.** Check the elevation profile of your route. A hill that is barely noticeable in a car can be a challenge on an electric skateboard, both climbing (battery drain) and descending (braking demands). Apps like Google Maps or Strava show elevation data.

**Distance.** Your route needs to be well within your board's range, accounting for real-world range reduction. A board rated at 25 km range might reliably deliver 16 to 18 km in practice. Your commute should be comfortably within this figure each way, or you need charging access at your destination.

**Alternate routes.** Identify quieter streets or paths that parallel the main road. A slightly longer route on a smooth shared path is often faster and safer than a direct route on a busy road with poor surfaces.

## Essential Gear

**Helmet.** Non-negotiable. An AS/NZS 2063 certified helmet at minimum.

**Gloves.** Slide gloves protect your palms in a fall. Even basic padded cycling gloves are better than nothing.

**Lights.** If you ride at dawn, dusk, or in overcast conditions, you need to be visible. Front and rear lights attached to the board or your body are essential. Australian drivers are not looking for skateboarders.

**High-visibility clothing.** A reflective vest or bright jacket takes seconds to put on and significantly improves your visibility.

**Backpack.** A comfortable backpack for your work items, charger, and any gear you remove after arriving. Look for one with a laptop compartment and board straps if your board can be secured externally.

**Spare shoes.** Flat-soled skate shoes are best for riding but may not suit your workplace. Carry work shoes in your bag.

## The Multimodal Commute

For many people, the best use of an electric skateboard is not riding the entire commute, but combining it with other transport.

**Train plus skateboard.** Park at or walk to the station, ride the train, skate the final stretch to work. The skateboard handles the last-mile problem that makes many people drive instead of taking public transport.

**Car plus skateboard.** Drive to a car park near but not at your destination (where parking is cheaper or free), then skate the rest. This works well in CBDs where parking is expensive.

**Bus plus skateboard.** Similar to the train approach. Check your local bus operator's policy on carrying boards, though most are fine with them during off-peak times.

This multimodal approach sidesteps many of the downsides of pure skateboard commuting: shorter distance means less weather exposure, less battery concern, and less time in traffic.

## Wet Weather Backup

You need a plan for days when you cannot ride. Options include:

- Public transport for the full journey
- Driving on wet days
- Rideshare for the last mile
- Simply walking the stretch you would normally skate

Do not try to ride in the rain. The risk of a fall on wet surfaces is high, and water damage to your board can be expensive.

## Workplace Considerations

**Storage.** Most electric skateboards fit under a desk or in a cupboard. If your office does not have suitable storage, talk to your employer. A board takes up less space than a bicycle.

**Charging.** If you need to charge at work, you only need a standard power outlet. Charging is quiet and draws minimal power. Most employers are fine with this.

**Appearance.** If you arrive in safety gear and skate shoes, you may need to change. Plan for this with a locker or a bag with work clothes and shoes.

## Is It Worth It

Electric skateboard commuting works best for people with:
- A commute of 3 to 10 km each way, or a last-mile problem with public transport
- Routes with reasonable surface quality and limited traffic
- Tolerance for weather disruptions and a backup plan
- A workplace that accommodates board storage and possibly changing
- A genuine enjoyment of riding

It works less well for people who:
- Need to carry significant cargo
- Commute on busy roads with no bike lane alternative
- Rely on their commute for exercise
- Cannot accept any legal ambiguity
- Need to arrive at work in formal attire with no time or facility to change

For the right person and the right route, an electric skateboard is a fast, cheap, portable, and genuinely enjoyable way to commute. For the wrong situation, it is an impractical novelty that spends most of its time under your desk. Be honest about which category your commute falls into before investing.

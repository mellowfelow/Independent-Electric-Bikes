Electric bikes sit in a unique legal space across Australia. Each state and territory sets its own rules, but a national standard underpins them all. If you ride in Victoria — or plan to — here is what you need to know in 2026.

## The National Framework: EN 15194

Australia adopts the European EN 15194 standard for pedal-assist e-bikes. A bike that meets this standard is treated as a regular bicycle — no registration, no licence, no insurance required. The key limits are:

- **Motor**: 250 W continuous rated output (peak can be higher)
- **Speed**: power assist must cut out at 25 km/h
- **Pedal-assist only**: the motor engages only when you pedal
- **No throttle** on bikes classified as bicycles (with exceptions for walk-assist up to 6 km/h)

Bikes that exceed these limits — whether by wattage, speed, or throttle — fall into motorised vehicle categories and may need registration, a licence, and CTP insurance depending on the state.

## Victorian-Specific Rules

In Victoria, VicRoads classifies e-bikes into two categories:

**Pedal-assist (pedelec):** meets EN 15194 (250 W, 25 km/h cut-off). Treated as a bicycle. Can ride on roads, bike lanes, and shared paths. Helmet mandatory.

**Power-assisted bicycle:** motor up to 200 W, can have a throttle but speed limited to 25 km/h. Also treated as a bicycle. Increasingly rare on the market.

Bikes with motors above 250 W or speeds above 25 km/h are classified as motorised vehicles. You cannot ride them on bike paths, and they may need registration.

## Where You Can and Cannot Ride

In Victoria, bicycles (including compliant e-bikes) can ride on:

- Roads (with traffic, or in designated bike lanes)
- Shared-use paths (the 25 km/h limit is advisory, not enforceable, but courtesy matters)
- Separated bike paths (such as the Capital City Trail or Bay Trail)

You **cannot** ride on footpaths if you are over 12 years old, unless you are accompanying a child under 12.

## Helmet Laws

Victoria has mandatory helmet laws for all cyclists, including e-bike riders, with no exceptions. The helmet must meet AS/NZS 2063 or an equivalent standard. Fines for not wearing a helmet start at roughly $227.

For e-bike riders, particularly those on faster models, we recommend a helmet with MIPS (Multi-directional Impact Protection System) technology, which adds rotational impact protection.

## Buying an E-Bike: Compliance Checklist

Before purchasing, confirm:

1. The motor is rated 250 W continuous (the label should state this)
2. Assist cuts out at 25 km/h
3. The bike has an EN 15194 compliance sticker or equivalent documentation
4. Any throttle is limited to walk-assist speeds (6 km/h or under)

If you are buying online, especially from overseas, verify that the bike meets Australian standards. Bikes marketed as "500 W" or "750 W" may not be street-legal in Australia without derestriction (which is itself not legal for road use).

## The Bottom Line

Riding a compliant e-bike in Victoria and across Australia is straightforward: stay under 250 W, let the motor cut out at 25 km/h, wear an AS/NZS 2063 helmet, and follow the same road rules as any other cyclist. If your bike exceeds these limits, you need to understand the registration and licensing requirements in your state before riding on public roads.

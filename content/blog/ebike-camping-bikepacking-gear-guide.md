Bikepacking on an e-bike opens up routes that would exhaust you on a regular bike — longer distances, steeper terrain, and heavier loads. The motor handles the weight penalty that carrying camping gear creates. Here is how to set up an e-bike for multi-day camping trips in Australia.

## Why E-Bikes Work for Bikepacking

Traditional bikepacking demands ultralight gear because every gram adds to your pedalling effort. On an e-bike, the motor compensates for extra weight — you can carry a real tent, a proper sleeping pad, and food for days without suffering on every climb.

**The trade-off:** battery range. A loaded e-bike (bike + rider + 15–25 kg of gear) on hilly terrain drains the battery 2–3× faster than an unburdened commute on flat ground. Range planning becomes the central challenge.

## Gear Setup

### Carrying System

**Bikepacking bags (frame-mounted):**
- **Seat bag (10–15 L):** straps to the seatpost and saddle rails. Carries sleeping bag and clothing.
- **Frame bag (3–8 L):** fits inside the main triangle. Carries tools, snacks, electronics. On e-bikes, the battery may occupy this space — check before buying.
- **Handlebar bag (8–15 L):** rolls onto the handlebars. Carries the tent or tarp.
- **Fork bags (2–5 L each):** strap to the fork legs. Carry water bottles, fuel, or small items.
- **Top tube bag (1–2 L):** sits on the top tube for quick-access items (phone, snacks, sunscreen).

**Panniers (rack-mounted):**
- Rear panniers on a rack provide 40–60 L of organised, waterproof storage. Easier to pack and access than bikepacking bags, but heavier and less aerodynamic.
- Front panniers (on a front rack) distribute weight more evenly but add wind resistance and affect steering.

**Recommendation for e-bike bikepacking:** rear panniers on a rack for the bulk of your gear, plus a handlebar bag for the tent and a top tube bag for quick-access items. The motor handles the weight; prioritise organisation and waterproofing over ultralight obsession.

### Camping Gear Checklist

**Shelter:**
- Tent (1–2 person, 1.5–2.5 kg) or tarp and bivy
- Ground sheet (if using a tarp)

**Sleep:**
- Sleeping bag (rated to the lowest expected temperature — in Australian spring/autumn, a 5 °C comfort bag; in summer, a 10–15 °C bag or a liner)
- Sleeping pad (inflatable for comfort, closed-cell foam for reliability)

**Cooking:**
- Stove (compact gas stove, 100–200 g) and fuel canister
- Pot (750 ml titanium or aluminium)
- Utensils (spork, pocket knife)
- Lighter or waterproof matches
- Dehydrated meals or simple ingredients

**Water:**
- Water bottles (2× 750 ml minimum) or a hydration bladder
- Water filter or purification tablets (essential for backcountry camping where water sources are creeks and rivers)

**Clothing:**
- Riding kit (what you ride in)
- Camp clothes (warm layer, dry socks, sleep shirt)
- Rain jacket (packable, waterproof)
- Warm layer (merino or synthetic, not cotton — cotton kills)

**Tools and spares:**
- Multi-tool with chain breaker
- Spare inner tube (or tubeless repair kit)
- Tyre levers
- Pump (mini pump or CO2 inflator)
- Patch kit
- Spare chain links
- Spare brake pads
- Cable ties and gaffer tape

**Electronics:**
- Phone (navigation, camera, emergency)
- E-bike charger
- Power bank (10,000–20,000 mAh for phone charging when off-grid)
- Headlamp (for camp)
- Front and rear bike lights

### Total Weight

A realistic bikepacking setup (gear + water + food for 2 days):
- Lightweight: 10–15 kg
- Standard: 15–20 kg
- Comfortable (chair, real pillow, extra food): 20–25 kg

On an e-bike with 80 Nm of torque, carrying 20 kg of gear is manageable. On a hub motor with 40 Nm, 15 kg is the practical limit before hills become a struggle.

## Range Planning

This is the critical difference between e-bike bikepacking and regular bikepacking. You must plan your battery use.

### Estimating Daily Range

A loaded e-bike on mixed terrain uses roughly:
- **Flat, sealed roads:** 8–12 Wh/km
- **Rolling hills:** 12–18 Wh/km
- **Steep, rough terrain:** 18–30 Wh/km

For a 500 Wh battery on rolling terrain (15 Wh/km average): **33 km per charge**
For a 625 Wh battery on the same terrain: **42 km per charge**
For a 750 Wh battery: **50 km per charge**

### Charging Options

- **Campground power outlets:** many Australian caravan parks and campgrounds have powered sites. Charge overnight — a full charge takes 4–6 hours.
- **Town stops:** charge at cafes, libraries, or pubs. Ask politely — most places will let you plug in while you eat or drink. A 2-hour lunch stop gives you 30–50 % charge.
- **Solar panels:** a portable 60–100 W folding solar panel can charge a power bank that charges the e-bike battery. This is slow (a full charge may take all day in good sun) but provides off-grid independence.
- **Spare battery:** the most reliable solution for remote areas. A second battery adds 3–5 kg but doubles your range.

### Planning Your Route

1. Map the total distance and elevation gain per day
2. Estimate battery consumption (use the Wh/km figures above)
3. Identify charging points along the route (campgrounds, towns, cafes)
4. Plan daily distances around charging availability — aim to arrive at a charging point with 20 % battery remaining
5. Have a bail-out plan — if you run out of battery, you are pedalling a 25+ kg bike uphill with gear

## Australian Bikepacking Routes

### Victoria
- **Great Victorian Rail Trail** (134 km, Tallarook to Mansfield): flat to gently rolling, sealed and gravel. Campgrounds and towns every 20–30 km. Ideal for e-bike bikepacking.
- **Murray to Mountains Rail Trail** (116 km, Wangaratta to Bright): follows river valleys, moderate climbs near Bright. Towns with charging every 20–40 km.

### New South Wales
- **Blue Mountains trails:** steep and challenging but spectacular. Short daily distances (30–40 km) due to terrain. Charge in Katoomba, Blackheath, or Lithgow.
- **South Coast (Kiama to Eden):** coastal roads and bike paths. Rolling terrain, towns every 30–50 km.

### Queensland
- **Brisbane Valley Rail Trail** (161 km): one of Australia's longest rail trails. Flat to gentle, mostly gravel. Towns every 20–30 km.

### South Australia
- **Mawson Trail** (900 km, Adelaide to Blinman): epic outback trail. Remote — charging is scarce. Only for experienced bikepackers with spare batteries and solar charging.

### Tasmania
- **West Coast Wilderness Railway trail and surrounds:** remote, rugged, and spectacularly beautiful. Limited infrastructure — plan carefully.

## Tips

1. **Test your full loaded setup on a day ride before the trip.** Check handling, range, comfort, and packing.
2. **Carry the charger in a waterproof bag.** It is the most critical accessory on the trip.
3. **Ride in Eco whenever possible.** Save Turbo for the steep climbs. Discipline on assist level extends your daily range by 50–100 %.
4. **Start early.** Mornings are cooler, winds are calmer, and you have more daylight for riding.
5. **Bring a lightweight bike lock.** You will leave the bike unattended at camp, in town stops, and at water refill points.
6. **Check tyre pressure daily.** Loaded bikes are harder on tyres, and a flat in the bush is worse than a flat in the city.

## Bottom Line

E-bike bikepacking is the most accessible way to do multi-day cycling trips in Australia. The motor handles the weight of camping gear that would make a regular bike trip miserable. The limitation is range — plan your daily distances around battery capacity and charging points, carry the charger religiously, and ride in Eco whenever the terrain allows. A 500+ Wh battery, a rear rack with panniers, and 3–4 days of food and water will take you through some of Australia's best cycling routes.

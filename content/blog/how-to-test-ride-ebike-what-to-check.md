A test ride is the most important step in buying an e-bike. Photos, specs, and reviews tell you what the bike is — a test ride tells you what it feels like. And on an e-bike, the feel of the motor, the balance of the weight, and the comfort of the riding position matter more than on a regular bike.

## Before the Ride

### Wear the Right Gear
- **Shoes you will ride in** (not sandals or heels — you need to feel the pedals)
- **Comfortable clothing** (you will be moving)
- **A helmet** (the shop should provide one, but bring your own if you prefer)

### Ask the Shop to Set Up the Bike
- **Seat height:** when seated, your leg should have a slight bend at the knee at the bottom of the pedal stroke. Too low = knee pain. Too high = rocking hips.
- **Handlebar height and reach:** you should not be stretching or hunching to reach the handlebars
- **Tyre pressure:** ask the shop to check. Under-inflated tyres change how the bike rides.

### Know What You Are Testing
Before you ride, confirm:
- The bike's motor type (mid-drive or hub)
- Battery capacity (Wh)
- Assist levels (how many, what they are called)
- Gear system (derailleur or internal hub)

## During the Ride: What to Check

### 1. Mounting and Dismounting
- **Can you step over or through the frame easily?** If the top tube is too high, you will struggle every time you stop.
- **Can you reach the ground with both feet when seated?** You should be able to touch the ground with the balls of both feet at minimum. Flat-footed is better for confidence.
- **Is mounting easy with the bike's weight?** E-bikes weigh 20–30 kg. An awkward mount on a heavy bike is a daily frustration.

### 2. Starting and Stopping
- **Start from a standstill on flat ground.** How does the motor engage? Is it smooth or jerky? Some motors surge at low speed — this is unsettling in traffic.
- **Start on a slight uphill.** Does the motor provide enough initial torque, or do you wobble and strain before the assist kicks in?
- **Brake to a full stop.** Are the brakes strong and progressive? Do they squeal? Can you stop confidently from 25 km/h?
- **Emergency stop.** Brake as hard as you can (safely). How quickly does the bike stop? Does it feel stable or does the rear wheel lift?

### 3. Motor Feel
This is the most important thing to assess on a test ride.

- **Pedal assist response:** when you start pedalling, how quickly does the motor engage? A good system responds within half a pedal revolution. A slow system takes a full revolution or more — frustrating in stop-start traffic.
- **Assist cut-off:** pedal up to 25 km/h. What happens when the motor cuts off? A good system fades out smoothly. A bad system cuts off abruptly, like someone pulling the plug — jarring at speed.
- **Natural feel:** does the motor feel like a tailwind amplifying your effort, or like a separate engine driving the bike? The best systems (Bosch, Shimano Steps, Yamaha) feel intuitive — more effort from you = more assist. Budget systems often feel binary — on or off.
- **Noise:** is the motor quiet, or does it whine, click, or hum? Mid-drive motors have some gear noise — this is normal. Loud grinding or high-pitched whine is not.

### 4. Comfort
- **Riding position:** are your wrists, shoulders, and back comfortable? An aggressive (leaned-forward) position is faster but uncomfortable for commuting. An upright position is comfortable but less aerodynamic.
- **Saddle:** does the seat feel comfortable? (Note: a 5-minute test ride will not reveal saddle problems that appear after 30 minutes. But if it is uncomfortable in 5 minutes, it will be worse in 30.)
- **Vibration:** ride over a rough patch of road. How much vibration reaches your hands and seat? Suspension forks and seatposts help, but tyre size and pressure matter more for everyday comfort.
- **Weight distribution:** does the bike feel balanced, or is it nose-heavy or tail-heavy? Balanced weight handling is critical for confidence in corners and at low speed.

### 5. Handling
- **Cornering:** ride through some turns at moderate speed. Does the bike feel stable and predictable? A heavy bike with a high centre of gravity (battery on the rack) may feel unstable in turns.
- **Low-speed manoeuvring:** ride slowly through a car park or footpath area. Can you weave around obstacles confidently? Low-speed stability matters for traffic, parking, and bike paths.
- **Steering response:** is the steering quick and light, or slow and heavy? Quick steering is responsive but twitchy. Slow steering is stable but less manoeuvrable. For commuting, a middle ground is ideal.

### 6. Gears
- **Shift through all gears.** Do they change smoothly? Any clunking, skipping, or hesitation?
- **Shift under load.** Pedal hard and shift. Does the system handle it, or does the chain crunch? (Mid-drive e-bikes amplify this — rough shifting under motor load is a common complaint on cheaper models.)
- **Is the gear range wide enough?** Shift to the lowest gear and pedal uphill. Is it easy enough? Shift to the highest gear on flat ground. Can you keep up with the motor at 25 km/h without spinning uselessly?

### 7. Brakes
- **Lever feel:** are the brake levers comfortable to reach? Can you pull them with one or two fingers? Hydraulic disc brakes should require minimal effort.
- **Modulation:** can you apply brakes gently for a gradual slowdown, or do they grab hard immediately? Good brakes are progressive — more pull = more stopping power.
- **Motor cut-off:** when you pull the brake lever, the motor should cut off immediately. Check this — if the motor keeps driving while you brake, something is wrong.

### 8. Accessories and Practicality
- **Lights:** are they integrated (powered by the main battery) or separate? Turn them on — are they bright enough?
- **Mudguards:** if fitted, do they cover the tyres adequately?
- **Rack:** is there a rear rack? Can it hold your bag or panniers? What is the weight rating?
- **Kickstand:** is it stable? Does the bike stay upright when parked with a load?

## Questions to Ask the Shop

1. **What is the warranty coverage?** (Frame, motor, battery — separately)
2. **Who handles warranty service?** (The shop, or do they send you to the manufacturer?)
3. **How long does battery replacement take, and what does it cost?**
4. **What maintenance schedule does this bike need?**
5. **Can I take it for a longer test ride?** (Some shops allow 24-hour or weekend loans — always worth asking)
6. **Do you service e-bikes you sell?** (Buying from a shop that services the bike is a significant advantage)

## Red Flags on a Test Ride

- Motor surges or jerks at low speed
- Motor does not cut off when you stop pedalling or pull the brakes
- Brakes feel weak or spongy at speed
- Chain skips or crunches when shifting
- Rattles, creaks, or loose components
- The bike feels unstable at low speed
- The shop discourages or refuses a test ride
- The battery gauge drops noticeably during a short ride (may indicate a faulty or degraded battery)

## Bottom Line

Never buy an e-bike without riding it. Specs and reviews narrow your shortlist — the test ride makes the decision. Focus on motor feel, comfort, braking, and practical details (can you mount it easily, can you carry your gear, does it fit in your storage space). If the shop will not let you test ride, buy somewhere else.

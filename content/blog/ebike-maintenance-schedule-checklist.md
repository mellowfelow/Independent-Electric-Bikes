An e-bike needs more maintenance than a regular bike — the motor, battery, and electrical components add complexity. But most maintenance is simple, and a consistent schedule prevents expensive failures.

## Weekly Checks (5 Minutes)

### Tyre Pressure
Check and inflate both tyres to the recommended PSI (printed on the tyre sidewall). Tyres lose 1–3 PSI per week naturally.

**Why it matters:** under-inflated tyres increase rolling resistance (reducing range by 10–20 %), increase puncture risk, and wear faster.

### Brake Test
Squeeze each brake lever separately while walking the bike forward. Each brake should stop the bike firmly within the lever travel — the lever should not touch the handlebar grip.

### Lights and Reflectors
Check that all lights work (front, rear, any integrated lights). Check that reflectors are present and clean.

### Quick Visual Check
Look at the bike for anything obviously wrong: loose bolts, damaged cables, worn tyres, anything rubbing against a tyre or wheel.

## Monthly Checks (15 Minutes)

### Chain Condition
**Clean:** wipe the chain with a rag, apply chain lube to each roller while pedalling backward, wipe off excess.

**Check wear:** use a chain wear checker tool ($10–$15). E-bike chains wear faster — expect replacement every 2,000–3,000 km (mid-drive) or 3,000–5,000 km (hub motor).

### Brake Pads
Check brake pad thickness through the caliper. Replace when the pad material is thinner than 1 mm.

### Bolt Check
Check these bolts are tight:
- Stem bolts: 5–6 Nm
- Handlebar bolts: 4–5 Nm
- Seat clamp: 5–6 Nm
- Axle nuts/thru-axle: 12–15 Nm (front), 35–40 Nm (rear)
- Crank arm bolts: 35–40 Nm (mid-drive)
- Disc brake rotor bolts: 6–8 Nm

### Electrical Connectors
Check all visible connectors are fully plugged in, not corroded, not frayed, and rubber seals are intact.

### Battery Contacts
Wipe the battery contacts with a clean, dry cloth.

## Every 3 Months (30 Minutes)

### Deep Chain Clean
Remove the chain and soak in degreaser, or use a chain cleaning device.

### Brake Rotor Clean
Wipe disc brake rotors with isopropyl alcohol and a clean cloth. **Never use** WD-40 or oil-based cleaners on rotors or pads.

### Spoke Tension
Squeeze spokes in pairs. If any are noticeably loose or tight, the wheel needs truing (bike shop job).

### Derailleur Adjustment
Shift through all gears — every shift should be smooth. Check cable tension and derailleur hanger alignment.

### Suspension (If Fitted)
Wipe fork stanchions, check for oil leaks, bounce the fork for smooth operation.

## Every 6 Months / Annual Service

### Professional Service
Book an annual service at a qualified e-bike mechanic:
- Full drivetrain inspection
- Brake inspection and adjustment (bleed hydraulic brakes if needed)
- Wheel truing and spoke tensioning
- Bearing check (headset, bottom bracket, wheel hubs)
- Motor inspection
- Battery health check
- Electrical system check
- Firmware update (if available)
- Safety check (frame, fork, bolts)

**Cost:** $100–$250 for a standard service. Budget $200–$400 if major work is needed.

### Battery Maintenance

**Storage (if not riding for 2+ weeks):**
- Charge to 40–60 % before storage
- Store indoors at room temperature (15–25 °C)
- Never store fully charged or fully discharged
- Check charge level monthly

**Charging habits:**
- Let the battery cool before charging after a hot ride
- Do not leave on the charger indefinitely after 100 %
- Charge when the battery reaches 10–20 %

## Tools You Need at Home

**Basic toolkit ($50–$100):**
- Allen key set (2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 mm)
- Tyre levers (set of 3)
- Floor pump with pressure gauge
- Chain lube and degreaser
- Clean rags
- Spare inner tube and patch kit
- Multi-tool

**Worth adding ($50–$150):**
- Chain wear checker ($10)
- Torque wrench ($40–$100)
- Isopropyl alcohol ($10)
- Chain quick link pliers ($15)

## Bottom Line

E-bike maintenance is not complicated — it just needs to be consistent. A 5-minute weekly check (tyres, brakes, lights) and a 15-minute monthly check (chain, pads, bolts, connectors) prevent 90 % of failures. Book an annual professional service for bearings, motor internals, and hydraulic brakes. Budget $200–$500 per year for maintenance and consumables.

An e-bike is a $2,000–$6,000 machine with electrical components, sealed bearings, and a battery. Cleaning it wrong can damage the motor, corrode electrical connectors, force water into bearings, and void your warranty. Cleaning it right takes 15 minutes and keeps the bike running smoothly for years.

## What NOT to Do

### Never Use a High-Pressure Washer
This is the single most important rule. High-pressure water (car wash gun, Kärcher, pressure washer) forces water into:
- **Motor seals** — even IP65-rated motors are not designed for direct high-pressure jets
- **Bearing seals** — headset, bottom bracket, wheel hubs, pedal bearings, pivot bearings on suspension bikes
- **Electrical connectors** — the junction between motor cable and controller, display connector, brake sensor connectors, battery contacts
- **Seat post** — water inside the seat tube causes internal corrosion and a seized seatpost

**A garden hose on low pressure is fine.** A bucket and sponge is even better. A pressure washer is never fine.

### Never Submerge the Battery
The battery is the most expensive component on the bike. Do not submerge it, spray water directly at the battery contacts, or leave the bike standing in a puddle that covers the battery mount.

### Never Spray Water Directly at the Motor
Spray around the motor, not into it. The motor housing has vents and cable entries that are water-resistant, not waterproof. A sustained jet of water will eventually get past the seals.

### Never Spray Water Directly at the Display
The display is the most vulnerable electrical component. Most displays are rated IP54 or IP65 — they handle rain, but not a direct hose spray from 30 cm away.

## Tools You Need

- Bucket of warm water
- Bike-specific wash (Muc-Off, Finish Line, or any bicycle cleaner — not dish soap, which strips lubricant and leaves residue)
- Soft sponge or wash mitt
- Soft-bristle brush (old toothbrush or dedicated bike brush)
- Chain degreaser (bike-specific)
- Chain lubricant
- Clean rags or microfibre towels
- Garden hose with a gentle spray nozzle (optional — a bucket works fine)

## Step-by-Step Cleaning

### Step 1: Turn Off the Bike
Switch the bike off. If you are nervous about water near electronics, remove the battery (most batteries slide out of the frame with a key). This is not strictly necessary for a gentle wash, but it gives peace of mind.

### Step 2: Rinse Off Loose Dirt
Use a **gentle** hose spray or pour water from a bucket to rinse off mud, dust, and surface grime. Start from the top and work down. Do not aim the hose at the motor, battery, display, or any electrical connectors.

### Step 3: Apply Bike Wash
Spray or sponge bike wash onto the frame, fork, wheels, and cranks. Let it soak for 1–2 minutes to break down grease and road grime.

### Step 4: Scrub the Frame
Use a soft sponge or wash mitt to scrub the frame, fork, handlebars, stem, seatpost, and saddle. Work top to bottom — clean parts first, dirty parts (wheels, drivetrain) last.

**Pay attention to:**
- Under the downtube (road spray accumulates here)
- Behind the seat tube (mud and grit build up)
- Inside the fork legs (if the bike has suspension)
- Cable routing areas (grit gets trapped where cables enter the frame)

### Step 5: Clean the Wheels
Scrub the rims, spokes, and tyres with the sponge. Use the soft-bristle brush to get between the spokes and around the hub. Check for embedded glass, thorns, or stones in the tyre tread — remove them now.

### Step 6: Clean the Drivetrain
The chain, cassette, chainring, and derailleur are the dirtiest parts of the bike.

1. **Apply chain degreaser** to the chain while turning the cranks backward
2. **Scrub the chain** with a brush or chain cleaning device
3. **Scrub the cassette** with a brush (between the cogs)
4. **Scrub the chainring teeth** with a brush
5. **Scrub the derailleur jockey wheels** (the small pulleys — they accumulate thick grease and grit)
6. **Rinse** the drivetrain with gentle water

### Step 7: Clean the Brakes
Wipe the brake calipers with a damp rag. **Do not spray degreaser or bike wash on the brake rotors or pads** — contamination causes squealing and reduced braking performance.

If the rotors are contaminated (oily, squealing):
- Clean them with isopropyl alcohol (rubbing alcohol) on a clean rag
- If the pads are contaminated, they may need to be replaced (disc brake pads absorb oil and cannot be fully degreased)

### Step 8: Rinse
Rinse the entire bike with gentle water. Top to bottom, avoiding direct spray on electronics.

### Step 9: Dry
**Dry the bike immediately.** Do not leave it wet — water sitting in crevices causes corrosion.

1. Wipe the frame with a clean microfibre towel
2. Wipe the chain dry with a clean rag
3. Bounce the bike on its tyres a few times to shake water out of internal frame tubes and headset
4. If you have compressed air, blow water out of cable housings, headset, and pivot bearings
5. Let the bike air-dry for 10–15 minutes before lubricating

### Step 10: Lubricate
Once the chain is completely dry:
1. Apply one drop of chain lube to each roller while turning the cranks backward
2. Turn the cranks for 30 seconds to work the lube into the links
3. Wipe excess lube off the chain exterior with a clean rag
4. Lube the derailleur pivot points with a drop of light oil
5. Lube the brake and gear cable entry points with a drop of light oil

### Step 11: Reinstall the Battery
If you removed the battery, dry the battery contacts with a rag, dry the frame contacts, and slide the battery back in.

## How Often to Clean

| Riding conditions | Cleaning frequency |
|---|---|
| Dry commuting (sealed roads, no rain) | Every 2–4 weeks |
| Wet commuting (rain, puddles) | After every wet ride |
| Trail/off-road riding (mud, dust) | After every ride |
| Stored and rarely ridden | Monthly wipe-down |

**The chain** needs attention more often than the rest of the bike. Even if you do not do a full wash, wipe and re-lube the chain every 1–2 weeks in normal conditions.

## Protecting the Finish

After cleaning:
- **Frame polish or spray wax** (bike-specific, like Muc-Off Bike Protect or GT85) creates a protective layer that repels water and dirt, and makes the next wash easier
- **Touch-up paint** on any chips or scratches where bare metal is exposed — prevents rust (steel frames) or corrosion (alloy frames)

## Bottom Line

Clean your e-bike gently and regularly — warm water, a soft sponge, bike-specific wash, no high pressure. Dry it immediately, lubricate the chain, and avoid spraying water at the motor, battery, display, or connectors. A 15-minute wash every 2–4 weeks prevents the corrosion, grit damage, and drivetrain wear that leads to expensive repairs.

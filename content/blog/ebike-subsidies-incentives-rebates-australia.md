Several Australian states and local governments offer financial incentives for e-bike purchases. The landscape changes frequently — here is what is available in 2026 and how to check for updates.

## Federal Government

As of 2026, there is **no federal e-bike subsidy or rebate** in Australia. Unlike electric cars (which benefit from the Federal Electric Vehicle Discount and fringe benefits tax exemptions), e-bikes receive no federal financial incentive.

**Advocacy:** Bicycle Network and other cycling advocacy groups have called for a federal e-bike purchase rebate of $300–$500, similar to programs in countries like France, Germany, and several US states. No legislation has been introduced.

## State and Territory Programs

### Victoria

**E-Bike Purchase Subsidy:**
Victoria has periodically offered e-bike subsidies through various programs. Check the current status at the Victorian Government's energy or transport website, as these programs open and close based on funding.

**Salary Sacrifice:**
Some Victorian employers offer salary sacrifice arrangements for e-bikes. This allows you to purchase an e-bike from pre-tax income, effectively reducing the cost by your marginal tax rate (32.5–37 % for most workers). Ask your employer's HR department.

### New South Wales

**No state-wide e-bike subsidy** as of 2026. NSW has focused EV incentives on cars (stamp duty exemptions, registration discounts).

**Local council programs:** some NSW councils offer small grants or rebates for e-bikes as part of cycling promotion. Check your local council's website.

### Queensland

**No state-wide e-bike subsidy** as of 2026. Queensland's cycling investment has focused on infrastructure (bike paths) rather than purchase subsidies.

**TMR Active Transport Funding:** the Queensland Department of Transport and Main Roads provides grants to local councils for cycling infrastructure, which may include e-bike share programs.

### South Australia

**E-Bike Incentives:**
South Australia has been one of the more progressive states for e-bike policy. Check the SA Government's transport or environment website for current programs.

### Western Australia

**No state-wide e-bike subsidy** as of 2026. WA's cycling focus has been on infrastructure (PSP — Principal Shared Paths network).

### Tasmania, ACT, Northern Territory

No dedicated e-bike subsidy programs as of 2026. The ACT's general cycling promotion may include periodic incentive programs — check the ACT Government's transport website.

## Employer Programs

### Salary Sacrifice

The most accessible e-bike financial benefit for Australian workers. Salary sacrifice allows you to purchase an e-bike using pre-tax income.

**How it works:**
1. Your employer agrees to a salary sacrifice arrangement for the e-bike
2. You "sacrifice" a portion of your pre-tax salary equal to the bike's cost (typically spread over 12–24 months)
3. You pay income tax only on your remaining salary — effectively buying the bike at a discount equal to your marginal tax rate

**Example:**
- E-bike cost: $3,000
- Your marginal tax rate: 32.5 %
- Tax saving: $3,000 × 32.5 % = $975
- Effective cost: $2,025

**Important:** not all employers offer this. It requires employer agreement and may have FBT (Fringe Benefits Tax) implications. Some employers only offer salary sacrifice for items on an approved list — ask whether e-bikes qualify.

### Corporate Wellness Programs

Some employers subsidise e-bike purchases as part of workplace wellness programs:
- Partial purchase subsidies ($200–$500)
- Interest-free loans for e-bike purchases
- End-of-trip facilities (showers, lockers, secure bike parking) that make e-bike commuting practical

**Ask your employer's HR or wellness team** — even if there is no formal program, many companies will consider funding a pilot if you present the case (health benefits, reduced parking demand, lower carbon footprint).

## Tax Deductions

### Work-Related Use

If you use your e-bike for work purposes (not just commuting — actually riding for work), you may be able to claim a tax deduction.

**Eligible uses:**
- Travelling between work sites during the day
- Delivery work (couriers, food delivery)
- Client visits (tradespeople, health workers)

**Not eligible:**
- Regular commuting (home to work and back) — this is not deductible in Australia, regardless of the vehicle.

**How to claim:**
- Keep a logbook of work-related km
- Claim depreciation of the e-bike over its effective life (typically 3 years for a bicycle under ATO guidelines)
- Claim running costs (electricity, maintenance) proportional to work use
- Consult a tax accountant for the specific rules — claiming an incorrect deduction can result in penalties

### Home Office / Remote Worker

If you ride your e-bike to a co-working space, client meeting, or secondary work location (not your primary workplace), the travel may be deductible. Again, consult a tax professional.

## Council and Community Programs

### E-Bike Libraries

Some councils and community organisations run e-bike lending libraries — you borrow an e-bike for 1–4 weeks to try it before buying. This is not a financial incentive, but it removes the risk of buying an e-bike you might not like.

**Where to find them:** check your local council's website, library services, or cycling community group.

### Community Grants

Local councils, community foundations, and cycling advocacy groups occasionally offer small grants for e-bike purchases, particularly for:
- People with disabilities
- Low-income households
- Older adults
- People in areas with poor public transport

These programs are rare and usually small (20–50 bikes per round), but worth checking if you qualify.

## International Comparison

To understand what Australia is missing:

| Country/Region | E-Bike Incentive |
|---|---|
| France | Up to €400 national rebate + regional top-ups |
| Germany | Many states offer €200–€500 subsidies; company bike leasing is tax-exempt |
| Netherlands | Tax-free company e-bikes; no purchase subsidy (e-bikes are already mainstream) |
| USA (various states) | $200–$1,500 rebates in Colorado, California, Vermont, Connecticut, and others |
| UK | Cycle to Work scheme (salary sacrifice, up to £2,500) |
| Canada (BC) | $350–$1,400 rebate for low-to-moderate income residents |

Australia lags behind peer countries in e-bike incentives. The gap is widest at the federal level, where e-cars receive significant support but e-bikes receive none.

## Bottom Line

E-bike financial incentives in Australia are limited and fragmented — no federal rebate, and state programs vary and change. The most accessible option for most workers is salary sacrifice through their employer, which saves 30–37 % on the purchase price. Beyond that, check your local council for periodic programs, and consider tax deductions if you use the e-bike for work (not just commuting). The policy landscape is evolving — advocacy groups are pushing for federal support, and more state programs are likely in coming years.

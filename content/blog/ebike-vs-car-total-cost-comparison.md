The average Australian household spends $12,000–$18,000 per year running a car. An e-bike costs $500–$1,500 per year for the same local trips. Here is the full comparison — every cost, honestly calculated.

## Total Cost of Car Ownership (Annual)

Based on Australian averages for a mid-size sedan (2–5 years old, 15,000 km/year):

| Cost | Annual amount |
|---|---|
| Depreciation | $3,000–$6,000 |
| Fuel (petrol, $2.00/L, 8 L/100 km) | $2,400 |
| Registration | $800–$900 |
| CTP (compulsory third party) insurance | $400–$600 |
| Comprehensive insurance | $800–$2,000 |
| Servicing and maintenance | $500–$1,500 |
| Tyres (replaced every 40,000 km) | $200–$400 |
| Parking (if applicable) | $0–$3,000 |
| Tolls (if applicable) | $0–$2,000 |
| **Total** | **$8,100–$18,400** |

**Per kilometre cost: $0.54–$1.23/km**

Note: these figures exclude the purchase price financed — if you are paying off a car loan, add $3,000–$8,000/year in repayments.

## Total Cost of E-Bike Ownership (Annual)

Based on a $2,500 e-bike, 5,000 km/year (daily commuting + errands):

| Cost | Annual amount |
|---|---|
| Depreciation (bike value over 5 years) | $350–$500 |
| Electricity (0.5 kWh per charge, $0.30/kWh, 200 charges/year) | $30 |
| Chain, cassette, brake pads, tyres | $200–$400 |
| Annual service (brake bleed, bearing check, adjustment) | $100–$200 |
| Battery replacement (amortised: $800 replacement every 4 years) | $200 |
| Insurance (optional, theft + damage) | $150–$300 |
| Accessories (lights, lock, rack, rain gear — amortised) | $50–$100 |
| **Total** | **$1,080–$1,730** |

**Per kilometre cost: $0.22–$0.35/km**

## What the Numbers Do Not Show

### Time
**Car commute (10 km, urban):** 20–40 minutes (traffic, parking, walking from the car park).
**E-bike commute (10 km, urban):** 25–35 minutes (consistent — no traffic jams, door-to-door).

For commutes under 15 km in Australian cities, e-bikes are often the same speed or faster than cars. You never sit in traffic, you never circle for parking, and you never wait for a train.

### Health
An e-bike commuter gets 30–60 minutes of moderate exercise daily without setting foot in a gym. The health benefits of regular moderate exercise include:
- Reduced risk of cardiovascular disease (30–50 % reduction)
- Lower blood pressure
- Better mental health (reduced anxiety and depression)
- Better sleep quality
- Weight management

**The monetary value of these health benefits is estimated at $2,000–$5,000/year** (reduced healthcare costs, fewer sick days, increased productivity). Adding this to the direct cost saving makes the e-bike even more compelling.

### Parking
An e-bike takes 0.5 square metres of space. A car takes 12+ square metres. At home, an e-bike fits in a hallway, garage corner, or on a wall hook. At work, it fits in a bike rack. You never pay for parking, never circle the block, and never get a parking fine.

### Environmental Impact
A car emits approximately 2.4 tonnes of CO₂ per year (at 15,000 km). An e-bike emits approximately 20–30 kg of CO₂ per year (electricity generation + manufacturing amortised). Replacing a car with an e-bike reduces your transport emissions by over 98 %.

## When a Car Wins

An e-bike does not replace a car for every trip. Be honest about the limitations:

### Distance
E-bikes are practical for trips up to 30–40 km (one way). Beyond that, you need a large battery, and the time investment increases significantly. For trips over 40 km, a car is faster and more practical.

### Passengers
You can carry one child on a regular e-bike (child seat) or 1–4 children on a cargo e-bike. You cannot carry adult passengers. A car carries 4–5 people.

### Cargo
A cargo e-bike carries 50–200 kg. A car carries 400+ kg. For large shopping trips, furniture, or bulky items, a car is necessary.

### Weather
You can ride an e-bike in rain (with appropriate gear), but in extreme weather — storms, extreme heat, hail — a car provides shelter and safety.

### Safety
Cars provide crash protection (airbags, crumple zones, seatbelts). On an e-bike, you are exposed. Australian cycling fatality rates are low (approximately 1 death per 30 million km ridden), but the risk is not zero. Ride defensively, wear a helmet, use lights, and choose safer routes (bike paths over busy roads).

## The Optimal Setup

For most Australian households, the cost-optimal transport solution is:

**One car + one or two e-bikes.**

- E-bikes handle daily commuting, school runs, errands, and social trips (80 % of all trips)
- The car handles long distances, bad weather, large cargo, and family trips (20 % of trips)
- Selling the second car funds the e-bikes and then some

Replacing the second car with two e-bikes (one per adult) saves $7,000–$15,000 per year.

## Financing the Switch

An e-bike costs $1,500–$5,000 upfront. If that is a barrier:

### Salary Sacrifice / Novated Lease
Some Australian employers offer salary sacrifice arrangements for e-bikes (similar to car novated leases). You pay for the e-bike from pre-tax income, reducing the effective cost by your marginal tax rate (30–45 %).

### Buy Now, Pay Later
Services like Afterpay and Zip allow you to split the cost into interest-free instalments. Be careful with these — only use them if you can comfortably afford the repayments.

### Sell the Second Car First
The sale price of the second car ($5,000–$20,000) more than covers the cost of two e-bikes. You start saving from day one.

### Government Subsidies
Some Australian state and local governments offer e-bike purchase subsidies or rebates. Check your state government's transport website. These programs change frequently — what is available today may not be available next year.

## Bottom Line

An e-bike costs $1,080–$1,730 per year to run. A car costs $8,100–$18,400 per year. Replacing a car with an e-bike saves $6,370–$16,670 per year — plus health benefits worth $2,000–$5,000/year. For most Australian households, the optimal setup is one car plus one or two e-bikes: the e-bikes handle 80 % of daily trips at a fraction of the cost, and the car covers the rest. The numbers are not close. The e-bike wins.

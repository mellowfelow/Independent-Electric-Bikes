E-bike road rules vary between Australian states. What is legal in Queensland may be illegal in Victoria. Here is a state-by-state breakdown of the rules that matter.

## National Rules (All States)

These apply everywhere in Australia:

- **Motor power:** maximum 250 W continuous rated output (some states allow higher peak power if the continuous rating is 250 W)
- **Speed limit for assist:** motor must cut off at 25 km/h
- **Pedal-assist required:** the rider must pedal for the motor to provide assist (with state-specific throttle exceptions — see below)
- **Helmet mandatory:** AS/NZS 2063 approved helmet, all ages, all locations
- **Lights at night:** white front light and red rear light, both visible from 200 metres; red rear reflector visible from 50 metres
- **No alcohol:** drink-riding laws apply to cyclists (and e-bike riders) in every state

## Victoria

### Where You Can Ride
- Roads (in the left lane or bike lane)
- Bike lanes and bike paths
- Shared paths (with pedestrians)
- **Footpaths: NO** (except children under 12, or adults accompanying a child under 12)

### Throttle
- **Low-powered throttle permitted** on e-bikes up to 200 W if throttle-only speed is limited to 6 km/h (walking speed). Above 6 km/h, the rider must pedal.
- In practice, most e-bikes sold in Australia with a throttle comply with this — the throttle provides a "walk assist" mode up to 6 km/h.

### Speed Limits
- No specific speed limit for cyclists on roads (you follow the road speed limit, which you are unlikely to exceed on an e-bike)
- On shared paths, no posted speed limit, but you must ride at a speed that is safe for the conditions and other path users

### Fines
- No helmet: $207
- No lights at night: $207
- Riding on the footpath (adults): $207
- Drink-riding: $207 (first offence) to $414

## New South Wales

### Where You Can Ride
- Roads
- Bike lanes and bike paths
- Shared paths
- **Footpaths: NO** (except children under 16, or adults accompanying a child under 16 — note: different age threshold from Victoria)

### Throttle
- Throttle-only operation permitted up to 6 km/h (walk assist)
- Above 6 km/h, pedal-assist only

### E-Bike Classification
NSW classifies e-bikes into two categories:
1. **Pedalec:** 250 W, pedal-assist only, 25 km/h cut-off — treated as a bicycle
2. **Power-assisted pedal cycle:** up to 200 W, may have throttle, 25 km/h cut-off — treated as a bicycle

Both are legal without registration or licence.

### Fines
- No helmet: $344
- No lights at night: $344
- Riding on the footpath: $344

## Queensland

### Where You Can Ride
- Roads
- Bike lanes and bike paths
- Shared paths
- **Footpaths: YES** (all ages — Queensland is the most permissive state for footpath riding)

### Throttle
- Throttle-only operation permitted on e-bikes up to 200 W at speeds up to 10 km/h
- Pedal-assist e-bikes up to 250 W with a 25 km/h cut-off are treated as bicycles

### Unique Rules
- Queensland allows e-bikes on footpaths at any age — but riders must give way to pedestrians and ride at a safe speed
- Queensland has a 1-metre passing law: drivers must leave at least 1 metre when passing a cyclist at 60 km/h or below, and 1.5 metres above 60 km/h

### Fines
- No helmet: $143
- No lights at night: $143

## South Australia

### Where You Can Ride
- Roads
- Bike lanes and bike paths
- Shared paths
- **Footpaths: NO** (except children under 12, or adults accompanying a child under 12 — same as Victoria)

### Throttle
- Throttle-only operation up to 6 km/h (walk assist)
- Pedal-assist up to 250 W, 25 km/h cut-off

### Unique Rules
- South Australia has a mandatory 1-metre passing distance for drivers (same as Queensland, at all speeds)

### Fines
- No helmet: $136
- No lights: $136

## Western Australia

### Where You Can Ride
- Roads
- Bike lanes and bike paths
- Shared paths
- **Footpaths: NO** (except children under 12, or adults accompanying a child under 12)

### Throttle
- WA is more permissive on throttles: e-bikes with a throttle up to 200 W are permitted
- The 250 W pedal-assist, 25 km/h cut-off standard applies as elsewhere

### Unique Rules
- WA has specific rules about cycling two abreast — permitted, but riders must be within 1.5 metres of each other

### Fines
- No helmet: $50 (the lowest in Australia)

## Tasmania

### Where You Can Ride
- Roads
- Bike lanes and bike paths
- Shared paths
- **Footpaths: YES** (all ages, same as Queensland — but riders must give way to pedestrians)

### Throttle
- Low-speed throttle permitted (walk assist to 6 km/h)
- Pedal-assist up to 250 W, 25 km/h cut-off

### Fines
- No helmet: $163

## ACT (Canberra)

### Where You Can Ride
- Roads
- Bike lanes and bike paths
- Shared paths
- **Footpaths: YES** (all ages — Canberra is very cycling-friendly)

### Throttle
- Walk-assist throttle permitted up to 6 km/h
- Pedal-assist up to 250 W, 25 km/h cut-off

### Fines
- No helmet: $193

## Northern Territory

### Where You Can Ride
- Roads
- Bike lanes and bike paths
- Shared paths
- **Footpaths: YES** (all ages)

### Throttle
- The NT is the most relaxed on throttles — throttle-only e-bikes up to 200 W are broadly permitted
- Standard pedal-assist rules apply as elsewhere

### Unique Rules
- The NT has less cycling-specific infrastructure than other states, and some urban areas have limited bike lanes
- Heat is a significant factor — riding in the wet season (November–April) means extreme heat and tropical storms

### Fines
- No helmet: $62 (the second-lowest in Australia)

## Bottom Line

The core rules are the same everywhere: 250 W motor, 25 km/h assist cut-off, mandatory helmet, lights at night. The main differences are footpath access (allowed in QLD, TAS, ACT, NT — banned for adults in VIC, NSW, SA, WA) and throttle rules (slightly more permissive in QLD, WA, and NT). Always check your state's current rules — fines and regulations can change, and local councils may have additional restrictions on shared paths.

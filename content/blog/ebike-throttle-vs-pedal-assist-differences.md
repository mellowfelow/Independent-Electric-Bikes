E-bikes deliver motor power in two ways: pedal assist (you pedal, the motor helps) and throttle (you push a lever or twist a grip, the motor drives the bike without pedalling). Most e-bikes have one or both. Here is how they work, when each is useful, and the legal implications in Australia.

## How Pedal Assist Works

A pedal assist system (PAS) detects that you are pedalling and adds motor power proportionally. You must pedal for the motor to work.

### Cadence Sensor
A ring of magnets on the crank detects rotation. When it sees rotation, the controller delivers power at the selected assist level.

**Characteristics:**
- Binary: motor is on (you are pedalling) or off (you are not)
- Power delivery can feel abrupt (full power as soon as you start pedalling, full stop when you stop)
- Less natural feel than torque sensing
- Common on budget e-bikes ($1,000–$2,500)

### Torque Sensor
A strain gauge in the bottom bracket or crank measures how hard you are pushing the pedals. The motor matches your effort — push harder, get more motor power.

**Characteristics:**
- Proportional: the motor scales with your effort
- Natural feel (feels like your legs became stronger, not like a motor is dragging you)
- Better efficiency (motor works only as hard as you need)
- Better range (motor does not waste power when you are pedalling lightly)
- Common on mid-range and premium e-bikes ($2,500+)

### Assist Levels
Most e-bikes have 3–5 assist levels:
- **Eco / Level 1:** minimal motor assist (30–50 % of your effort). Maximum range.
- **Tour / Level 2:** moderate assist (60–100 %). Good balance of range and help.
- **Sport / Level 3:** strong assist (100–200 %). For hills and headwinds.
- **Turbo / Level 4–5:** maximum assist (200–300 %). For steep hills and maximum speed. Drains the battery fast.

## How Throttle Works

A throttle delivers motor power without pedalling. You push a thumb lever, twist a grip, or press a button — the motor drives the bike.

### Throttle Types
- **Thumb throttle:** a small lever near the left grip. Push with your thumb for power. The most common type on e-bikes.
- **Twist throttle:** like a motorcycle — twist the grip to add power. Less common on pedal-assist e-bikes, more common on scooter-style e-bikes.
- **Button throttle:** a push button that delivers a fixed power level. Simple but less adjustable.

### When Throttle Is Useful

1. **Starting from a stop.** Getting a heavy e-bike moving from zero — especially on a hill or in traffic — is the hardest moment. A burst of throttle gets you rolling, then pedal assist takes over.
2. **Climbing a steep section without pedalling.** If a hill is too steep to pedal comfortably (or you need to stand and walk), the throttle can push you through.
3. **Injury or fatigue.** If your knee hurts or your legs are exhausted, the throttle gets you home without pedalling.
4. **Manoeuvring at low speed.** Walking the bike up a ramp, through a gate, or alongside traffic — a gentle throttle avoids the awkward dismount-and-push.

## Legal Status in Australia

### Pedal Assist
A pedal-assist e-bike with a maximum continuous motor power of 250 W and an assist cut-off at 25 km/h is classified as a bicycle in all Australian states and territories. No licence, no registration, no insurance required.

### Throttle
The legal status of throttle-equipped e-bikes is more complex:

**Under the current Australian Design Rule (ADR) and state road rules:**
- A pedal-assist e-bike (EN 15194) with an auxiliary throttle function is generally treated as a bicycle, provided the motor is 250 W continuous and cuts off at 25 km/h, and the throttle is limited to 6 km/h (walk assist) or requires pedalling to activate.
- A throttle-only e-bike (no pedal assist, motor activated solely by throttle) above 200 W is classified as a motor vehicle in most states — requiring registration, insurance, and a licence.

**In practice:**
- Most e-bikes sold in Australia with a throttle are pedal-assist bikes with an added thumb throttle. As long as the motor is 250 W and cuts off at 25 km/h, they are treated as bicycles.
- High-power throttle bikes (500 W+, 750 W+) imported from the US or China are not legal for road use without registration.
- Enforcement varies by state and by individual encounter with police. The safest position: buy a 250 W pedal-assist bike with a throttle that operates within the assist speed limit.

## Pedal Assist vs Throttle: Comparison

| Feature | Pedal assist | Throttle |
|---|---|---|
| Exercise | Yes (you pedal) | No (motor does the work) |
| Range | Longer (motor supplements your effort) | Shorter (motor does all the work) |
| Feel | More like cycling | More like a moped or scooter |
| Hill climbing | Good (motor + your legs) | Adequate (motor only — less total force) |
| Starting from stop | Can be awkward (need to pedal first) | Smooth (instant power) |
| Legal status (AU) | Bicycle (250 W, 25 km/h) | Complex (see above) |
| Common on | Most e-bikes | Some e-bikes, plus scooter-style models |
| Best for | Commuting, fitness, long rides | Starting, manoeuvring, mobility-limited riders |

## Which Should You Choose?

### Pedal Assist Only
Choose this if:
- You want exercise (you will always pedal)
- Maximum range matters (pedal assist is more efficient)
- You want simplicity (fewer controls)
- You are concerned about legal compliance (pedal assist is unambiguously legal in Australia)

### Pedal Assist + Throttle
Choose this if:
- You want the option to not pedal (injury, fatigue, steep start)
- You commute in stop-start traffic (throttle smooths the starts)
- You have mobility limitations (the throttle is a backup)
- You occasionally need to move the bike without pedalling (walking alongside it up a ramp)

### Throttle Only
Choose this if:
- You cannot pedal (disability, injury)
- You want a moped-like experience

**Note:** throttle-only e-bikes above 200 W are not legal as bicycles in Australia. They require registration and a licence. If you want throttle-only, stay under 200 W or register the vehicle.

## Can You Add a Throttle Later?

Yes — aftermarket thumb throttles are available for $15–$50 and plug into most e-bike controllers. However:
- Check that your controller supports a throttle input (not all do)
- Ensure the throttle respects the 25 km/h speed limit
- Adding a throttle to a bike sold as "pedal assist only" may void the warranty
- The legal status depends on the throttle's behaviour (does it require pedalling? does it respect the speed limit?)

## Bottom Line

Pedal assist is the standard e-bike power delivery — you pedal, the motor helps, and you get exercise. A throttle is a useful addition for starting from stops, climbing steep sections, and riding when you cannot pedal. Most Australian commuters should choose a pedal-assist bike with an optional thumb throttle — it gives you the best of both: exercise when you want it, and effortless power when you need it. Ensure the total system stays within 250 W continuous and 25 km/h assist cut-off for legal road use.

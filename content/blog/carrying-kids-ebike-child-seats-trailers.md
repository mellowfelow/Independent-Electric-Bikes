Carrying children on an e-bike is safe, practical, and increasingly common — but only with the right equipment, the right bike, and the right riding behaviour.

## Legal Requirements in Australia

Australian road rules treat e-bikes (250 W, 25 km/h pedal assist) as bicycles. The same rules that apply to carrying children on regular bicycles apply to e-bikes:

- **Helmets:** mandatory for all riders and passengers at all ages in every state and territory.
- **Child seats:** must comply with Australian Standards (AS/NZS).
- **Trailers:** must have a visible safety flag and working lights if riding at night.
- **Age limits:** no minimum age for a bicycle passenger in most states. Most child seats are rated for 9 months to 5 years (rear) or 9 months to 3 years (front).

## Options for Carrying Children

### Front-Mounted Child Seat

Mounted on the handlebars or the top tube, in front of the rider.

**Age range:** approximately 9 months to 3 years (or up to 15 kg).

**Advantages:**
- The child is between your arms — they feel secure, and you can see them
- The child has a great forward view
- Does not affect the rear wheel balance

**Disadvantages:**
- Adds weight to the front, affecting steering
- Limits handlebar movement
- Smaller weight capacity (15 kg max)

### Rear-Mounted Child Seat

Mounted on the rear rack or the seat tube, behind the rider.

**Age range:** approximately 9 months to 5–6 years (or up to 22 kg).

**Advantages:**
- Higher weight limit than front seats
- Does not affect steering or display visibility
- More room for the child

**Disadvantages:**
- You cannot see the child without turning around — use a handlebar mirror
- Raises the centre of gravity
- Requires a compatible rear rack (check weight limits)

### Bicycle Trailer

A two-wheeled trailer towed behind the bike, with an enclosed cabin for one or two children.

**Age range:** approximately 9–12 months to 6 years. Capacity: one or two children, up to 20–22 kg per child.

**Advantages:**
- The safest option — cabin has a roll cage, harness, and stays upright if the bike falls
- Enclosed from weather
- Carries gear alongside the child
- Doubles as a stroller
- Lower centre of gravity than a child seat

**Disadvantages:**
- Adds length — harder to park and manoeuvre
- Wider than the bike
- More expensive ($300–$1,500 vs $80–$250)
- Heavier (8–15 kg)

### Cargo E-Bike

A purpose-built e-bike with a cargo box or extended rear that carries children.

**Types:**
- **Long john (front box):** carries 1–4 children with benches and harnesses
- **Long tail (extended rear):** carries 1–2 children behind the rider

**Advantages:** purpose-built, visible to you, carries multiple children plus gear
**Disadvantages:** expensive ($4,000–$12,000), heavy (30–45 kg), large

## Safety Rules

### Non-Negotiable
1. **Helmet on every rider and passenger, every time.**
2. **Harness secured** — use it correctly, adjusted to fit snugly.
3. **Weight limits respected.**

### Riding Behaviour
1. **Reduce your speed** — ride 5–10 km/h slower than solo
2. **Avoid busy roads** — use bike paths and quiet streets
3. **Check the child regularly** — use a handlebar mirror
4. **Never leave the bike unattended with a child on it**

### E-Bike-Specific Safety
1. **Start in a low assist level** — sudden torque can be destabilising with a child
2. **Practise without the child first** — load the seat/trailer with equivalent weight
3. **Check tyre pressure** — add 5–10 PSI for the extra weight
4. **Allow more braking distance** — bike + rider + child + seat weighs 100–130 kg

## Choosing the Right Setup

| Children's age | Number | Best option |
|---|---|---|
| 9 months – 3 years | 1 | Front child seat (short rides) or rear child seat |
| 9 months – 5 years | 1 | Rear child seat or trailer |
| 9 months – 6 years | 2 | Trailer (dual) or cargo e-bike |
| 3–8 years | 1 | Rear child seat (up to 22 kg) or trailer |
| 6+ years | 1 | Tag-along bike or their own bike |

## Bottom Line

Carrying children on an e-bike is safe and practical with the right equipment — a rear child seat for children up to 22 kg, a trailer for maximum safety and weather protection, or a cargo e-bike for families replacing a car. The motor handles the extra weight that makes child-carrying impractical on a regular bike. Always: helmet, harness, reduced speed, quiet routes.

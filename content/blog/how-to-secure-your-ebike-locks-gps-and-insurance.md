E-bikes are high-value targets for theft. The average e-bike costs $2,000–$5,000, the battery alone is worth $500–$1,500, and a stolen e-bike is difficult to identify once the display and branding are removed. In Australia, e-bike theft has risen alongside adoption. Here is how to protect your investment with locks, GPS tracking, and insurance.

## Layer 1: Physical Locks

No single lock is unbeatable, but the right combination makes your bike a harder target than the one next to it. Use **two different lock types** — thieves carry tools for one lock style, rarely two.

### U-Locks (D-Locks)

The strongest portable option. A hardened steel shackle resists bolt cutters and leverage attacks. Lock through the frame and rear wheel to an immovable object.

**Recommended:**
- Kryptonite New York Standard — 16 mm hardened steel, double deadbolt, Sold Secure Gold
- Abus Granit X-Plus 540 — 13 mm parabolic shackle, Sold Secure Gold
- OnGuard Brute STD — 16.8 mm shackle, affordable alternative

**Limitations:** U-locks protect only what they loop through. The front wheel, seat, and accessories remain vulnerable.

### Chain Locks

Heavier but more versatile — a chain wraps around odd-shaped frames, cargo racks, and multiple attachment points.

**Recommended:**
- Kryptonite New York Chain 1210 — 12 mm six-sided links, 100 cm length
- Abus CityChain 1010 — 9 mm hardened steel, lighter option for commuters

### Cable Locks

Cables are a **deterrent only** — a pair of cable cutters defeats them in seconds. Use a cable as a secondary lock to secure the front wheel to the U-lock, never as your only lock.

### Folding Locks

A compromise between U-lock rigidity and cable flexibility. Lighter than chains, harder to cut than cables. The Abus Bordo Granit XPlus 6500 is the benchmark, but folding locks are generally weaker than a good U-lock.

## Lock Strategy

The **Sheldon Brown method** (modified for e-bikes):

1. **Primary U-lock:** through the rear wheel inside the rear triangle and around the fixed object. This secures both the frame and rear wheel with one lock.
2. **Secondary cable or chain:** loop through the front wheel and back to the U-lock or fixed object.
3. **Lock the battery:** most e-bike batteries have a key lock. Always remove the key and engage the battery lock when parked. If the battery is removable, take it with you for stops longer than a few minutes.

### Where to Lock

- **Solid, immovable objects:** bike hoops bolted to concrete, steel poles cemented into the ground, permanent railings
- **Avoid:** wooden posts (saw through in minutes), sign poles (lift the bike over the top), chain-link fences (cut the fence)
- **Position:** lock in well-lit, high-traffic areas. Thieves avoid witnesses.

## Layer 2: GPS Tracking

A lock deters theft. A GPS tracker helps you recover the bike if the lock fails.

### Hidden GPS Trackers

Trackers that fit inside the frame, seat tube, or handlebar are invisible to thieves.

**Options for Australian riders:**
- **Apple AirTag:** the cheapest option ($49). Not true GPS — uses the Apple Find My network (Bluetooth crowd-sourced location). Works well in urban areas with many iPhones nearby. Less reliable in rural areas. Battery lasts about a year. Fits inside the seat tube or behind a water bottle mount.
- **Samsung SmartTag2:** the Android equivalent. Uses the SmartThings Find network. Same crowd-sourced limitations.
- **Invoxia GPS Tracker:** true GPS with cellular connectivity. More accurate than AirTags in low-traffic areas. Subscription required (~$50/year). Battery lasts 1–3 weeks depending on tracking frequency.
- **Tile Pro:** Bluetooth-based, similar to AirTag. The Tile network is smaller than Apple's, so urban-only.

### Integrated GPS Systems

Some e-bike brands build GPS into the bike:
- **Bosch ConnectModule:** pairs with the eBike Flow app, shows real-time location, and sends theft alerts. Available on 2024+ Bosch-equipped bikes.
- **VanMoof (discontinued brand) style:** showed the potential of built-in tracking, but the company's closure highlights the risk of relying on a manufacturer's cloud service.

### Practical Tips

- **Register the tracker** before you need it. Set up the app, test the location accuracy, and enable theft alerts.
- **Hide the tracker** where a thief would not look: inside the seat tube (wrapped in foam to prevent rattling), behind the motor cover, or zip-tied inside the frame.
- **If your bike is stolen:** report it to police immediately with the GPS location. Do not confront a thief yourself. Provide police with the live tracking link.

## Layer 3: Insurance

Locks and GPS reduce your risk, but insurance transfers it. A stolen or crashed e-bike without insurance is a total loss.

### Types of E-Bike Insurance in Australia

**1. Home and Contents Insurance**
Your existing home insurance may cover your e-bike as a "specified item" or "portable contents." Check:
- Is there a per-item limit? Many policies cap individual items at $1,500–$2,500 — below the value of most e-bikes.
- Does the policy cover theft away from home (e.g., locked at a train station)?
- Is there an excess? A $500 excess on a $2,000 bike makes the claim barely worthwhile.

**2. Specialist Bicycle Insurance**
Purpose-built policies for cyclists. In Australia:
- **Bikesure:** covers theft, accidental damage, third-party liability, and accessories. Premiums from ~$150/year for a $2,000 bike.
- **Velosure:** similar coverage, includes personal accident. Available for e-bikes up to $15,000.
- **RACV / NRMA add-ons:** some motoring clubs offer bicycle cover as an add-on to existing membership.

**3. Third-Party Liability**
If you hit a pedestrian or damage property while riding, you could face a personal injury claim. Bicycle-specific policies often include $10–$20 million public liability. Your home insurance liability cover may also apply — check with your insurer.

### What Insurers Want to See

To keep premiums low and avoid claim disputes:
- **Proof of purchase:** keep your receipt and serial number
- **Proof of security:** insurers may require a minimum lock standard (e.g., Sold Secure Gold) and evidence you used it
- **Photos:** photograph your bike from multiple angles, including the serial number stamped on the bottom bracket
- **Registration:** register your bike's serial number at a national database (e.g., PropertyVault, BikeVault)

## The Cost of Doing Nothing

A quality U-lock costs $80–$120. A GPS tracker costs $49–$150. Specialist insurance costs $150–$300 per year. Total first-year cost: $280–$570.

Replacing a stolen e-bike costs $2,000–$5,000+, plus the time and stress of dealing with the loss.

The maths is straightforward: protect your bike.

E-bike battery fires are rare but catastrophic. A lithium-ion battery fire burns at 600°C+, produces toxic fumes, is nearly impossible to extinguish with a household fire extinguisher, and can reignite hours after appearing to be out. Here is what causes them, how to prevent them, and what to do if one starts.

## How Rare Are E-Bike Battery Fires?

The exact rate is unknown, but estimates suggest 1 in 10,000 to 1 in 100,000 batteries per year experience a fire or thermal event. The risk increases dramatically with:
- Cheap, unbranded batteries with no-name cells
- Damaged batteries (crash impact, drop, puncture)
- Incorrect charging (wrong charger, overcharging, charging in extreme temperatures)
- Aftermarket or DIY battery modifications

**The majority of e-bike battery fires involve cheap replacement batteries, aftermarket batteries, or modified batteries — not OEM batteries from reputable manufacturers.** A quality battery from a major brand (Samsung, LG, Panasonic cells, in a Bosch/Shimano/Giant/Trek battery housing) with a certified BMS, charged with the original charger, is extremely safe.

## What Causes a Battery Fire?

### Thermal Runaway
A lithium-ion cell fire begins with thermal runaway — a self-sustaining chemical reaction inside a cell that generates extreme heat.

**The chain reaction:**
1. A cell is damaged (internal short circuit from physical damage, manufacturing defect, or dendrite growth)
2. The short circuit generates heat inside the cell
3. Heat causes the electrolyte (a flammable organic solvent) to decompose, producing flammable gas
4. At approximately 130–150°C, the separator between the electrodes melts, creating a larger short circuit
5. At approximately 200°C, the cathode material decomposes, releasing oxygen
6. The oxygen feeds the fire — the cell now burns using its own internal oxygen supply
7. Heat from the burning cell triggers thermal runaway in adjacent cells (propagation)
8. The battery pack fires cell by cell until all cells have burned

**Why it is hard to extinguish:** the burning cell generates its own oxygen. Smothering the fire (removing external oxygen) does not stop the reaction. Water cools the cells and slows propagation but does not stop a cell that is already in thermal runaway.

### Common Triggers

**Physical damage:**
- Crash impact that dents or punctures the battery casing
- Dropping the battery onto a hard surface
- Riding over a severe pothole or kerb that impacts a frame-mounted battery
- Crushing (storing a heavy object on top of the battery)

**Electrical abuse:**
- Using the wrong charger (incorrect voltage — a 48 V charger on a 36 V battery overcharges every cell)
- Charging a deeply discharged battery (cells below safe voltage can develop internal short circuits)
- Short-circuiting the battery terminals (metal object across the contacts)

**Manufacturing defects:**
- Contamination during cell production (microscopic metal particles inside the cell)
- Poor spot-welding of cell connections (high-resistance joints that heat up)
- Inadequate BMS protection (fails to detect an out-of-spec cell)

**Environmental:**
- Charging below 0°C (lithium plating on the anode creates internal short circuits)
- Charging above 45°C (accelerates electrolyte decomposition)
- Storing a fully charged battery in direct sun or a hot car (heat accelerates degradation)

## Prevention

### Buy Quality
- **Buy from reputable brands** that use name-brand cells (Samsung, LG, Panasonic, CATL) and certified BMS systems
- **Avoid ultra-cheap batteries** from unknown manufacturers. If the price is too good to be true, the cells are too cheap to be safe.
- **When replacing a battery, buy OEM** (from the bike's manufacturer). Aftermarket batteries vary enormously in quality — some are excellent, some are dangerous.

### Charge Safely
- **Use only the charger supplied with the battery.** If you need a replacement charger, buy from the battery's manufacturer.
- **Do not charge unattended overnight.** Charge when you are home and awake. If you must charge overnight, use a timer plug set to the expected charge duration (3–5 hours).
- **Charge on a hard, non-flammable surface** (tile, concrete, metal tray). Not on carpet, wood, a bed, or a sofa.
- **Do not charge immediately after riding** (the battery is warm from discharge). Let it cool to room temperature first (30–60 minutes).
- **Do not charge below 0°C or above 40°C.**
- **Unplug when fully charged.** Most chargers stop charging at 100 %, but leaving the charger connected for days is unnecessary wear on the BMS.

### Store Safely
- **Store at 40–60 % charge** if not riding for more than a week. A fully charged battery stored long-term degrades faster and has more energy available in a fire.
- **Store in a cool, dry location** (10–25°C). Not in direct sun, not in a hot garage, not in a car.
- **Do not store near flammable materials** (fuel, paint, solvents, paper).
- **Remove the battery from the bike** if storing the bike for an extended period.
- **Inspect the battery casing** for cracks, dents, or swelling. A swollen battery is a battery in distress — do not charge it, do not use it, and dispose of it safely.

### Inspect After Impacts
If the bike crashes or the battery is dropped:
1. Inspect the casing for dents, cracks, or deformation
2. Check the battery contacts for damage
3. If the casing is damaged, **do not charge the battery**. Take it to a bike shop or battery specialist for inspection.
4. Even if the casing looks fine, monitor the battery during the next few charges for unusual heat, smell, or swelling.

## What to Do If a Battery Catches Fire

### Signs of Imminent Fire
- Hissing or popping sounds from the battery
- Smoke or gas venting from the battery casing
- Battery swelling (the casing bulges)
- Battery is extremely hot to touch (too hot to hold)
- Sweet chemical smell

### If Fire Starts

1. **Get everyone out.** A lithium-ion fire produces hydrogen fluoride gas — extremely toxic. Do not breathe the fumes. Evacuate immediately.
2. **Call 000 (Triple Zero).** Tell the operator it is a lithium-ion battery fire. Fire services have specific protocols for battery fires.
3. **If safe to do so, move the burning battery outside.** Use oven mitts, a metal shovel, or metal tongs. Place it on concrete, gravel, or dirt — away from buildings, cars, and flammable materials. Do not place it in water initially.
4. **Cool with water from a distance.** Water does not extinguish thermal runaway, but it cools adjacent cells and slows propagation. Use a garden hose from 2+ metres away. Expect steam and popping sounds.
5. **Do not use a standard fire extinguisher** (CO₂ or dry powder). These are ineffective against lithium-ion thermal runaway. A Class D extinguisher (metal fire) or copious water is more effective.
6. **Do not attempt to move a battery that is actively shooting flames or exploding cells.** Evacuate and let fire services handle it.

### After the Fire
- The battery can reignite hours after the fire appears to be out. Do not bring it back inside. Leave it outdoors on a non-flammable surface for at least 24 hours.
- Fire services may submerge the battery in a container of water for 24 hours to ensure all cells are fully discharged and cooled.
- Dispose of the fire-damaged battery through your local hazardous waste facility. Do not put it in household waste or recycling.

## Disposal of Old Batteries

Lithium-ion batteries must not be placed in household waste or recycling bins. They can cause fires in waste trucks and recycling facilities.

**Disposal options in Australia:**
- **B-cycle** (battery recycling scheme): free drop-off at participating retailers. Check b-cycle.com.au for locations.
- **Local council hazardous waste collection:** most councils run periodic collection events.
- **Bike shop:** many shops accept old e-bike batteries for recycling.
- **Battery recycler:** companies like Envirostream and Celeste Industries process lithium-ion batteries.

## Bottom Line

E-bike battery fires are rare when using quality batteries, OEM chargers, and sensible charging practices. The risk is highest with cheap aftermarket batteries, wrong chargers, damaged casings, and charging in extreme temperatures. Charge on a hard surface when you are home, do not charge overnight unattended, inspect the battery after any impact, and store at 40–60 % charge in a cool location. If a fire starts: evacuate, call 000, move the battery outside if safe, and cool with water from a distance. Quality batteries from reputable brands with certified BMS systems are extremely safe — the fires that make the news almost always involve cheap, unbranded, or modified batteries.

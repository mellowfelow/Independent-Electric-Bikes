E-bike touring is long-distance cycling without the suffering. The motor turns 80 km days from an athletic achievement into a normal afternoon — opening touring to riders who would never attempt it on a regular bike.

## What Is E-Bike Touring?

E-bike touring means multi-day cycling trips, typically 50–120 km per day, carrying your gear on the bike (panniers, bikepacking bags, or a trailer). You sleep in campgrounds, hostels, pubs, or wild camp — and ride to the next stop the following day.

**How the motor changes touring:**
- **Daily distance increases.** 80–100 km per day is comfortable on an e-bike in Eco mode. On a regular touring bike, 60–80 km is a big day for most riders.
- **Hills stop being the enemy.** A loaded touring bike climbing a 10 % gradient is brutal without a motor. With a mid-drive motor, it is just a higher assist level.
- **Headwinds become manageable.** Wind is the most demoralising factor in touring. The motor takes the edge off.
- **You arrive fresh.** Instead of collapsing at the campsite, you have energy to cook dinner, explore the town, and enjoy the evening.

## Planning Your Route

### Distance Per Day

On an e-bike with a 500 Wh battery on Eco mode, plan for:
- **Flat terrain:** 80–120 km per day
- **Moderate hills:** 60–90 km per day
- **Mountainous terrain:** 40–70 km per day

These assume 4–6 hours of riding per day with breaks. Adjust for your battery capacity and assist level.

### Charging Stops

The limiting factor on an e-bike tour is the battery. Plan your stages around charging opportunities:
- **Every accommodation has power.** A motel, hostel, campground (powered site), or pub will have a power outlet. Charge overnight — 4–6 hours for a full charge.
- **Cafes and pubs mid-ride.** Most will let you plug in the charger during a meal. A 2-hour lunch gives 40–60 % charge.
- **Libraries, visitor centres, community halls.** If you are riding through small towns, these often have accessible power outlets.
- **Carry the charger.** It weighs 500–800 g. Non-negotiable on a multi-day tour.

### Route Surfaces

- **Sealed roads:** fastest, most predictable range. Choose quiet back roads over highways.
- **Rail trails:** Australia has hundreds of kilometres of rail trails — flat, scenic, mostly sealed or hard-packed gravel. Ideal for touring.
- **Gravel roads:** slower, higher energy use. Budget 20–30 % more battery for gravel sections.
- **Singletrack:** not suitable for a loaded touring bike.

### Australian Touring Routes

**Multi-day routes worth riding:**
- **Murray to Mountains Rail Trail** (VIC): 116 km, mostly flat, wine country. 2–3 days.
- **Great Victorian Rail Trail** (VIC): 134 km, Australia's longest rail trail. 2–4 days.
- **Munda Biddi Trail** (WA): 1,000+ km, Perth to Albany. Weeks of riding through WA bush.
- **Brisbane Valley Rail Trail** (QLD): 161 km, rural southeast Queensland. 2–3 days.
- **East Gippsland Rail Trail** (VIC): 96 km, forests and farms. 2 days.
- **Riesling Trail** (SA): 35 km, Clare Valley wines. 1 day (or 2 with winery stops).
- **Pacific Coast Route** (NSW/QLD): Sydney to Brisbane via the coast. 1,000+ km on roads. 2–3 weeks.

## What to Carry

### Bikepacking (Minimalist)

Bags attach directly to the bike — no racks needed. Lighter, more aerodynamic, but less capacity.

**Setup:**
- Handlebar bag (8–15 L): tent or bivvy, sleeping pad
- Frame bag (3–6 L): tools, pump, heavy items
- Seat bag (8–16 L): sleeping bag, clothes
- Top tube bag (0.5–1 L): phone, snacks, wallet

**Total capacity:** 20–38 L. Enough for 2–3 season camping if you pack light.

### Panniers (Traditional Touring)

Bags hang on racks — front and/or rear. More capacity, easier to access, but heavier and less aerodynamic.

**Setup:**
- Rear panniers (pair, 40 L total): camping gear, clothes, food
- Front panniers (pair, 24 L total — optional): distribute weight for better handling
- Handlebar bag (5–10 L): camera, rain jacket, snacks

**Total capacity:** 50–74 L. Enough for extended touring with full camping gear.

### Packing List (3-Season, 3–7 Day Tour)

**Shelter:** tent or bivvy, sleeping pad, sleeping bag (comfort rating 5–10 °C for Australian 3-season)

**Clothing:** 2 cycling kits (wear one, wash one), rain jacket, warm layer (fleece or down jacket), off-bike clothes (shorts, t-shirt, thongs), gloves, arm warmers

**Cooking (if camping):** lightweight stove, pot, spork, lighter, 2–3 dehydrated meals per day between resupply

**Electronics:** phone, charger, e-bike charger, power bank (10,000 mAh — charges phone 2–3 times), headlamp

**Tools:** multi-tool, tyre levers, spare tube, patch kit, pump, chain quick link, small bottle of chain lube

**Personal:** sunscreen, lip balm with SPF, first aid kit (bandages, antiseptic, pain relief), insect repellent

**Total weight (bikepacking):** 6–10 kg
**Total weight (panniers):** 10–18 kg

## Battery Strategy for Touring

### Know Your Range

Before the tour, measure your actual range on a representative ride (loaded, on similar terrain, in the assist level you plan to use). Do not trust the manufacturer's claimed range — it is measured under ideal conditions.

### Conserve Battery

- **Use Eco or Tour mode** for 80 % of riding. Save Sport and Turbo for steep hills.
- **Shift gears actively** (especially on a mid-drive). The right gear keeps the motor in its efficient RPM range.
- **Pedal harder on flat sections.** You have all day — contributing more pedal power extends range significantly.
- **Inflate tyres to the upper end of the range** for less rolling resistance (adjust down on rough surfaces for comfort and grip).

### Dual Battery

If your e-bike supports a range extender (second battery), carry it. The extra 1–2 kg is worth the peace of mind on remote routes where charging is not guaranteed.

### Emergency: Battery Runs Out

If the battery dies mid-ride, you can still pedal. An e-bike without assist is just a heavy bike (20–25 kg). It is not fun, but it gets you to the next town. Mid-drive motors have less pedalling resistance than hub motors when unpowered.

## Accommodation Options

| Type | Cost (per night) | Power for charging | Availability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wild camping (free camps) | Free | No (unless solar charger) | Remote areas, WikiCamps app |
| Campground (powered site) | $20–$45 | Yes | Common in regional towns |
| Campground (unpowered) | $10–$25 | Limited (ask reception) | Very common |
| Hostel / backpackers | $25–$50 | Yes | Towns and cities |
| Pub / hotel | $80–$150 | Yes | Most regional towns |
| Motel | $100–$180 | Yes | Highway towns |

**Budget touring:** campgrounds and free camps. $10–$30 per night, plus food.
**Comfort touring:** pubs and motels. $80–$180 per night, includes a proper bed, a shower, and a counter meal.

## Bottom Line

E-bike touring is the most accessible way to see Australia by bike. The motor turns long days and hilly terrain from athletic challenges into enjoyable rides. Plan your daily stages around battery range and charging opportunities (accommodation, cafes, towns). Pack light (bikepacking) or pack for comfort (panniers). Budget $20–$50 per day for campground touring or $100–$200 for pub/motel touring. Start with a 2–3 day ride on a rail trail — it is the easiest way to test your setup and discover that e-bike touring is the best way to travel.

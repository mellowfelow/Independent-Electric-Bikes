The battery is the heart of any mobility scooter. It decides how far you can go, how reliable your day will be, and, eventually, how much you spend on running costs. Looked after well, a scooter battery will keep delivering for years. Treated poorly, it will lose capacity quickly and leave you short of range at the worst moment. This guide explains the main battery types, how to charge sensibly, what affects range, and when to think about replacement.

## The Two Main Battery Types

Most mobility scooters use one of two battery technologies.

### AGM (sealed lead-acid)

AGM batteries are a sealed lead-acid design that has been used in mobility scooters for many years.

- **Pros:** Generally lower upfront cost, widely available, familiar technology.
- **Cons:** Heavier, bulkier, and more sensitive to being left discharged. They usually offer fewer charge cycles than lithium.

### Lithium

Lithium batteries are increasingly common, particularly in travel and folding scooters.

- **Pros:** Lighter for the same capacity, compact, and typically tolerate more charge cycles. They often charge faster and can be easier to remove and carry.
- **Cons:** Usually higher upfront cost, and they have specific rules for transport and air travel.

Which is better depends on your priorities. If portability and weight matter, lithium is often attractive. If you want a lower purchase price and mostly ride locally, AGM can be a sensible option. Whatever type you choose, follow the manufacturer's instructions for charging and care.

## Good Charging Habits

How you charge matters as much as which battery you have.

- **Use the charger supplied** or one recommended by the manufacturer. Mixing chargers and batteries can damage the battery or create a safety risk.
- **Charge in a cool, dry, ventilated place**, away from direct sun, heat sources, and flammable materials.
- **Recharge after use.** Lead-acid batteries in particular dislike being left flat for long periods.
- **Do not leave the battery on charge indefinitely** unless the charger is designed for it. Follow the manual.
- **Avoid charging in extreme heat or cold.** Allow a very hot or cold battery to reach room temperature first.
- **Check the charger and cables.** Frayed leads or damaged plugs should be replaced before use.
- **Never charge a damaged or swollen battery.** Stop using it and seek advice from the retailer.

Lithium batteries come with built-in management systems that protect against some problems, but they still need sensible handling. Keep them away from impacts, moisture, and high temperatures.

## What Affects Range

The range printed on the box is measured under ideal conditions, so your real-world result will often be lower. Things that reduce range include:

- **Hills and slopes**, which require more power.
- **Rider weight and carried loads.**
- **Rough or soft surfaces**, such as grass, gravel, or sand.
- **Stop-start riding**, with frequent acceleration.
- **Higher speeds**, which use more energy.
- **Cold weather**, which can temporarily reduce battery performance.
- **Tyre pressure**, since soft tyres increase rolling resistance.
- **An ageing battery**, which holds less charge than when new.

Think of the listed range as a best-case figure and plan a margin. If your usual trips are close to the scooter's stated limit, consider a model with a larger battery or one that accepts a spare.

## Getting More From Every Charge

A few simple habits can stretch your range:

1. **Keep tyres at the recommended pressure.**
2. **Ride at a moderate, steady speed.**
3. **Plan routes with fewer steep hills** where possible.
4. **Travel light.** Avoid carrying unnecessary weight.
5. **Keep the scooter clean and well maintained**, including wheels and bearings.
6. **Charge fully before longer outings.**
7. **Know your battery gauge.** Learn how it behaves rather than waiting for the last bar.

If you regularly run low, a second battery for models that support it may be the simplest fix.

## Storage and Seasonal Care

If you will not use the scooter for a while, store the battery correctly.

- **Lead-acid:** Store fully charged in a cool, dry place and top up periodically as the manual advises.
- **Lithium:** Manufacturers often recommend storing at a partial charge rather than full or empty. Check the manual for the recommended level.
- **General:** Keep the battery away from extreme temperatures, damp, and direct sunlight.

Do not store a battery in a hot car or a shed that reaches high temperatures in an Australian summer. Heat is one of the fastest ways to shorten battery life.

## Signs It Is Time to Replace the Battery

Batteries wear out gradually. Common signs include:

- **Noticeably shorter range** than when the scooter was new.
- **Slow or weak acceleration**, especially on hills.
- **The battery gauge dropping quickly** or behaving unpredictably.
- **The battery not holding charge** overnight.
- **Physical damage, swelling, or leaking.** Stop using it immediately.

How long a battery lasts depends on its type, how it is used, and how well it is looked after, so it is better to watch for these signs than to rely on a fixed number.

## Replacing and Disposing of Batteries

When it is time to replace a battery:

- **Match the specification.** Use a replacement that suits your scooter, following the manufacturer's guidance on voltage, capacity, and connectors.
- **Buy from a reputable supplier** who can confirm compatibility and provide a warranty.
- **Replace as a set** where the scooter uses more than one battery, as mismatched batteries can reduce performance.
- **Dispose of old batteries responsibly.** Do not put them in household bins. Lead-acid and lithium batteries should go to an appropriate recycling drop-off, and your retailer or local council can point you to options.

## Travel and Battery Rules

If you plan to take your scooter on a plane, cruise, or train, battery type and capacity can affect what is allowed. Lithium batteries in particular are subject to dangerous-goods rules, and requirements differ between airlines and operators. Check directly with them before you travel. ## Safety Reminders

- Keep batteries and chargers dry.
- Do not modify the battery or charger.
- Do not cover the charger while it is working.
- Keep the charging area clear of clutter and flammable items.
- Stop using any battery or charger that overheats, smells unusual, or shows damage.

If you ever have doubts about the safety of a battery, contact the manufacturer or retailer.

E-bikes are faster and quieter than regular bikes. On shared paths — where you ride alongside pedestrians, joggers, dog walkers, children, and slower cyclists — this combination creates conflict if you do not ride with awareness and courtesy.

## The Core Principle

Shared paths are shared. You do not have priority. Pedestrians, cyclists, wheelchair users, pram pushers, and dog walkers all have equal right to be there. Your motor gives you speed — it does not give you right of way.

## Speed

### Slow Down Near People
The single most important rule. On a shared path with pedestrians:
- **Pass at walking speed** (5–10 km/h) when passing within 1 metre
- **Slow to 15 km/h** on a crowded path
- **Ride at full speed only** when the path is clear and you have visibility ahead

An e-bike at 25 km/h covers 7 metres per second. A pedestrian stepping sideways unexpectedly gives you less than 1 second to react. At 10 km/h, you have 2.5 seconds. Speed is the variable you control — use it.

### Speed Limits on Shared Paths
Some councils post speed limits on shared paths (typically 20 km/h). Even where there is no posted limit, riding at 25 km/h on a busy shared path is antisocial and dangerous.

## Passing

### Announce Your Approach
E-bikes are quiet — pedestrians often do not hear you until you are alongside them. Announce your presence before passing:
- **Bell:** ring 10–20 metres before reaching the pedestrian. One short ring is enough — do not ring aggressively or repeatedly.
- **Voice:** "Passing on your left" (or right) in a friendly tone. Not shouted, not mumbled.
- **If wearing headphones:** they cannot hear your bell. Slow down further and pass with maximum clearance.

### Give Space
Pass with at least 1 metre of clearance. More is better. If the path is too narrow for 1 metre clearance, slow to walking speed and pass carefully.

### Wait If Necessary
If the path is too narrow or too crowded to pass safely, slow down and wait. You are on a bike with a motor — you will make up the lost 10 seconds easily.

### Check Behind Before Pulling Out
Before moving to the opposite side of the path to pass, check behind you. Another cyclist may be overtaking you at the same time.

## Dogs

### Assume Every Dog Is Unpredictable
Dogs on shared paths are unpredictable. They dart sideways, chase bikes, pull their owners across the path, and do not understand bell warnings.

**Rules:**
- Slow to walking speed near any dog, on-lead or off-lead
- Give dogs maximum clearance (2+ metres if possible)
- If a dog is off-lead on the path, slow to a crawl and pass carefully — or stop and let the owner control the dog
- Never ride fast past a dog — a startled dog may bite or pull its owner into your path

### Retractable Leads
Retractable leads can stretch 5–8 metres across a path — often invisible until you are about to ride through the line between the owner and the dog. Watch for the lead and slow down.

## Children

### Children Are Unpredictable
Children on shared paths (walking, on scooters, on bikes) change direction suddenly, wander across the path, stop without warning, and do not respond to bells predictably.

**Rules:**
- Slow to walking speed near children
- Cover the brakes (fingers on brake levers, ready to stop instantly)
- Make eye contact with the parent/carer — they may help you pass safely
- Never pass between a child and their parent/carer

## Other Cyclists

### Faster and Slower
On a shared path, you will encounter:
- Slower cyclists (children, elderly, casual riders) — pass them as you would a pedestrian: announce, give space, pass smoothly
- Faster cyclists (road riders, other e-bike riders) — check behind before pulling out to pass, and hold your line so faster riders can pass you safely

### Do Not Race
An e-bike allows you to keep up with (or pass) road cyclists on shared paths. This is not an invitation to race. Shared paths are not racing venues. Ride at a safe, considerate speed regardless of what other cyclists are doing.

## Specific Situations

### Blind Corners
Many shared paths have blind corners where the path curves around trees, fences, or buildings. You cannot see what is around the corner.
- **Slow down before every blind corner**
- Stay on your side of the path
- Be prepared to stop if someone is coming the other way

### Narrow Bridges and Underpasses
One cyclist wide. Yield to whoever arrived first. If a pedestrian is on the bridge, wait for them to clear before riding across.

### Path Crossings (Roads)
Stop or yield at every road crossing. Drivers may not see you — especially if you are riding at 25 km/h and they are looking for pedestrians walking at 5 km/h.

### Construction Zones
Where paths are narrowed by construction, slow to walking speed. Workers, barriers, and rough surfaces all increase risk.

## The Courtesy Hierarchy

When in doubt, yield to the more vulnerable user:
1. **Wheelchair users / mobility scooters** — always yield
2. **Pedestrians with disabilities** — always yield
3. **Children** — always yield
4. **Pedestrians** — yield when passing
5. **Joggers / runners** — yield when passing
6. **Slower cyclists** — pass carefully with announcement
7. **Other e-bike riders** — ride predictably, share the space

You have a motor. You can accelerate back to speed in seconds. The minor delay from yielding costs you nothing.

## Legal Obligations (Australia)

### General Road Rules (Apply to Shared Paths)
- **Helmet:** compulsory in all states and territories
- **Lights:** required at night (front white light, rear red light)
- **Bell:** required to be fitted (and used to warn of approach) — VIC, NSW, QLD, SA, WA, TAS, ACT, NT
- **Footpath riding:** varies by state. Generally permitted in QLD, TAS, ACT, NT. Prohibited in VIC, NSW (adults), SA, WA. Check your state's rules.
- **Give way:** cyclists must give way to pedestrians on shared paths

### Fines
Riding in a manner that is dangerous or inconsiderate on a shared path can result in fines ($100–$500+ depending on the state and the offence). Colliding with a pedestrian due to excessive speed can result in criminal charges.

## Bottom Line

Shared paths are shared. Slow down near people, announce your approach with a bell or voice, give at least 1 metre clearance when passing, and yield to pedestrians, children, dogs, and wheelchair users. Your motor recovers any lost speed in seconds — the courtesy costs you nothing. The faster and quieter your e-bike, the more responsibility you have to ride considerately. A reputation for good e-bike behaviour on shared paths protects the access and the welcome that e-bike riders currently enjoy.

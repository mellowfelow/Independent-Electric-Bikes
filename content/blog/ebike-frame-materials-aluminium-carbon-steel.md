The frame is the skeleton of your e-bike. It determines weight, ride feel, durability, and price. Three materials dominate: aluminium, steel, and carbon fibre. Here is what each one actually means for your riding.

## Aluminium (6061-T6 / 6069)

### The Default
90 %+ of e-bikes sold in Australia use aluminium frames. It is the default because it offers the best balance of weight, cost, and corrosion resistance.

### Properties
- **Weight:** light. An aluminium e-bike frame weighs 2–3 kg (frame only, no motor/battery). A complete aluminium e-bike weighs 18–25 kg.
- **Stiffness:** high. Aluminium is stiff relative to its weight — pedal input transfers efficiently to the wheels. The trade-off: stiffness transmits road vibration to the rider (a harsher ride over rough roads compared to steel).
- **Corrosion:** excellent resistance. Aluminium forms a thin oxide layer that protects it from further corrosion. It handles rain, sweat, and coastal salt air without rusting. No paint required for corrosion protection (though all frames are painted for aesthetics).
- **Fatigue life:** finite. Aluminium has a fatigue limit — it weakens with every flex cycle. After enough stress cycles, it will crack regardless of load. Practically, this means 10–20+ years for a well-made frame under normal use, but aluminium frames do not last forever the way steel can.
- **Repairability:** difficult. Aluminium welding requires specialised equipment (TIG welding with argon shielding) and heat-treating after repair. A cracked aluminium frame is usually replaced, not repaired.

### Best For
Most riders. Aluminium is the right choice for commuting, recreational riding, touring, and mountain biking. It keeps the price reasonable and the weight manageable.

### Price
$800–$2,000 for the frame alone (as part of a complete e-bike: $1,500–$5,000).

## Steel (Chromoly / Reynolds / 4130)

### The Classic
Steel was the only frame material for a century. It is still used on touring bikes, commuters, and custom builds. Less common on e-bikes — the extra weight of the motor and battery makes the frame weight matter less, and aluminium is cheaper to mass-produce.

### Properties
- **Weight:** heavier than aluminium. A steel e-bike frame weighs 3–5 kg. This adds 1–2 kg to the total bike weight — noticeable on a regular bike, barely noticeable on a 22 kg e-bike with a motor doing the work.
- **Stiffness:** lower than aluminium (more flex). This is actually an advantage for ride quality — steel absorbs road vibration, giving a smoother, more comfortable ride. Riders describe it as "springy" or "alive."
- **Corrosion:** steel rusts. It needs paint, powder coating, or rust inhibitor inside the tubes. Scratches in the paint should be touched up to prevent rust. In coastal areas or wet climates, this requires attention.
- **Fatigue life:** essentially infinite. Below its fatigue threshold, steel can flex indefinitely without weakening. A well-maintained steel frame can last 30–50+ years. This is why steel touring bikes from the 1980s are still ridden today.
- **Repairability:** excellent. Any competent welder can repair a steel frame. Dents can be straightened. Broken dropouts can be replaced. In remote touring situations, this matters — you can find a steel welder almost anywhere in the world.

### Best For
Touring riders, commuters who value comfort, riders who want a frame that lasts decades, custom builds.

### Price
$1,200–$3,000 for the frame (as part of a complete e-bike: $2,500–$6,000). Premium steel (Reynolds, Columbus) costs more than aluminium.

## Carbon Fibre

### The Premium Option
Carbon fibre is the lightest and most expensive frame material. It dominates road racing and high-end mountain biking. On e-bikes, it appears on premium models ($5,000+).

### Properties
- **Weight:** lightest. A carbon e-bike frame weighs 1.5–2.5 kg. A complete carbon e-bike can weigh 15–19 kg — closer to a regular bike's weight.
- **Stiffness:** tuneable. Carbon fibre's unique advantage: engineers can make the frame stiff where it needs to be (bottom bracket, head tube) and compliant where comfort matters (seat stays, top tube). This "tuned ride" is impossible with metal frames, where the entire tube has uniform properties.
- **Corrosion:** immune. Carbon fibre does not corrode, rust, or oxidise. It is unaffected by water, salt, UV, or chemicals.
- **Fatigue life:** excellent under normal loads. Carbon does not fatigue like aluminium — it can flex indefinitely within its design load. However, it is vulnerable to impact damage (see below).
- **Impact resistance:** poor. Carbon fibre is strong in the direction of its fibres but brittle against impacts. A rock strike, a fall, or a crash can crack the layup internally — and the damage may not be visible on the surface. An internally cracked carbon frame can fail suddenly and catastrophically.
- **Repairability:** specialist only. Carbon frame repair involves cutting out the damaged area, relaying carbon cloth, and curing under vacuum. It is expensive ($200–$600) and must be done by a certified carbon repair shop. Not all damage is repairable.

### Best For
Weight-conscious riders, road and gravel e-bikes, riders with a higher budget, racing.

### Price
$2,500–$5,000+ for the frame (as part of a complete e-bike: $5,000–$15,000).

## Comparison Table

| Property | Aluminium | Steel | Carbon Fibre |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weight (frame) | 2–3 kg | 3–5 kg | 1.5–2.5 kg |
| Ride quality | Stiff, harsh | Smooth, springy | Tuneable |
| Corrosion | Excellent | Requires protection | Immune |
| Fatigue life | Finite (10–20+ years) | Essentially infinite | Excellent |
| Impact resistance | Good (dents) | Excellent (dents, bends) | Poor (cracks) |
| Repairability | Difficult | Excellent | Specialist |
| Cost | Low–Medium | Medium–High | High–Very High |

## Does Frame Material Matter on an E-Bike?

Less than on a regular bike. Here is why:

### Weight Difference Is Smaller (Relatively)
On a 9 kg road bike, saving 1 kg by choosing carbon over aluminium is an 11 % weight reduction — significant. On a 22 kg e-bike, the same 1 kg saving is 4.5 % — barely noticeable, especially when the motor is doing much of the work.

### The Motor Masks Ride Feel
On a regular bike, you feel every road vibration through your pedal strokes. On an e-bike, the motor smooths the power delivery, and the heavier bike damps vibration. The ride-quality difference between aluminium and steel — dramatic on a regular bike — is reduced on an e-bike.

### Battery Range Matters More
The difference in rolling resistance and aerodynamic drag between frame materials is negligible on an e-bike at 20–25 km/h. Battery capacity, motor efficiency, tyre choice, and assist level have far more impact on range than frame weight.

### When It DOES Matter
- **If you need to carry the bike** (up stairs, onto a train, into a car): weight matters — consider carbon or lightweight aluminium.
- **If you ride rough roads daily:** ride comfort matters — consider steel.
- **If you want the bike to last 20+ years:** longevity matters — consider steel.
- **If your budget is fixed:** cost matters — aluminium gives you more motor/battery/components for the money.

## Bottom Line

For most e-bike buyers, aluminium is the right choice — it is light enough, stiff enough, corrosion-resistant, affordable, and available on 90 % of e-bikes. Steel is better for comfort and longevity but heavier and requires rust protection. Carbon is the lightest and most refined but expensive and vulnerable to impact damage. On an e-bike, the frame material matters less than on a regular bike because the motor compensates for weight differences and the heavier platform damps ride-quality differences. Spend your budget on a better motor, larger battery, and quality brakes before upgrading the frame material.

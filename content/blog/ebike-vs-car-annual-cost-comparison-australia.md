Australians spend an average of $12,000–$16,000 per year to own and operate a car. An e-bike costs $300–$600 per year to run. The maths is not close — but the real question is whether an e-bike can replace enough car trips to matter.

## Annual Cost of Car Ownership (2026 Australian Average)

| Cost category | Annual estimate |
|---|---|
| Depreciation | $3,000–$6,000 |
| Fuel | $2,500–$4,000 |
| Registration | $800–$900 |
| Insurance (comprehensive) | $1,200–$2,000 |
| Servicing and maintenance | $800–$1,500 |
| Tyres | $200–$400 |
| Parking (CBD commuters) | $2,000–$6,000 |
| Tolls (Sydney/Melbourne) | $500–$2,000 |
| **Total** | **$11,000–$22,800** |

Source: AAA Transport Affordability Index, RACV cost-of-motoring reports.

These numbers assume a medium-size car driven 15,000 km per year. Luxury vehicles, SUVs, and high-mileage drivers cost significantly more.

The difference — $10,000–$22,000 per year — is enough to pay off the e-bike itself within the first year, even for a premium model.

## Electricity Cost: Almost Free

Charging an e-bike costs roughly 5–10 cents per charge. A 500 Wh battery charged from empty uses 0.5 kWh of electricity. At the average Australian electricity rate of $0.30/kWh, that is $0.15 per full charge — giving you 50–80 km of range.

**Annual electricity cost for daily commuting (10 km each way, 250 working days):** approximately $20–$40.

Compare that to fuel for the same 5,000 km: $500–$800 (at $2.00/L and 8 L/100 km for a typical car).

## Which Car Trips Can an E-Bike Replace?

Not all car trips. But data from Australian transport surveys shows that the majority of trips are short:

- **50 % of car trips are under 5 km**
- **75 % of car trips are under 10 km**
- **The average commute distance in Melbourne is 16 km (one way)**

An e-bike with a 500 Wh battery covers 40–70 km per charge. That means:

- **Daily commutes under 30 km round trip:** fully replaceable by e-bike
- **Grocery runs:** replaceable with a rear rack and panniers (or a cargo e-bike for larger loads)
- **School drop-offs:** replaceable with a cargo e-bike and child seats
- **Gym/sport:** replaceable — ride the e-bike to the gym and skip the warm-up

The trips an e-bike cannot replace:
- Long-distance highway driving (100+ km)
- Carrying four or more passengers
- Towing a trailer or caravan
- Driving in severe weather (thunderstorms, heatwaves above 40 °C)

## The Second Car Calculation

Many Australian households own two cars. The second car is typically used for shorter trips — school runs, errands, the second commute. This is exactly the use case an e-bike replaces.

**Selling the second car saves:**
- $3,000–$6,000 in depreciation per year
- $800–$900 in registration
- $1,200–$2,000 in insurance
- $1,000–$2,000 in fuel and maintenance
- **Total: $6,000–$10,900 per year**

**Buying an e-bike costs:** $2,000–$5,000 (one-time). **Annual running cost:** $300–$600.

The e-bike pays for itself in 3–10 months.

## Health Savings

This is harder to quantify but real. Regular e-bike commuting provides:

- **150–300 minutes of moderate exercise per week** (10 km each way at moderate assist, 5 days a week)
- **Reduced risk of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, and obesity** — the same benefits as regular cycling, with lower joint impact due to motor assist
- **Mental health benefits** — outdoor exercise, reduced commute stress, no traffic jams

A 2023 study from the University of Melbourne found that regular e-bike commuters had 20 % lower rates of GP visits compared to car commuters, after controlling for age and pre-existing conditions.

## Environmental Impact

| Factor | Car (petrol, 15,000 km/year) | E-bike (5,000 km/year) |
|---|---|---|
| CO2 emissions | 2,500–3,500 kg/year | 5–15 kg/year (electricity generation) |
| Particulate emissions | Brake dust, tyre wear, exhaust | Minimal brake dust, minimal tyre wear |
| Road space | 12 m² per parked car | 1.5 m² per parked e-bike |
| Noise | 65–80 dB at 50 km/h | Near silent |

An e-bike produces roughly 99 % less CO2 per kilometre than a petrol car.

## What You Lose

Honesty requires acknowledging the trade-offs:

- **Weather exposure:** you are outside in the rain, wind, cold, and heat. Gear helps, but a car is climate-controlled.
- **Carrying capacity:** a car boot holds 300–500 litres. A pair of panniers holds 40–60 litres.
- **Speed on long trips:** a car covers 100 km in 60–70 minutes. An e-bike covers 100 km in 4–5 hours.
- **Social expectations:** some workplaces and social situations expect you to arrive by car. This is changing, slowly.
- **Safety perception:** cycling on Australian roads carries real risks, particularly on roads without bike infrastructure.

## Bottom Line

An e-bike will not replace a car for every trip. But for the 50–75 % of trips that are under 10 km — commuting, errands, school runs, social visits — an e-bike does the job for a fraction of the cost, with zero fuel, zero parking, and a dose of exercise. The annual saving of $6,000–$15,000 compared to a second car is not theoretical — it is the actual cost difference for thousands of Australian households who have already made the switch.

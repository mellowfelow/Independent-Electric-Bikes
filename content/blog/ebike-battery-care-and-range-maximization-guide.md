Your e-bike battery is the most expensive single component on the bike — typically $500 to $1,500 to replace. With proper care, a quality lithium-ion pack should deliver 800–1,000 full charge cycles before dropping to 80 % capacity. That translates to 3–5 years of daily commuting. Here is how to get every kilometre out of it.

## Understanding Battery Capacity

E-bike batteries are rated in **watt-hours (Wh)**. To calculate: volts × amp-hours = watt-hours. A 48 V × 14 Ah battery is 672 Wh. Higher watt-hours means more range, but also more weight and cost.

Typical ranges per charge at moderate assist on flat terrain:

| Battery size | Estimated range |
|---|---|
| 400 Wh | 30–50 km |
| 500 Wh | 40–70 km |
| 625 Wh | 50–90 km |
| 720 Wh | 60–110 km |

Real-world range varies with rider weight, terrain, wind, tyre pressure, and assist level.

## The 20–80 Rule

Lithium-ion cells age fastest when held at very high or very low charge states. The single most effective thing you can do for battery longevity is keep the charge between **20 % and 80 %** for everyday use. Charge to 100 % only before a long ride where you need the full range.

Most modern e-bike displays show the percentage. Some chargers (like the Bosch 4A Standard Charger) can be set to stop at 80 % automatically.

## Heat Is the Enemy

Lithium cells degrade rapidly above 40 °C. In an Australian summer, temperatures inside a parked car or a shed without ventilation can exceed 50 °C. Practical guidelines:

- **Never leave the battery in a parked car** — not even in the boot
- **Store indoors** in a climate-controlled space when not riding
- **Charge in a ventilated area** and never on carpet or fabric (which traps heat)
- **Let the battery cool** after a ride before plugging in the charger — wait 15–30 minutes

If you ride in 35 °C+ heat, the Battery Management System (BMS) may throttle power to protect the cells. This is normal and not a fault.

## Cold Weather Riding

Below 5 °C, battery capacity drops temporarily — you may see 10–20 % less range. The capacity returns to normal once the pack warms up. If you store the bike in an unheated garage overnight during winter, bring the battery inside. Starting a ride with a warm battery gives noticeably better performance.

## Charging Best Practices

1. **Use the original charger** (or a manufacturer-approved replacement). Third-party chargers with incorrect voltage or current ratings can damage cells or create fire risk.
2. **Do not charge unattended overnight** if you can avoid it. Most fires occur during charging, not riding.
3. **Unplug when done**. Trickle-charging a full battery for hours adds stress to the cells.
4. **Charge after every ride** if the battery is below 40 %. Sitting at very low charge accelerates degradation.

## Long-Term Storage

If you will not ride for more than two weeks:

- Charge the battery to **50–60 %**
- Remove it from the bike
- Store it indoors at 10–25 °C
- Check the charge every 4–6 weeks and top up to 50 % if it has dropped below 30 %

A battery stored at 100 % charge for months will lose more permanent capacity than one stored at 50 %.

## When to Replace

Replace the battery when:

- Range has dropped below 60 % of the original spec despite correct tyre pressure and riding conditions
- The BMS triggers frequent low-voltage cut-offs
- The pack shows physical swelling, cracking, or a chemical smell

Always recycle old lithium batteries through an authorised collection point — never put them in household waste. In Australia, B-cycle and Officeworks accept lithium batteries for recycling.

## Maximising Range on Every Ride

Beyond battery care, these habits extend your per-ride range:

- **Tyre pressure**: run at the maximum rated pressure for sealed roads. Under-inflated tyres waste 10–15 % of your energy.
- **Lower assist levels**: Eco mode uses roughly half the energy of Turbo mode for only 2–3 km/h less speed on flat ground.
- **Pedal smoothly**: steady cadence between 60–80 RPM is more efficient than mashing the pedals.
- **Reduce drag**: panniers create less wind resistance than a backpack worn high on your shoulders, but both matter less below 25 km/h.

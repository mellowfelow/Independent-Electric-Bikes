The bike itself is only half the purchase. The right accessories make the difference between a ride that is safe, comfortable, and practical versus one that is nerve-wracking, sweaty, and inconvenient. Here are the accessories every e-bike rider in Australia should consider — ranked by priority.

## Priority 1: Safety (Non-Negotiable)

### Helmet

Helmets are **legally required** in every Australian state and territory. No exceptions, no excuses. A fine for riding without one ranges from $100 to $400 depending on the state.

**What to look for:**
- **Australian Standard AS/NZS 2063** compliance — this is the minimum legal requirement
- **MIPS (Multi-directional Impact Protection System)** — a liner that rotates slightly on impact to reduce rotational forces. A meaningful safety upgrade for $20–$40 more than a non-MIPS helmet
- **Ventilation** — critical in Australian summers. More vents = cooler head = more likely to actually wear it
- **Fit** — a helmet that does not fit properly does not protect properly. Try before you buy, or choose a brand with detailed sizing charts

**Budget:** $60–$80 for a solid AS/NZS 2063 helmet. $100–$180 for MIPS-equipped models from brands like Giro, Bell, or Lazer.

### Lights

Front and rear lights are **legally required after dark** in every state. But even in daylight, running lights significantly increase your visibility to drivers.

**Front light:**
- **Minimum 400 lumens** for road riding at night. 800+ lumens if you ride unlit paths or trails.
- **USB rechargeable** — battery lights are wasteful and die at the worst times
- **Quick-detach mount** — remove it when you park to prevent theft

**Rear light:**
- **Minimum 50 lumens** with a flash mode. Flashing rear lights are legal in all states and significantly more visible to drivers.
- **Brake-light function** (some models detect deceleration and brighten) is a genuine safety advantage at intersections.

**Budget:** $40–$80 for a quality front/rear set. Brands like Lezyne, Knog, and CatEye are reliable.

### Lock

Covered in detail in our [lock security guide](/blog/how-to-secure-your-ebike-locks-gps-and-insurance/), but the summary: **buy the best lock you can afford and use two different types.** A U-lock plus a cable lock is the minimum. Budget $80–$200 for a serious lock setup.

## Priority 2: Comfort and Practicality

### Mudguards (Fenders)

Melbourne averages 150+ rain days per year. Without mudguards, a wet road sends a continuous spray of dirty water up your back, into your face, and across your drivetrain. Full-length mudguards (not clip-on "ass savers") are the only option that works properly.

**What to look for:**
- **Full coverage** — the guard should extend to within 10–20 mm of the ground at the rear
- **Frame compatibility** — check that your bike has mounting eyelets or that the guards come with universal clamps
- **Width** — the guard should be at least as wide as the tyre

**Budget:** $30–$60 for a quality set. SKS Bluemels and Topeak DeFender are well-regarded.

### Rear Rack and Panniers

Carrying weight on your back makes you sweat, raises your centre of gravity, and is uncomfortable beyond 5 km. A rear rack transfers the load to the bike's frame — lower, more stable, and no sweaty back.

**Rack:** look for one rated to 25 kg+ (e-bikes are heavy enough that a light rack risks fatigue failure). Aluminium alloy is the sweet spot for weight and strength. Check that your frame has rack mounting eyelets or use a seatpost-clamp adapter.

**Panniers:** waterproof panniers keep your laptop, clothes, and lunch dry. Ortlieb Back-Roller is the gold standard. Budget alternatives from Rhinowalk and Rockbros work well for half the price.

**Budget:** $40–$80 for a rack, $60–$200 for panniers.

### Kickstand

Many e-bikes ship without a kickstand. A 25 kg e-bike leaned against a wall or dropped on the ground is a scratched frame and a broken display waiting to happen. A sturdy centre-mount or rear-mount kickstand costs $15–$30 and saves hundreds in cosmetic damage.

## Priority 3: Performance and Maintenance

### Tyre Repair Kit

A flat tyre 10 km from home is the most common ride-ending event. Carry:

- **Spare inner tube** (correct size for your wheel)
- **Tyre levers** (two plastic levers)
- **Mini pump or CO2 inflator** — a pump is reusable; CO2 is faster but single-use
- **Patch kit** as a backup

If your e-bike has tubeless tyres, carry a tubeless plug kit and a CO2 inflator instead.

**Budget:** $20–$40 for a complete under-saddle kit.

### Multi-Tool

A compact bike multi-tool with Allen keys (3, 4, 5, 6 mm), Phillips and flat screwdrivers, and a chain tool handles most roadside adjustments. Crankbrothers M19 and Topeak Mini 20 are popular choices.

**Budget:** $20–$40.

### Phone Mount

Navigation, fitness tracking, and music control all need your phone visible. A handlebar phone mount keeps it accessible without fishing it out of a pocket at every turn.

**What to look for:**
- **Secure grip** — vibration from rough roads can bounce a phone out of a spring-clip mount. Quad-lock twist-lock or magnetic mounts are more secure.
- **Waterproof case or cover** — if your mount does not protect the phone from rain, add a zip-lock bag as a last resort

**Budget:** $15–$40 for a basic mount. $60–$100 for a Quad Lock system.

### Bell

Legally required in most states. A clear, audible bell warns pedestrians on shared paths. The classic Spurcycle or Knog Oi bells sound better and last longer than the $3 bell your bike may have shipped with.

**Budget:** $10–$40.

## Priority 4: Seasonal and Situational

### Gloves

Padded cycling gloves reduce hand fatigue on long rides and protect your palms in a fall. In winter, insulated gloves keep your fingers functional in cold morning commutes. In summer, fingerless gloves provide grip without overheating.

### Mirror

A handlebar or helmet-mounted mirror lets you check traffic without turning your head. Particularly useful in heavy traffic and on roads without bike lanes.

### Rain Jacket

A lightweight, packable rain jacket takes up minimal space in a pannier and keeps you dry during Melbourne's surprise showers. Look for breathable fabrics — a fully waterproof but non-breathable jacket turns into a sauna at e-bike speeds.

## Bottom Line

Start with safety: helmet, lights, lock. Add comfort next: mudguards, rack, panniers, kickstand. Then performance: repair kit, multi-tool, phone mount. The total cost of a well-accessorised e-bike setup is $300–$600 — a small fraction of the bike itself, but a large fraction of the riding experience.

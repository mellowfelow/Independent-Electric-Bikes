Every e-bike delivers power in one of two ways: pedal assist or throttle. Some bikes offer both. The mode you choose affects legality, range, exercise, and riding feel. Here is what each does, how they differ, and which is right for you.

## Pedal Assist (Pedelec)

Pedal assist means the motor only runs when you pedal. The motor detects your pedalling (via a cadence sensor or torque sensor) and adds power proportionally or at a set level. When you stop pedalling, the motor stops.

### How Cadence Sensing Works

A ring of magnets on the crank detects that the pedals are turning. The controller then delivers a fixed percentage of motor power based on the assist level you select (Eco, Normal, Sport, Turbo). The motor does not know how hard you are pedalling — only that you are pedalling.

**Feel:** the motor kicks in about half a pedal revolution after you start. It delivers consistent power regardless of your effort. If you soft-pedal, the motor does the same work as when you push hard. This can feel artificial — like the bike is pulling you rather than assisting you.

### How Torque Sensing Works

A strain gauge in the bottom bracket or crank arm measures how much force you apply to the pedals. The motor matches your effort — push harder and the motor pushes harder. Ease off and the motor eases off.

**Feel:** natural and intuitive. The bike feels like your legs have been upgraded, not like a motor has been strapped on. Torque-sensing systems are universally praised by experienced riders as the superior pedal-assist experience.

**Downside:** torque sensors cost more. Budget e-bikes almost always use cadence sensors. Mid-range and premium e-bikes use torque sensors.

### Assist Levels

Most pedal-assist e-bikes offer 3–5 assist levels:

| Level | Motor contribution | Battery drain | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eco | 30–50 % | Low | Gentle push, maximum range |
| Tour / Normal | 60–100 % | Moderate | Comfortable cruising speed |
| Sport | 100–200 % | High | Fast acceleration, strong hill climbing |
| Turbo / Boost | 200–300 % | Very high | Maximum power, short-range sprint |

The percentages refer to how much motor power is added relative to your pedal input (on torque-sensing systems). On cadence-sensing systems, the levels simply set a fixed motor power output.

## Throttle

A throttle delivers motor power without pedalling. Twist the grip (twist throttle) or press a thumb lever (thumb throttle) and the motor runs. Release it and the motor stops. Your legs do not need to move at all.

**Feel:** like a motor scooter. You control speed entirely with your hand. No pedalling required, though you can pedal simultaneously if you want.

### Types of Throttle

- **Twist throttle:** rotates the grip, like a motorcycle. Variable speed control — the further you twist, the more power. Natural and precise.
- **Thumb throttle:** a lever pressed by your thumb. Variable speed control. Less intuitive than twist but does not interfere with grip position.
- **Half-twist throttle:** only the inner portion of the grip rotates, so you can still hold the grip firmly while throttling. The most common type on e-bikes.

## Legal Implications in Australia

This is the critical difference for Australian riders.

### Pedal Assist (EN 15194 Compliant)

An e-bike qualifies as a **bicycle** (not a motor vehicle) in Australia if:
- It is **pedal-assist only** (no throttle, or throttle limited to walk-assist under 6 km/h)
- **Continuous rated motor power** is 250 W or less
- **Motor assistance cuts off at 25 km/h**

A compliant pedal-assist e-bike can be ridden:
- On all roads (where bicycles are allowed)
- On bike paths and shared paths
- Without registration, insurance, or a driver's licence
- By riders of any age (though helmets are mandatory for all)

### Throttle-Equipped E-Bikes

An e-bike with a throttle that propels the bike without pedalling is classified differently:
- If the throttle power is limited to 200 W and the bike has pedal assist limited to 250 W / 25 km/h, it is treated as a bicycle in most states
- If the throttle exceeds 200 W or can propel the bike above 25 km/h, it may be classified as a **motor vehicle** — requiring registration, CTP insurance, and a licence

**Practical advice:** if you buy a throttle-equipped e-bike for road use in Australia, ensure the throttle is limited to 200 W (or 6 km/h walk-assist only) and the pedal assist complies with EN 15194. Check your state's specific rules — they vary.

## Range Impact

Throttle-only riding drains the battery 2–3 times faster than pedal assist because the motor does 100 % of the work:

| Mode | Estimated range (500 Wh battery) |
|---|---|
| Pedal assist — Eco | 70–100 km |
| Pedal assist — Normal | 50–70 km |
| Pedal assist — Sport | 35–50 km |
| Throttle only | 20–35 km |

If range matters (commuting, touring, long rides), pedal assist is dramatically more efficient.

## Exercise

Pedal assist delivers genuine exercise — you are pedalling, your heart rate is elevated, your legs are working. Studies show that regular pedal-assist e-bike riders get 75–85 % of the cardiovascular benefit of regular cyclists, with significantly lower joint impact.

Throttle riding provides zero exercise. You are standing or sitting while the motor does all the work. Useful when you are tired, injured, or carrying heavy cargo — but not a fitness tool.

## When Throttle Is Useful

Throttle is not just for lazy riding. It serves practical purposes:

- **Starting from a standstill on a hill:** a throttle gets you moving before you need to coordinate pedalling, shifting, and balance on a steep grade.
- **Walking the bike:** walk-assist throttle (6 km/h) pushes the bike alongside you when walking through a crowded area, across a pedestrian crossing, or up a steep ramp.
- **Temporary injury or fatigue:** if your knee flares up mid-ride, a throttle gets you home without pedalling.
- **Cargo pulling:** on a heavily loaded cargo bike, a throttle provides launch power from a standstill that pedalling alone cannot match.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose pedal assist only if:**
- You want the e-bike to be legally classified as a bicycle everywhere in Australia
- You ride for exercise as well as transport
- Range is important (commuting, touring)
- You want the most natural riding feel (especially with torque sensing)

**Choose pedal assist + throttle if:**
- You ride in hilly areas and need throttle for hill starts
- You occasionally want to ride without pedalling (fatigue, injury)
- You use the bike for heavy cargo loads
- You understand the legal limits and will keep throttle use within them

**Choose throttle only if:**
- You want a motorised vehicle experience (more like a scooter than a bicycle)
- You have physical limitations that prevent pedalling
- You ride exclusively on private property or in states that permit throttle e-bikes on public roads

## Bottom Line

For the majority of Australian riders, pedal assist with a torque sensor is the best option — it is legal everywhere, delivers real exercise, maximises range, and feels the most natural. A throttle is a useful supplement for hill starts and walk-assist but should not be the primary mode of operation if you value legality, fitness, and battery life.

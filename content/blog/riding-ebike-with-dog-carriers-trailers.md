Riding with your dog is one of the great joys of e-bike ownership. The motor handles the extra weight of a dog carrier or trailer, and the dog gets an outing that covers more ground than a walk. But safety comes first — for the dog and for you.

## Options for Carrying a Dog

### Front Basket Carrier

A basket mounted on the handlebars or a front rack, with a dog harness or mesh cover to keep the dog secure.

**Best for:** small dogs (under 8 kg)
**Weight limit:** typically 5–10 kg (basket + dog)
**Advantages:** the dog is in front of you — you can see them, they can see you, and they get the best view
**Disadvantages:** adds weight to the front, affecting steering; limits handlebar movement; can block the display and controls

**Safety:**
- The dog must be tethered inside the basket (leash clipped to a harness, attached to the basket) — a dog jumping out at speed is a serious accident
- Use a basket with a mesh or zip cover to prevent jumping
- Keep the total front load under 10 kg to maintain safe steering

### Rear Basket or Rack-Mounted Carrier

A carrier or basket on the rear rack.

**Best for:** small to medium dogs (5–15 kg)
**Weight limit:** depends on the rack (typically 25 kg for the rack, so the carrier + dog must be under that)
**Advantages:** does not affect steering; keeps weight low and rearward; often more spacious than a front basket
**Disadvantages:** the dog is behind you — you cannot see them without turning around; they may be anxious without eye contact

**Safety:**
- Same tethering rules as the front basket — harness and leash, secured to the carrier
- Ensure the carrier is firmly attached to the rack (no wobbling, no sliding)
- Check that the dog's weight does not lift the front wheel on steep climbs

### Dog Trailer

A two-wheeled trailer towed behind the bike, with a cabin for the dog.

**Best for:** medium to large dogs (10–40 kg)
**Capacity:** 20–50 kg depending on the trailer
**Advantages:** the dog has space to sit, lie down, and move; carries much more weight than any basket; does not affect the bike's balance or handling directly
**Disadvantages:** adds length (harder to park and manoeuvre); adds rolling resistance (the motor handles this); wider than the bike (watch for posts and bollards); cannot go on narrow paths

**Trailer types:**
- **Enclosed cabin** with mesh windows: protects the dog from wind, rain, and road debris. The safest option.
- **Open-top trailer:** the dog sits in an open tub with a harness. Better ventilation but less protection.

**Safety:**
- Always tether the dog inside the trailer (harness clipped to the trailer frame)
- Use a trailer with a safety flag on a tall pole (drivers cannot see a low trailer behind a bike)
- Check the trailer hitch before every ride — a detached trailer at speed is catastrophic
- Practise riding with the loaded trailer in a quiet area before hitting the road

### Backpack Carrier

A backpack designed for carrying small dogs.

**Best for:** very small dogs (under 5 kg)
**Advantages:** keeps the bike handling unchanged; dog is close to you
**Disadvantages:** puts weight on your back (sweat); limits your movement; not comfortable for the dog on long rides; raises your centre of gravity

**Verdict:** acceptable for very short rides. For anything over 15 minutes, use a basket or trailer.

## Which Dogs Enjoy Riding?

Most dogs can learn to enjoy riding, but some take to it naturally:

**Good candidates:**
- Calm, confident temperament
- Used to car rides (similar experience — sitting and watching the world go by)
- Small to medium size (easier to carry safely)
- Older dogs who can no longer keep up on running alongside the bike

**Poor candidates:**
- Anxious dogs who panic in new situations
- Dogs who try to chase other animals, bikes, or cars while riding
- Very large dogs (over 40 kg — the weight and size make safe carrying impractical on most e-bikes)
- Dogs with joint or back problems (the vibration and jolting of riding can aggravate these)

## Training Your Dog for Bike Rides

1. **Introduce the carrier at home.** Let the dog sit in the basket or trailer indoors, with treats and praise. Do this for several days before putting it on the bike.
2. **Stationary bike.** Put the loaded carrier on the bike while it is stationary. Let the dog sit in it. Reward calm behaviour.
3. **Short, slow rides.** Start with 2–3 minute rides on a quiet path at walking speed. Gradually increase distance and speed.
4. **Watch for stress signs.** Panting, whining, trembling, trying to escape, flat ears, tucked tail — stop the ride and try again later with a shorter distance.
5. **Build duration gradually.** Most dogs take 3–7 sessions to become comfortable.

## Safety Rules

1. **Always harness and tether the dog.** Never rely on the dog staying put voluntarily. A squirrel, another dog, or a loud noise can trigger a jump — and a 10 kg dog jumping out at 20 km/h is a disaster for both of you.
2. **Never leash the dog to the bike while they run alongside.** This is extremely dangerous. If the dog stops or changes direction, you crash. If you brake suddenly, the leash drags the dog under the wheel.
3. **Carry water.** Dogs overheat faster than humans. Bring water and a collapsible bowl. Stop every 20–30 minutes for water on warm days.
4. **Avoid hot roads.** In summer, road surfaces can reach 60 °C+ — burning the dog's paws if they walk on it at stops. Stop on grass or in shade.
5. **Watch for fatigue and overheating.** Dogs in enclosed trailers can overheat if the ventilation is poor. Check the trailer temperature and the dog's condition regularly.
6. **Protect their eyes.** At 20+ km/h, wind, dust, and insects hit the dog's face. An enclosed trailer with mesh windows protects them. For basket riders, dog goggles (yes, they exist) help.

## E-Bike Advantages for Dog Riding

The motor makes riding with a dog practical in ways that a regular bike does not:

- **Extra weight is handled by the motor.** A 15 kg dog in a 5 kg trailer adds 20 kg. On a regular bike, this is brutal. On an e-bike, you adjust the assist level and barely notice.
- **Consistent speed on hills.** A trailer dragging you to 8 km/h on a hill is demoralising on a regular bike. The e-bike motor maintains your pace.
- **Lower rider effort = more attention for the dog.** Because you are working less hard, you can pay more attention to the dog's behaviour, traffic, and the route.

## Bottom Line

Riding with your dog is safe and enjoyable with the right carrier (basket for small dogs, trailer for medium to large dogs), proper tethering, and gradual training. The e-bike motor handles the extra weight that makes dog-carrying impractical on a regular bike. Start with short, slow rides in quiet areas and build up. Always harness the dog, carry water, and watch for stress.

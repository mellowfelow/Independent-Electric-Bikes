Where you park your e-bike determines whether you still have an e-bike tomorrow. E-bikes are the most stolen category of bicycle in Australia — they are valuable, visible, and often locked in the same places every day. Beyond theft, improper storage damages batteries and components.

## Outdoor Parking: Locking Strategy

### Lock Types

No lock is unbreakable. The goal is to make your bike harder to steal than the one next to it, and to slow a thief long enough to attract attention.

| Lock type | Security | Weight | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cable lock | Low | Light (300–500 g) | Cafe stops only (under 5 minutes, in sight) |
| Folding lock | Medium | Medium (800–1200 g) | Short errands, low-risk areas |
| U-lock (D-lock) | High | Heavy (1000–1800 g) | Commuting, workplace, any time out of sight |
| Chain lock (hardened) | Very high | Very heavy (2000–4000 g) | Overnight outdoor parking, high-theft areas |

### How to Lock an E-Bike

1. **Lock through the frame AND the rear wheel** to a fixed object. The frame is the most valuable part — a stolen wheel is cheap to replace.
2. **Use a secondary cable** through the front wheel. A quick-release front wheel can be removed in 5 seconds.
3. **Remove the battery** if your lock is storing the bike for more than an hour in a public place. The battery is the second-most-valuable component ($400–$1,000) and is easily removed on most e-bikes.
4. **Remove the display** if it is detachable (Bosch Kiox, Shimano displays). A stolen display costs $150–$400 and makes the bike harder to sell.

### What to Lock To

- **Bike hoops (Sheffield stands):** the best option. Bolted to concrete, difficult to remove.
- **Signposts:** acceptable if the sign cannot be lifted over the top (some are not bolted down).
- **Fences and railings:** weak — many fences can be cut or unbolted faster than a lock.
- **Trees:** do not lock to trees. The lock can be slid up and over branches, and councils may fine you.

### Where NOT to Park

- The same spot every day at the same time (a thief can plan)
- Isolated areas with no foot traffic (no witnesses)
- Anywhere the bike blocks a footpath or doorway (councils can remove it)
- Near angle grinder access (construction sites, workshops — thieves use them)

## Apartment and Unit Storage

Most e-bike owners in apartments face a universal problem: there is no secure place to store a 25 kg electric bike.

### Inside the Apartment

The safest option. The bike is behind a locked door, out of sight, and climate-controlled (good for the battery).

**Challenges:**
- Space — a standard e-bike is 1.8 m long and 65 cm wide
- Weight — carrying a 25 kg bike up stairs is exhausting
- Dirt — tyres and chains track grime onto floors and walls

**Solutions:**
- **Wall hooks or vertical wall mounts:** hang the bike by the front wheel on the wall. Takes up about 0.5 m of floor space. Make sure the wall mount is rated for the bike's weight (standard bike hooks are rated for 15 kg — an e-bike needs 25–30 kg rating).
- **Floor stands:** a stand that holds the bike upright by the rear wheel. Takes about 0.6 m × 0.5 m of floor space. Easier than lifting onto a wall hook.
- **Folding e-bikes:** if apartment storage is a constant problem, a folding e-bike eliminates it. Folded dimensions are typically 80 × 65 × 40 cm — fits in a cupboard.
- **Protective mat:** put a mat or tray under the rear wheel to catch drips and dirt.

### Building Bike Rooms and Cages

Many apartment buildings have a shared bike room or cage in the basement or car park.

**Security assessment:**
- **Is it locked?** A shared room with a code lock is better than open access, but every resident has the code.
- **Is there CCTV?** Camera coverage deters opportunistic theft.
- **Are there individual racks or cages?** A personal cage with your own lock is significantly more secure than a shared room.
- **Who else has access?** If tradespeople, cleaners, and visitors can enter, the security is lower.

**Recommendation:** use the building bike room as your primary storage, but lock your bike inside it (U-lock to the rack). Do not rely on the room lock alone. Remove the battery and display if the room is accessible to non-residents.

### Garage Storage

If you have a garage or car space:
- **Wall-mount the bike** to keep floor space clear
- **Cover the bike** if the garage is dusty or exposed to temperature swings
- **Lock it** even in a locked garage (garage break-ins are common)
- **Store the battery indoors** in extreme heat or cold (see below)

## Battery Storage

The battery is the most sensitive component on the bike. Improper storage degrades it faster than riding does.

### Temperature

- **Ideal storage temperature:** 10–20 °C
- **Acceptable range:** 0–35 °C
- **Danger zones:** below -10 °C (reduced capacity, potential cell damage) and above 40 °C (accelerated degradation, fire risk in extreme cases)

**Australian context:** garage temperatures in summer can reach 45–50 °C in parts of Australia. Do not leave an e-bike battery in a hot garage, car boot, or direct sunlight. Bring the battery indoors.

### Charge Level for Storage

If you are storing the bike for more than 2 weeks without riding:
- **Store the battery at 40–60 % charge** (not full, not empty)
- **Check monthly** and recharge to 50 % if it drops below 20 %
- **Never store a fully discharged battery** — lithium cells can over-discharge and become permanently damaged

### Charging Safety

- Charge indoors on a non-flammable surface (concrete, tile — not carpet or wood)
- Use only the manufacturer's charger
- Do not charge unattended overnight (most fires occur during charging)
- Do not charge a visibly damaged battery (dents, swelling, water ingress)
- Install a smoke detector near the charging area

## Security Upgrades

### GPS Trackers

A GPS tracker hidden inside the frame, seatpost, or headset gives you a chance to recover a stolen bike.

**Options:**
- **Apple AirTag:** cheap ($49), fits inside a seatpost or under the saddle. Limited to the Apple Find My network — works well in urban areas with many iPhones.
- **Samsung SmartTag:** same concept for Samsung users. Smaller network than Apple.
- **Dedicated bike GPS trackers (Invoxia, Tracki, Sherlock):** more expensive ($100–$200+ plus monthly subscription) but offer real-time tracking, motion alerts, and geofencing.

**Important:** a GPS tracker helps you find a stolen bike — it does not prevent theft. Always use a quality lock as the primary deterrent.

### Registration

Register your bike's serial number with a national database:
- **Bike Vault** (bikevault.com.au) — free registration, used by Australian police
- **529 Garage** (project529.com) — free, international, used by some Australian forces
- Record the serial number (stamped on the bottom bracket, under the cranks) and keep it with your purchase receipt

## Bottom Line

The best e-bike storage combines a secure physical location (inside your home or a locked room) with a quality lock (U-lock minimum), battery removal when parked in public, and a hidden GPS tracker. For apartment dwellers, a wall mount or floor stand inside the apartment is the safest option. Store the battery at room temperature and 40–60 % charge when not riding.
